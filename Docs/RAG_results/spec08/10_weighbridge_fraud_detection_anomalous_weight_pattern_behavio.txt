Criminal typologies at the truck scale are diverse and continually evolving. Key vectors include 49:

1. **Improper Positioning:** Drivers intentionally resting axles off the scale plate to manipulate gross or tare weights, thereby altering the calculated net payload.  
2. **Load-Cell Tampering:** The installation of remote-controlled bypass chips or stealth hardware into the analogue load-cell circuitry, allowing perpetrators to artificially alter the signal sent to the terminal.  
3. **Tare Weight Manipulation:** Adding rocks, sandbags, or hidden water tanks to the vehicle during the initial tare weigh-in, and dumping them before loading, creating a phantom payload that the *acopio* pays for but never receives.  
4. **Altering Data and Collusion:** Weighbridge operators conspiring with drivers to alter digital records within unsecured software or generate fraudulent weight tickets for ghost trucks that were never actually delivered.

Traditional rule-based fraud detection is insufficient due to the dynamic nature of these attacks and the continuous adaptation of fraudsters.53 An intelligent ERP must deploy machine learning anomaly detection frameworks to continuously monitor the telemetry from the weighbridge hardware.53

Models such as Isolation Forests or One-Class Support Vector Machines (SVM) are specifically designed to operate on highly imbalanced datasets where fraudulent events are rare compared to legitimate traffic.39 Crucially, these unsupervised algorithms do not require labeled examples of every possible fraud method; instead, they ingest vast amounts of historical data to establish a high-dimensional baseline of "normal" operational behavior.53

By aggregating diverse inputs—such as the historical tare weights of specific truck license plates, the temporal duration of the loading process, micro-fluctuations in load-cell voltage stability, and cross-referencing with overhead camera image parameters—the AI can instantly detect contextual and collective anomalies.39 For instance, if a truck's tare weight deviates from its 6-month historical mean by 3%, or if the time elapsed between the gross weigh-in and tare weigh-out is mathematically inconsistent with the physical distance to the unloading pit, the ERP automatically flags the transaction.
