# **Systemic Integration and Regulatory Architecture of the Carta de Porte Electrónica (CPE) and Código de Trazabilidad de Granos (CTG) in Argentina (2024-2026)**

## **1\. Executive Introduction and the Architectural Paradigm Shift**

The regulatory framework governing the transport, traceability, and taxation of agricultural commodities within the Argentine Republic has undergone a profound structural metamorphosis over the past two decades. For enterprise architects, systems integrators, and developers tasked with building Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) software for the agricultural sector, comprehending the historical continuum and current technical specifications of these resolutions is not merely an exercise in legal compliance. It is a fundamental prerequisite for designing robust, highly available, and fault-tolerant middleware engines capable of navigating synchronous state validations, backward compatibility requirements, complex audit trails, and the inevitable deprecation of legacy systemic pathways.

The transition from the physical issuance of paper documentation to real-time, cryptographically signed electronic records has culminated in a highly centralized, algorithmic data ecosystem. This ecosystem is currently managed by the Agencia de Recaudación y Control Aduanero (ARCA), the institutional successor to the Administración Federal de Ingresos Públicos (AFIP), which assumed comprehensive oversight following national administrative restructuring.1 The contemporary architecture demands that ERP systems abandon asynchronous, human-in-the-loop batch processing in favor of synchronous Web Service architectures that bind the physical logistics of grain movement directly to the fiscal realization of tax liabilities in real-time. This report delivers an exhaustive, technical, and regulatory analysis of the Carta de Porte Electrónica (CPE) and the Código de Trazabilidad de Granos (CTG) lifecycles as of the 2024-2026 fiscal horizon, specifically tailored for enterprise software integration.

## **2\. Regulatory Genealogy: From Physical Quotas to Algorithmic Oversight**

To architect a compliant ERP system that gracefully handles legacy data migration and current operational mandates, integrators must understand the evolutionary trajectory of the Carta de Porte. The regulatory environment has consistently moved toward reducing physical friction while exponentially increasing systemic data harvesting.

### **2.1 The Genesis of Traceability: RG 2596/2009 and the Paper Era**

The modern lineage of grain transport documentation and systemic control began with the promulgation of Resolución General 2596/2009.3 This foundational regulation established the mandatory use of the physical Carta de Porte as the sole valid and legally recognized document for the commercialization and transport of grains not intended for sowing, specifically encompassing major cereals and oilseeds.4 Under this historical regime, the physical issuance of the document was tightly controlled and artificially bottlenecked. The tax authority assigned strict printing quotas based on taxpayer categories and historical compliance profiles, creating a system where logistics were inherently constrained by the physical availability of paper forms.3

This framework was subsequently modified and expanded by RG 2678/2009 and RG 3292/2012, which introduced early attempts at systemic, inter-agency controls.3 These amendments mandated joint oversight by the Oficina Nacional de Control Comercial Agropecuario (ONCCA) and the Subsecretaría de Transporte Automotor, theoretically unifying the control of both road (Automotor) and rail (Ferroviaria) transport.5 However, the fundamental reliance on physical paper left the system inherently vulnerable to logistical delays, sophisticated forgery, and severe data fragmentation across disparate governmental silos.

### **2.2 The Digital Revolution: RG 5017/2021 and the Birth of the CPE**

The pivotal architectural and operational shift occurred with the implementation of Resolución General Conjunta 5017/2021.6 Drafted as a collaborative mandate between AFIP, the Ministry of Agriculture, Livestock and Fisheries, and the Ministry of Transport, this resolution officially deprecated the physical Carta de Porte and introduced the Carta de Porte Electrónica (CPE).7

As of the strict cutoff date of November 1, 2021, the physical issuance of paper waybills and the manual request for the Grain Traceability Code (CTG) via legacy portals were permanently terminated.9 The CPE architecture centralized the request, data loading, and issuance of the transport voucher into a single, synchronous systemic step. Crucially, it integrated the generation of the CTG directly into the electronic payload response.11 This regulatory shift forced ERP systems to fundamentally re-architect their transport modules, migrating from batch-printing and manual data entry modules to real-time Web Service integrations capable of constructing complex XML/SOAP payloads and parsing base64 encoded PDF responses on the fly.9

### **2.3 Sectoral Expansion: RG 5235/2022 and Grain Derivatives**

Following the successful stabilization and digitization of the primary grain transport network, the regulatory bodies expanded the electronic requirement to encompass processed agricultural outputs. Resolución General Conjunta 5235/2022 established the Carta de Porte Electrónica \- Derivados Granarios (CPEDG) as the exclusive and mandatory document for transporting processed by-products.1 This regulation effectively replaced the traditional commercial delivery note (Remito) that had previously governed the movement of these specific goods, forcing industrial processors to integrate their outbound logistics systems with the federal tax API.1

### **2.4 The ARCA Modernization and Administrative Simplification: RG 5821/2026**

The current operational baseline for enterprise integrators is defined by Resolución General Conjunta 5821/2026, issued jointly by the Ministry of Economy and ARCA in February 2026\.1 This resolution represents a maturation of the digital ecosystem, introducing critical optimizations aimed at reducing unnecessary administrative friction while reinforcing core tax compliance.

RG 5821/2026 formally codifies the deep integration of the Sistema de Información Simplificado Agrícola (SISA) as the ultimate, centralized source of truth for operator authorization, effectively replacing the obsolete and fragmented RUCA registry for grain operators.1 Furthermore, this resolution introduces vital modifications to both RG 5017/2021 and RG 5235/2022 by establishing explicit exemptions for internal logistics, closed-loop pipeline transport, and short-distance conveyor belt movements. These exemptions fundamentally alter the conditional logic required within ERP transport and warehouse management modules, allowing for more fluid internal operations without triggering federal Web Service calls.1

## **3\. The Cryptographic State Machine: Mapping the Document Lifecycle**

For a software integration to function reliably within the Argentine agricultural supply chain, the local ERP database must act as a perfect, highly synchronized mirror of the state machine maintained on ARCA's centralized servers. The lifecycle of a Carta de Porte Electrónica is governed by strict deterministic rules. ERP systems must replicate this state machine locally to ensure data consistency, drive user interface logic, and prevent invalid transition requests that would result in Web Service rejections and potential operational blockages.

The lifecycle is categorized into distinct transport modes, primarily Automotor (Truck) and Ferroviaria (Train), each possessing nuanced validation rules and temporal constraints.13

### **3.1 Exhaustive Mapping of State Transitions**

The progression of a CPE from its conceptual inception to its definitive fiscal closure involves multiple systemic actors, decentralized geolocation events, and sequential Web Service invocations. ### **3.2 Complex Routing: Contingency Protocols and Exception Handling**

Enterprise ERP systems must possess robust exception-handling logic to manage the chaotic realities of agricultural logistics. If a truck breaks down on a provincial highway, the ERP cannot simply wait for the 5-day expiration timer to elapse. The system must provide a user interface for the logistics manager to trigger the InformarContingencia method via the ARCA API.9 This alters the systemic state to "Activa con contingencia declarada," which legally protects the cargo from confiscation by provincial police or federal tax inspectors during the delay.9 Operating a vehicle while the document is in this state is a severe infraction; the transport is legally paralyzed. Once the mechanical or logistical issue is resolved, a corresponding Cierre de Contingencia must be transmitted to resume the transit timer and allow movement.9

In scenarios where the destination plant rejects the cargo upon arrival—a common occurrence due to biological factors like moisture content exceeding contract specifications—the Destination CUIT issues a rejection via ARCA. The ERP of the original requesting CUIT must be architected to catch this state change, ideally via polling the ARCA services or subscribing to webhooks if made available. Upon detecting a rejection, the origin ERP must immediately issue either a Desvío (Diversion) to route the truck to an alternative active plant, or a Regreso Origen (Return to Origin) to bring the cargo back to the producer.9 The Desvío payload requires the CUIT of the original applicant, the identification number of the existing CPE, and the comprehensive SISA data of the new destination.13

### **3.3 Time-to-Live (TTL) Horizons and Background Processing**

The temporal validity of the transport document is strictly enforced by ARCA's centralized servers. An active CPE for Automotor (Truck) transport possesses a maximum Time-to-Live (TTL) of exactly 5 calendar days from the moment of authorization.11 For Ferroviaria (Rail) transport, acknowledging the inherently slower nature of logistical marshaling and multi-car switching, the TTL is extended to 30 days.11

Operating a vehicle with an expired CPE is legally equivalent to operating entirely without documentation, triggering the maximum severity of penalties under the National Tax Procedures Law (Law 11683).15 Therefore, ERP systems must implement resilient background workers (e.g., CRON jobs or message-driven daemons) that continuously monitor the TTL of all active CPEs. These background processes should be configured to alert logistics managers and transport coordinators at least 24 hours prior to expiration, enabling preemptive contingency declarations or rerouting efforts.

## **4\. Schema Topography: Mandatory Data Fields for ERP Payloads**

The technical integration with the ARCA backend utilizes the CpeService Web Service, specifically Version 2.0.5 as of the most recent updates.13 Communication is highly synchronous and relies on complex SOAP/XML payloads. ERP developers must understand the stark distinction between the data required to *create* the document (which is largely predictive and declarative) and the data required to *close* the document at the destination (which must be strictly empirical).

### **4.1 Phase 1: Creation and Authorization Payload**

To successfully invoke the AutorizarCPEAutomotor or AutorizarCPEFerroviaria methods, the ERP must assemble a structured XML payload encompassing multiple nested complex types.13 A failure to provide any mandatory field, or a failure to respect data typing constraints, results in a synchronous HTTP 500 error or a specific Business Validation Error (e.g., Code 950: Required field missing).9

**WSAA Authentication Header:**

Every request must include the Auth complex data type. This necessitates a precursor sequence where the ERP authenticates against the Web Services de Autenticación y Autorización (WSAA) using an X.509 cryptographic certificate.

* token: The temporary, cryptographically signed access token returned by WSAA.13  
* sign: The digital signature validating the integrity of the token.13  
* cuit: The precise Tax ID of the emitting citizen or the authorized digital representative.13

**Header Data (CabeceraSolicitud):**

* sucursal: The branch number of the issuing entity, defined by the simple type NumeroSucursal (integer).13  
* numeroOrden: A sequential order number maintained locally by the ERP. ARCA validates this sequence to prevent duplicate submissions.13  
* numeroPlanta: The specific physical plant number from which the grain originates, which must be actively registered in the SISA database.13

**Origin and Intervening Parties (IntervinientesSolicitud):**

* cuitSolicitante: The primary entity requesting the document and assuming legal liability for the payload.9  
* esUsuarioIndustria: A boolean flag indicating whether the requesting user is operating as an industrial processor.13  
* domicilioOrigen: A complex object containing the detailed geographic address of the origin.13

**Logistics and Load Data:**

* fechaHoraPartida: The anticipated timestamp of departure.13  
* kmRecorrer: The estimated distance to be traveled. This field is critical as it triggers ARCA's internal reference tariff logic, flagging potential under-billing in the transport sector.9  
* cuitPagadorFlete: Made strictly mandatory in API version 2.0.3, this requires the Tax ID of the economic entity responsible for paying the freight carrier.13  
* transporte: A deeply nested complex object containing the Carrier's CUIT, the Driver's national identification details, and the NumeroDominio (the vehicle's license plate, which must strictly conform to a 6-7 character alphanumeric regex).9  
* coordenadas: Functional geolocation coordinates (latitude/longitude), introduced as a requirement in API version 2.0.4 to enhance geographic traceability.13

**Rail Specific Data (Ferroviaria):**

* ramalDescarga: The specific discharge branch line the train will utilize at the destination port or facility.13

### **4.2 Phase 2: Destination and Closure Payload**

The fields completed at the destination are fundamentally different in nature. While the creation phase relies on estimates regarding weight and departure time, the destination phase demands exact, empirically verified data obtained from calibrated weighbridges.

When executing the ConfirmacionDefinitiva method to finalize the transaction, the ERP deployed at the receiving plant must transmit:

* cuitSolicitante: The CUIT of the original applicant, to ensure referential integrity.13  
* cartaPorte: The unique identification number of the CPE.13  
* pesoBrutoDescarg: The exact gross weight of the truck or rail wagon upon initial arrival at the scale.13  
* pesoTaraDescarga: The exact tare (empty) weight of the vehicle after the grain has been unloaded.13

Upon receiving this payload, the ARCA system automatically calculates the net weight delivered. It then executes a variance algorithm, comparing the empirical net weight against the estimated weight declared at origin. If the difference exceeds legally tolerated margins (accounting for natural moisture loss during transit), ARCA generates a systemic variance report that may trigger automated audits.9

## **5\. Ontological Roles and Tax Liabilities in the Commercial Matrix**

The Argentine agricultural commercialization chain is characterized by high levels of intermediation, involving producers, physical elevators, financial brokers, and multinational exporters. RG 5821/2026 and its preceding norms demand the strict cryptographic mapping of every single commercial actor to a specific systemic role within the XML payload.1 Misrepresenting a role is not merely a technical error; it constitutes a fiscal infraction, as it routes tax liabilities and withholding obligations to the incorrect legal entity.

### **5.1 Defining the Systemic Actors**

* **CUIT Solicitante (The Applicant):** This is the entity generating the systemic request. The applicant can be the primary Producer, an Acopiador (Grain Elevator/Collector), or an Acondicionadora (Conditioning Plant).9 Crucially, the Solicitante assumes total legal and fiscal responsibility for the veracity of the data declared in the payload. They are also legally liable for ensuring that the physical truck respects the maximum load weights per vehicle axis as stipulated by National Decree No. 32/2018.1  
* **Remitente Comercial (The Commercial Sender):** This role represents the economic owner of the grain at the exact moment of transport, which is critical for value-added tax (IVA) tracking.  
  * *Venta Primaria (Primary Sale):* This field is utilized for the very first sale of grain, typically moving from a Producer to an Acopiador or directly to an Exporter.9  
  * *Venta Secundaria 1 & 2 (Secondary Sales):* These fields are populated when financial intermediaries (such as Operadores or Canjeadores) trade the ownership of the grain while it is physically in transit or sitting in temporary storage.9  
* **Destino (The Destination):** This represents the physical geolocation where the grain will be unloaded. The payload requires the CUIT of the destination entity, and the ARCA backend performs a synchronous validation to ensure that this specific CUIT possesses an "Active" physical plant status in the SISA registry at that exact geographic location.1  
* **Destinatario (The Recipient):** This is the commercial entity receiving legal ownership of the grain at the end of the journey. Often, the physical Destino (e.g., a port facility owned by a logistics firm) is entirely distinct from the commercial Destinatario (e.g., a multinational agricultural trading house).9  
* **Transportista (The Carrier):** The logistics company or individual owner of the transport vehicle. Their CUIT must be valid and active in federal registries, and the vehicle domain (license plate) must match national automotive registries.9  
* **Chofer (The Driver):** The human operator of the vehicle. Data requirements include their national identification number (CUIL/CUIT). The driver holds the immediate legal responsibility for physically exhibiting the CPE—either via a digital PDF on a mobile device or a printed paper format—to law enforcement or tax authorities during transit.18

To illustrate the sheer architectural complexity an ERP must support, one must examine "Caso 10" from ARCA's official practical implementation guidelines. An enterprise ERP must maintain robust relational database structures capable of linking up to seven distinct legal entities to a single transport event. Furthermore, the ERP should ideally pre-validate each of these CUITs against a locally cached mirror of the SISA registry before attempting transmission, thereby avoiding unnecessary SOAP faults and API rate-limiting penalties.

## **6\. CTG Generation, Assignment, and Field Verification Protocols**

Prior to the regulatory overhaul in 2021, the Código de Trazabilidad de Granos (CTG) was an entirely separate entity, often requested via a distinct systemic process, sometimes via telephone interfaces, and subsequently attached to a physical waybill. The current Web Service architecture fundamentally alters this relationship, merging the transport authorization and traceability assignment into a unified cryptographic transaction.

### **6.1 Synchronous Systemic Assignment**

The CTG is no longer manually generated by the operator, nor is it typed into a user interface. Under the modern CpeService architecture, the CTG is systematically generated and assigned exclusively by ARCA's backend during the execution of the AutorizarCPEAutomotor or AutorizarCPEFerroviaria transaction.9

When the ERP submits a structurally valid and business-rule-compliant issuance payload, ARCA processes the data, commits the record to its central database, and returns a complex response object (either TransporteAutomotorRespuesta or DetalleAutomotorRespuesta).9 Imbedded within this response is the active nroCTG, which is strictly defined as a numeric, 12-digit string.19 The ERP must capture this nroCTG and store it relationally against the internal shipment record. This 12-digit code serves as the immutable primary key for all subsequent lifecycle updates, including arrival confirmation, unloading, final closure, and the eventual linkage to fiscal settlement documents.

### **6.2 Decentralized Verification and Auditing Mechanisms**

To facilitate real-time auditing by provincial police (such as the Policía Caminera in Córdoba), toll operators, or federal tax inspectors who operate in remote areas without direct access to the full ARCA backend, the system utilizes highly effective decentralized verification mechanisms.

* **Cryptographic QR Code:** The base64 encoded PDF returned by the ARCA API contains an embedded, dynamically generated QR code. Scanning this code with any standard smartphone retrieves real-time data directly from ARCA's public verification endpoints. The inspector can instantly view the CPE number, the 12-digit CTG, issue and expiration timestamps, origin and destination coordinates, exact grain type, vehicle license plate, and critically, the current systemic state (e.g., Activa, Anulada, Rechazada).11  
* **Cellular SMS Gateway:** Recognizing the reality of poor mobile data coverage in vast agricultural regions, ARCA maintains an SMS gateway. An inspector or law enforcement officer can send an SMS message to the shortcode 2347\. By sending the text string "PATENTE \[Plate Number\]" or "CPE \[CPE Number\]", the ARCA database returns a plain-text string verifying if that specific truck is currently authorized to transport the declared grain on that specific route.9 This ensures that enforcement is possible even in completely offline environments.

## **7\. Fiscal Realization: Bridging the CPE with the Liquidación Primaria de Granos**

The Carta de Porte Electrónica is fundamentally the logistical component of the transaction; it tracks the physical mass of the commodity moving through space and time. However, the financial realization of the trade and the subsequent calculation of tax withholdings occur through the Liquidación Primaria de Granos (LPG), historically referred to in the industry as Formulario 1116\.17 The systemic interaction between the CPE/CTG and the LPG is the most critical juncture in the ERP architecture; the physical movement of the grain must perfectly and mathematically reconcile with the fiscal settlement.

### **7.1 The LPG Web Service Integration**

The integration for financial settlements is handled by an entirely separate Web Service ecosystem: LpgService (currently operating on Version 1.23).19 This service manages complex financial operations, primarily liquidacionAutorizar, which is invoked to obtain an Electronic Operation Code (COE) that legally validates the trade.23

When an Acopiador or an Exporter receives grain from a Producer, they are legally mandated to issue the LPG to trigger payment and tax withholdings.11 To prevent pervasive tax fraud, "black market" sales, and the double-counting of inventory, the LPG must be cryptographically linked to the specific transport documents that delivered the physical grain. This linkage is achieved via the asociarLiquidacionAContrato operation within the LpgService API.19

The ERP must assemble a settlement payload containing the following critical linkages:

* nroCtg: The 12-digit CTG number originally obtained during the CPE authorization phase.19  
* tipoCtg: The classification category of the CTG.19  
* kilosConfirmados: The exact net weight that was empirically confirmed during the ConfirmacionDefinitiva phase of the CPE lifecycle.19

If an ERP attempts to authorize an LPG for a financial volume of grain that mathematically exceeds the kilosConfirmados linked to the submitted array of CTGs, ARCA's backend—which continuously monitors the "cuenta corriente granaria" (grain checking account) of every taxpayer—will synchronously reject the settlement authorization.9 The physical kilograms delivered act as a hard cap on the financial kilograms that can be settled.

### **7.2 Ontological Mapping: Species Consistency and "Grano Padre" Logic**

A frequent logistical reality in the agricultural sector is that grain is transported under a general, presumptive classification but is subsequently reclassified following rigorous laboratory quality analysis at the destination port. The ARCA systems possess a sophisticated biological ontology to handle this reality without triggering automated tax evasion flags.

The system legally permits the issuance of an LPG for a fundamentally different grain variety than what was originally declared on the active CPE, provided both species belong to the same biological "Grano Padre" (Parent Grain) within ARCA's master data.10 For example, a CPE might be issued at the farm level for "Brewery Barley" (Cebada Cervecera). If the destination laboratory determines the protein or moisture content renders it unsuitable for brewing, the ERP can seamlessly issue the final LPG for "Feed Barley" (Cebada Forrajera). Because both specific grain codes map back to the parent species of Barley in the federal database, no inconsistency error is generated.10 ERP developers must implement this exact "Grano Padre" ontological mapping within their local Item Master Data tables to prevent unnecessary API rejections and financial delays.

## **8\. Navigating the Provincial Regulatory Labyrinth**

A major architectural and operational hurdle for ERP systems deployed in Argentina is that strict compliance with federal ARCA mandates does not absolve the carrier from provincial regulatory compliance. Provincial tax authorities (Direcciones de Rentas) enforce their own sophisticated digital transit codes. Their primary objective is to capture the Gross Income Tax (Impuesto a los Ingresos Brutos) on any economic value moving through their sovereign territories. A single truck moving from a farm in Córdoba to a deep-water port in Rosario (Santa Fe), while crossing a sliver of Buenos Aires province, must comply with three distinct digital regimes simultaneously.

### **8.1 ARBA (Buenos Aires): Código de Operación de Traslados (COT)**

The Agencia de Recaudación de la Provincia de Buenos Aires (ARBA) mandates the highly regulated Código de Operación de Traslados (COT).25

* **Financial Thresholds:** As of January 1, 2026, the minimum financial threshold for mandatory COT generation was significantly updated. A COT is required for any goods valued over $9,529,691 ARS, or for any shipment weighing equal to or greater than 4,500 kilograms.26 Given that a standard tandem grain truck carries approximately 30,000 kilograms, virtually 100% of grain transports legally require a COT if they touch Buenos Aires soil.  
* **Adhered Jurisdictions:** ARBA's COT system acts as an interoperable hub. The document is required if either the origin or the destination resides within adhered provinces, which currently include Buenos Aires, Santa Fe, Mendoza, the Autonomous City of Buenos Aires (CABA), and Catamarca.27  
* **2026 Digital Shifts:** By March 2026, ARBA is enforcing a transition to fully digital retention certificates (Formulario A-122R) via its Web Service, strictly eliminating legacy free-format forms.28 ERPs must possess a parallel module that authenticates via the Clave de Identificación de Transporte (CIT) to generate the COT seamlessly alongside the federal CPE.29

### **8.2 API (Santa Fe): Alignment and Dynamic Aliquots**

The Administración Provincial de Impuestos (API) of Santa Fe operates a parallel, yet distinct, tracking requirement.

* **Divergent Thresholds:** API triggers the tracking requirement at a drastically lower financial threshold of merely $60,000 ARS, though it mercifully shares the 4,500 kilogram weight threshold with ARBA.29  
* **Systemic Interaction:** The API COT service cannot be accessed natively; it must be specifically enabled via the ARCA Relational Administrator portal.29 In 2026, API introduced highly complex dynamic aliquots based on a constantly updating digital registry of retention agents.28 If a grain buyer located in Santa Fe is not updated in the ERP's master data against the live API registry, the retention calculations on the subsequent LPG will be mathematically incorrect, inevitably leading to fiscal debt and provincial audits.

### **8.3 DGR (Córdoba): Legal Frameworks and Physical Compliance**

The Dirección General de Rentas (DGR) of Córdoba regulates internal transit under the framework of the updated Tax Code (Ley Impositiva Anual 2026, Law N° 11090).30 While Córdoba smartly leverages ARCA's federal documentation to avoid redundant data entry, specific provincial promotional regimes (such as Ley 11062\) require distinct digital resolutions and guarantees (e.g., Resolución Normativa 31/2026) for industrial operators.32 Transporting grains within Córdoba also subjects the vehicle to stringent, physical checks by the Policía Caminera. These enforcement officers utilize mobile terminals to verify the systemic validity of the federal CPE/CTG against the specific provincial tax status of the transport operator.30

**Architectural Takeaway for Integrators:** An enterprise-grade ERP must feature a "Geofencing Routing Engine." When a logistics user requests a CPE from Origin X to Destination Y, the ERP must mathematically calculate the proposed physical route. If the route polygon intersects the provincial borders of Buenos Aires, the ERP must synchronously queue a COT payload to the ARBA Web Service. If it intersects Santa Fe, a payload must go to API. The system must merge the ARCA CTG token and the provincial COT tokens onto a single, unified digital manifest for the driver.

## **9\. Micro-Logistics: Short Freight and Internal Transfer Exemptions**

Agricultural logistics frequently involve high-volume micro-movements that do not warrant the full bureaucratic weight, distance calculations, and tracking overhead of a long-distance CPE. Recognizing this operational reality, ARCA provides systemic exceptions and expedited workflows, heavily modified and clarified by the recent RG 5821/2026.1

### **9.1 The "Flete Corto" Regime (Short Freight)**

The Carta de Porte Automotor Flete Corto is a highly streamlined digital document designed exclusively for initial, short-distance movements from the harvest field to a nearby consolidation point or local elevator.1 While historically understood as applying to distances under 100km, the systemic validations focus more on actor relationships than pure geographic distance.

* **Strict Eligibility:** The use of Flete Corto is restricted exclusively to primary grain producers who hold an optimal, low-risk fiscal profile within SISA (specifically, States 1 or 2).1  
* **Operational Conditions:** The destination plant must be fully "active" in SISA, and critically, the issuer (the Productor) and the destination entity must share the exact same CUIT.1 It cannot be utilized for commercial sales to third parties.  
* **Systemic Workflow and Automation:** The ERP can be configured to generate a Flete Corto automatically upon the producer's digital acceptance. This acceptance must be logged in the system up to 72 hours before the physical transport begins.9 Even if an Acopiador generates the systemic request on behalf of the farmer, the Producer must systemically confirm it using their own fiscal credentials.34

### **9.2 Silo-Bolsa to Plant: RG 5821/2026 Exemptions**

Prior to 2026, moving grain from a temporary silo-bag (silo-bolsa) located on a producer's vast property to a processing plant located on the exact same property occupied a perilous legal gray area. Often, operators were forced to issue a full CPE if the tractor crossed internal dirt paths, generating massive volumes of useless API traffic.

Resolución General Conjunta 5821/2026 explicitly eliminates the mandatory use of the CPE (specifically targeting grain derivatives and internal primary grain logistics) to radically reduce administrative burdens.1 The legal exemption applies unconditionally if the merchandise is:

1. **Entregada directamente en planta:** Delivered directly within the physically fenced confines of the authorized plant.1  
2. **Transported via Ductos:** Moved continuously through industrial pipelines.1  
3. **Transported via Cintas Transportadoras:** Moved via mechanical conveyor belts.1  
4. **Other internal land methods:** Any closed-loop terrestrial transport (e.g., internal dump trucks) that does not traverse public municipal, provincial, or federal roadways.1

For ERP architects, this regulatory clarification implies that the Warehouse Management System (WMS) module can execute internal stock transfers (e.g., from "Location A: Silo Bolsa" to "Location B: Main Silo") natively, without triggering the ARCA Web Service integration, provided the geographic coordinates of both locations map to the same SISA-registered property polygon.

## **10\. Punitive Frameworks and Enterprise Risk Mitigation**

The enforcement mechanisms surrounding the CPE and CTG are purposely draconian, designed by the federal government to eliminate the multi-billion peso black market in untaxed grain commercialization. Failures in ERP integration—whether due to unhandled exceptions, poor data validation, or asynchronous desynchronization—that result in trucks moving without active, cryptographically valid CPEs trigger immediate, devastating punitive actions under the National Tax Procedures Law (Ley 11683).35

### **10.1 Severe Fiscal Infractions (Law 11683\)**

Operating a commercial vehicle without a valid CPE, or operating with a CPE containing manipulated data (e.g., declaring a lower-taxed grain type to alter fiscal brackets), constitutes a severe breach of Articles 40, 45, and 46 of Law 11683\.35 The state employs three primary punitive instruments:

* **Clausura (Physical Closure):** The business establishment, processing plant, or administrative office of the offending party can be subjected to an immediate, forced physical closure ranging from 2 to 6 days.15 Jurisprudence from federal tax courts indicates that judges routinely uphold these closures, arguing that the CPE is the fundamental pillar guaranteeing the transparency of the entire commercial chain.15  
* **Decomiso (Total Confiscation):** The most severe and immediate logistical penalty is the absolute confiscation of the merchandise. If a truck is stopped by provincial authorities and the SMS query to 2347 reveals that no active CPE exists for that specific license plate, the state possesses the legal authority to immediately seize the 30 tons of grain.15 Tax courts have ruled that confiscation is entirely proportionate when the origin and legal ownership of the grain cannot be backed by an immutable systemic document.15  
* **Multas (Financial Fines):** Omissions, data negligence, or active defrauding of the state can result in crippling fines ranging from 1 to 4 times the total amount of the evaded tax value.35

### **10.2 The Digital Death Penalty: Systemic SISA Suspension**

Beyond traditional monetary fines and physical confiscations, the 2026 regulations reinforce the concept of systemic death penalties for non-compliance. Article 21 and Article 22 of RG 5821/2026 unequivocally state that regulatory non-compliance will lead to the immediate "inactivation" of the offending operator and all their physical plants within the federal SISA registry.1

If ARCA switches a CUIT's SISA status to inactive, the ARCA Web Services will synchronously, and without human intervention, reject all subsequent API requests from that entity. The ERP will begin receiving endless streams of Error Code 502 or 503 ("CUIT not active in required activities").13 This algorithmic lock-out effectively paralyzes the company's ability to buy, sell, process, or transport any agricultural goods within the borders of the Argentine Republic until the fiscal situation is resolved in federal court.

### **10.3 Strategic Implementation Imperatives for Error Mitigation**

Building a compliant ERP module for the Argentine agricultural sector in the 2024-2026 horizon is an exercise in high-availability distributed systems engineering. The integration cannot be treated as a simple data-entry form posting JSON to a REST endpoint; it is mission-critical middleware that connects the physical movement of heavy assets to federal tax databases in real-time.

To mitigate the existential risks posed by Law 11683 and SISA inactivation, ERP architects must implement the following systemic safeguards.
