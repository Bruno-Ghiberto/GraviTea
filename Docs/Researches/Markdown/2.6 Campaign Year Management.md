# Campaign (Campaña) Cycles in Argentine Grain Agriculture: A Guide for Acopiador ERP Software

## Executive Summary

In Argentine grain agriculture, the **campaña** (campaign or crop year) is the foundational dimension around which inventory, contracts, producer accounts, and financial reporting are structured. Unlike a simple calendar year, each grain has its own marketing year that begins when the harvest enters the commercial pipeline. For an acopiador operating ERP software, the campaign is not just a label — it is a legally mandated segregation axis required by AFIP (Argentina's federal tax authority) for all stock declarations, grain movements, and commercial documentation. This report explains the campaign system in detail, its regulatory underpinnings, and its practical implications for ERP design and operation.

## What Defines a Campaign Year

### The Basic Concept

A campaña agrícola in Argentina refers to a complete production-and-marketing cycle for a given grain: from sowing through harvest and into the commercial period during which that grain is traded, stored, and dispatched. The campaign is named using a split-year format (e.g., 2025/26) and each grain's commercial year begins in the month when the new harvest typically enters the market.[^1][^2]

The Bolsa de Comercio de Rosario (BCR) confirms explicitly that "cada cultivo comienza su campaña comercial en diferentes meses del año" — each crop starts its commercial campaign in a different month of the year. For instance, in the 2020/21 cycle, wheat began its new commercial year in December 2020, corn in March 2021, and soybeans in April 2021.[^1]

### Marketing Year Start/End by Grain

The official marketing years used by USDA/FAS, BCR, and Argentine regulatory agencies are as follows:[^3][^2][^4][^1]

| Grain | Spanish Name | Marketing Year | Campaign Example |
|-------|-------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Wheat | Trigo | December – November | 2025/26 starts Dec 2025 |
| Barley | Cebada | December – November | 2025/26 starts Dec 2025 |
| Corn | Maíz | March – February | 2025/26 starts Mar 2026 |
| Sorghum | Sorgo | March – February | 2025/26 starts Mar 2026 |
| Soybean | Soja | April – March | 2025/26 starts Apr 2026 |
| Sunflower | Girasol | March – February | 2025/26 starts Mar 2026 |

The BCR documented that "la campaña de trigo 2021/22 tuvo comienzo el primer día de diciembre del año 2021 y finalizó el 30 de noviembre del año 2022". This confirms that the wheat year is a clean December 1 to November 30 window.[^2]

### Naming Conventions

All campaigns use the split-year "XX/YY" format. However, because fine harvest crops (wheat, barley) are both planted and harvested largely within a single calendar year, informal references sometimes drop the second year — e.g., "campaña trigo 2025" instead of "2025/26". In contrast, coarse harvest crops naturally span two calendar years (planted in year X, harvested in year X+1), making the split-year format intuitive for soja 2025/26 or maíz 2025/26.[^5][^6]

An ERP system should always store the campaign in the full format (e.g., "2025/26") to avoid ambiguity, with the grain type determining the actual date range.

## Fine Harvest (Cosecha Fina) vs. Coarse Harvest (Cosecha Gruesa)

Argentine agriculture divides its annual production into two major seasons. Understanding this division is essential for ERP design because it determines when grain enters the system, when campaigns overlap, and when storage demand peaks.

### Cosecha Fina (Fine Harvest — Winter Crops)

- **Grains**: Wheat (trigo), barley (cebada), oats (avena), rye (centeno), canola/colza, linseed (lino), chickpeas (garbanzo), lentils (lenteja), peas (arveja), triticale, safflower (cártamo)[^7][^8]
- **Sowing**: May through July[^9][^10]
- **Harvest**: November through January[^11][^9]
- **Commercial year begins**: December (wheat/barley)[^3][^2]
- **BCBA Campaign Launch**: The Bolsa de Cereales de Buenos Aires formally launches the "Campaña Fina" around April/May each year with production estimates, area projections, and price analysis[^12][^13]

### Cosecha Gruesa (Coarse Harvest — Summer Crops)

- **Grains**: Soybean (soja), corn (maíz), sorghum (sorgo), sunflower (girasol), peanuts (maní)[^9][^11]
- **Sowing**: September through January (corn in two phases: Sep-Oct and Dec; soy Nov-Jan)[^11][^9]
- **Harvest**: February through July (sunflower Feb-Apr; soy Mar-Jun; corn Mar-Jul)[^14][^9]
- **Commercial year begins**: March (corn, sorghum, sunflower), April (soybeans)[^3][^1]
- **BCBA Campaign Launch**: The "Campaña Gruesa" launch occurs around September/October with initial estimates[^15][^16]

The Bolsa de Comercio de Rosario reported that in early 2026 the first soybean lot of campaign 2025/26 arrived at port in February from an early-maturing crop in Formosa, but the formal commercial remate in Rosario occurs in April.[^17]

## AFIP Regulatory Framework: Campaign as a Legal Axis

The campaign is not merely a customary convention — it is a **legally mandated identification axis** in Argentina's grain traceability and tax system.

### RG 3593/2014 — Systemic Registration of Grain Movements and Stock

AFIP Resolution General 3593/2014 establishes the "Régimen de Registración Sistémica de Movimientos y Existencias de Granos," which requires all registered grain operators (including acopiadores) to declare and track grain stock by three dimensions: **planta** (physical plant), **grano** (grain type), and **cosecha** (campaign).[^18][^19]

The regulation states that "todo movimiento o traslado de granos es precedido por existencias o ingresos previos del mismo grano y de la campaña correspondiente respecto de cada planta de almacenaje" — every grain movement must be preceded by existing stock of the same grain AND the same campaign at that storage plant. If there is a mismatch, the system rejects the transaction automatically.[^18]

This means an ERP system **must** maintain separate stock records per grain per campaign per plant, even if physically the grain is co-mingled in the same silo.

### SISA — Información Productiva

The SISA (Sistema de Información Simplificado Agrícola) requires producers to report stock and production capacity per campaign in two submissions per year:[^20][^7]

- **IP1** (due Sep 1 – Oct 31): Existencias (stock) as of September 30, plus fine harvest planted area. This covers winter crop surface declarations.[^20][^7]
- **IP2** (due Dec 1 – Feb 28): Summer crop (gruesa) planted area declarations.[^8][^20]

### RG 3691 — Certificación Primaria de Depósito (C-1116A)

When an acopiador receives grain from a producer for deposit, a "Certificación Primaria de Depósito de Granos" must be issued. This certificate is filtered and identified by **Productor/Depositante, Grano, Campaña, and Localidad**. The campaign field is mandatory and determines which logical "account" the deposit is credited to.[^21]

### Documentary Chain

The entire regulatory documentary chain relies on campaign identification:[^22][^23]

| Document | Code | Purpose | Campaign Required |
|----------|------|---------|-------------------|
| Certificado de Depósito | C-1116 A | Acknowledges grain receipt/deposit | Yes |
| Compraventa-Liquidación | C-1116 B | Documents purchase transaction | Yes |
| Mandato/Consignación | C-1116 C | Documents consignment sale | Yes |
| Retiro/Transferencia | C-1116 RT | Documents withdrawal/transfer | Yes |
| Carta de Porte Electrónica | CPE | Authorizes grain transport | Yes (via CTG) |
| Liquidación Primaria | LPG (electronic) | Primary settlement to producer | Yes |

## How Campaign Affects Acopiador ERP Operations

### Grain Inventory Tracking

An acopiador's ERP must track stock along three mandatory axes: **plant → grain → campaign**. Even when physical co-mingling occurs in the same silo cell, the AFIP's systemic registration (RG 3593) requires logical segregation by campaign.[^19][^18]

In practice, this means:

- The same silo cell might contain wheat from campaign 2024/25 and wheat from campaign 2025/26 after the new harvest begins in December.
- The ERP tracks these as separate logical lots. Incoming Cartas de Porte with a CTG (Código de Trazabilidad de Granos) are registered against the corresponding campaign.[^18]
- When the AFIP's online system displays stock for declaration, it shows the last 3 campaigns for operators to fill in quantities per plant.[^24]
- Segregation protocols should be applied where practical, and the INTA recommends strict protocols to minimize contamination between crops and campaigns.[^25][^26]

### Producer Current Accounts (Cuentas Corrientes)

Producer current accounts in an acopiador ERP are typically managed **in kilos**, per grain, per campaign. The system works as follows:

- When a producer delivers grain, a C-1116 A (Certificado de Depósito) is issued, crediting the producer's account with the net kilos for that grain and campaign.[^23][^22]
- Storage fees (almacenaje), drying charges (secada), cleaning (zarandeo), and paritaria deductions are applied against the producer's kilo balance within that campaign.
- When the producer sells ("fija precio"), a C-1116 B or C is issued. If the producer withdraws ("retira"), a C-1116 RT is issued.[^23]
- Each campaign's account is separate. A producer may simultaneously have: (a) 200 tons of wheat on deposit from campaign 2024/25, and (b) 500 tons of soybean "a fijar" from campaign 2025/26.

The BCR describes how "a fijar" (price-to-be-fixed) operations constitute the largest volume of acopiador business, with producers frequently delaying price fixation as the campaign progresses.[^27]

### Financial Reporting

Campaign-based financial reporting is essential for an acopiador because revenue and costs must be allocated to the correct production cycle:

- **Revenue**: Commission income (typically ~4% on sales value), storage fees, and conditioning services are earned per campaign.[^28]
- **Cost allocation**: Variable storage costs, handling costs, and shrinkage (merma) are campaign-specific.
- **Storage fee formula**: The BCR's training materials express the acopiador's storage profitability as: A = a × P × m(p) − d × m(a), where *a* is the monthly storage tariff as a percentage of grain value, *P* is the grain price, *m(p)* is months storage is charged, and *d* × *m(a)* represents the actual monthly cost times months stored.[^28]
- **Campaign P&L**: An acopiador should be able to produce a profit and loss statement per campaign to understand the margin on each crop cycle.

### Contract Management

All forward contracts, canje (barter) operations, and "a fijar" agreements are tied to a specific campaign:[^29][^27]

- The AFIP requires mandatory registration of grain purchase contracts through the system, identifying the campaign.[^29]
- Contracts reference "cosecha" as a mandatory field. A contract for "soja campaña 2025/26" commits to grain from that specific production cycle.
- Canje operations (advance of inputs against future grain delivery) are explicitly campaign-bound, as the acopiador provides seed/fertilizer before harvest and receives grain from the designated campaign.[^27]

### Storage Fee Calculation

Storage fees (almacenaje) are not strictly "per campaign" in their calculation — they are typically charged based on the **duration of storage** from the date of deposit. However, the fees are associated with the campaign of the deposited grain:

- The standard tariff is approximately 0.15–0.20% monthly of the grain's market value, with surcharges for certain grains like sunflower (+40%) and soybean (+10%).[^28]
- Fees accrue from the date of the C-1116 A until the grain is sold (C-1116 B/C) or withdrawn (C-1116 RT).
- Since producers from different campaigns may have grain deposited simultaneously, the ERP must calculate fees per producer per campaign.

## Handling Campaign Overlap

Campaign overlap is one of the most operationally complex aspects of an acopiador's business. Here is the typical timeline that creates overlap:

### Timeline of Overlap

1. **November–January**: Wheat/barley harvest from the fina campaign (e.g., 2025/26) begins arriving at the acopio. The wheat commercial year opens December 1.[^2][^9]
2. **February–March**: The first sunflower and early corn lots from the gruesa campaign (also 2025/26) start arriving. Simultaneously, some wheat from the fina may still be in storage — either unsold ("a fijar") or awaiting export dispatch.
3. **April–June**: Peak soybean arrival. The soy commercial year for 2025/26 opens April 1. At this point, the acopio may hold wheat from fina 2025/26, corn from gruesa 2025/26, AND potentially remnant stock from the previous gruesa campaign 2024/25.[^30][^1]

### How the ERP Handles It

- **Logical segregation**: The AFIP system enforces that stock declarations are per grain per campaign. The ERP mirrors this with a composite key: (plant_id, grain_code, campaign_id).[^18]
- **Physical segregation when possible**: Acopiadores try to empty cells between campaigns, but with limited silo capacity, co-mingling of the same grain from different campaigns is common. The "Giro de la Instalación" (plant turnover ratio) measures how efficiently the acopio cycles grain through its capacity.[^27]
- **Carry stock (stock de enlace)**: Grain carried over from a previous campaign into the new one is explicitly tracked. The BCR reports this as "stocks iniciales" at the beginning of each new commercial year. For 2025/26 wheat, the carry from 2024/25 was estimated at 2.3 million tons nationally.[^31]
- **Position netting**: The acopiador calculates a net exposed position across all campaigns and all operations (purchases, "a fijar" holdings, forward sales, canje obligations) to manage price risk.[^27]

## Official Definitions and Institutional Roles

### Who Defines the Campaign?

There is no single law that enumerates the exact start and end dates for each grain's campaign. Instead, the dates are established through a combination of:[^32][^2][^3]

- **Customary commercial practice**: The trading community has long used December–November for wheat, March–February for corn, and April–March for soybeans.
- **USDA/FAS standardization**: The USDA formally lists Argentine marketing years in its PSD (Production, Supply, and Distribution) database. Argentina's wheat MY starts in December, corn in March.[^4][^3]
- **BCR/BCBA commercial reports**: Both the Bolsa de Comercio de Rosario (BCR) and the Bolsa de Cereales de Buenos Aires (BCBA) use these dates consistently in their supply-and-demand balance sheets, campaign launches, and commercialization indicators.[^1][^2]
- **SAGyP/SIO Granos**: The Ministry's grain information system (SIO Granos) publishes monthly commercialization reports aligned to these marketing years. The calendar on the SAGyP website organizes reports by crop and by the corresponding marketing year months.[^33][^34]
- **AFIP/SISA**: While AFIP does not define the exact commercial year, it mandates campaign identification on all stock declarations and movements. The "cosecha" (campaign) field is a required attribute in the systemic registration regime.[^19][^18]

The **International Wheat Agreement** historically defined the "Año Agrícola" for Argentina as August 1 to July 31, but this pertains to the international trade year and is distinct from the domestic commercial campaign that begins in December.[^35]

## Reports Required Per Campaign

An acopiador's ERP should generate the following campaign-specific reports:

### Stock Reports

- **Opening stock position**: Carry-in from the previous campaign by grain and plant, as of the first day of the new commercial year.
- **Closing stock position**: Remaining stock at campaign end (e.g., November 30 for wheat), which becomes the opening stock for the next campaign.
- **AFIP stock declarations**: Stock by planta, grano, and cosecha, aligned with RG 3593 requirements and the SISA deadlines (existencias al 30 de septiembre for IP1).[^20][^18]

### Movement Reports

- **Total receipts (entradas)** per campaign: All Cartas de Porte confirmed as arrivals, segregated by campaign.
- **Total dispatches (salidas)** per campaign: All outbound Cartas de Porte (to exports, industry, or transfers to other plants), by campaign.
- **Internal movements**: Remitos "R" or "X" for intra-plant or intra-company transfers, by campaign.[^18]

### Commercial Settlement Reports

- **Producer settlements per campaign**: Summary of all C-1116 A deposits, C-1116 B/C liquidations, C-1116 RT withdrawals/transfers, and pending "a fijar" balances — all per producer, per grain, per campaign.[^22][^23]
- **Pending "a fijar" position**: Tons of grain deposited but not yet priced, by campaign. The BCR noted that as of January 2025, soybeans had 16.4 million tons nationally without a fixed price, the highest proportion in a decade.[^36]
- **Storage fee liquidation**: Per producer, per campaign — days stored, applicable rate, grain value reference, total charges.

### Regulatory Reports

- **SIO Granos submissions**: Mandatory registration of all purchase/sale contracts, segregated by campaign.[^34]
- **DJVE tracking**: Declaraciones Juradas de Ventas al Exterior (export sales declarations), by campaign.[^34]
- **AFIP Systemic Registration**: Real-time registration of all movements in the "Registro Sistémico de Movimientos y Existencias de Granos".[^19][^18]

## Practical ERP Design Implications

For an acopiador building or configuring ERP software, the campaign dimension should be treated as a **first-class entity** alongside grain type and plant:

- **Master table**: A "Campañas" master table should list each campaign with its grain-specific start/end dates, status (active/closed), and associated parameters.
- **Composite keys**: Every stock, movement, contract, and settlement record should include (plant, grain, campaign) as part of its key structure.
- **Multi-campaign views**: Dashboards should display simultaneous campaigns side by side, since at any given time 2–4 campaigns may be active across different grains.
- **Campaign closing workflow**: A formal process to close a campaign should reconcile stock, verify pending producer balances, calculate final merma (shrinkage), and generate closing stock declarations.
- **Regulatory compliance**: The system must interface with AFIP's web services for CTG generation, CPE emission, Certificación Primaria, Liquidación Primaria, and the systemic stock registration — all of which require campaign identification.[^21][^22][^18]
