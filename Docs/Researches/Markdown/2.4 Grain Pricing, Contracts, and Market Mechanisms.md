# Argentine Grain Pricing & Contract Mechanisms for Acopiadores: An ERP Design Guide

This report provides a comprehensive technical reference for building pricing and contract management features in an ERP system tailored to the Argentine acopiador (grain storage/collection operator). It covers price reference sources, contract types, buying and selling workflows, documentation requirements, and currency considerations, drawing from BCR publications, MATba/ROFEX documentation, ARCA (formerly AFIP) regulations, and industry sources.

***

## Price Reference Sources

Argentine grain pricing relies on several interconnected reference systems. An ERP for acopiadores must integrate with at least two or three of these to support the various contract modalities.

### Pizarra Rosario (Cámara Arbitral de Cereales — CAC/BCR)

The "Pizarra" is the most widely used daily reference price for spot grain in Argentina, published by the Cámara Arbitral de Cereales (CAC) of the Bolsa de Comercio de Rosario (BCR). It has over 120 years of history and is used as the basis for "a fijar" contracts, land rental calculations, and tax assessments.[^1][^2]

**How it is published:**

- Every business day at approximately 10:00 AM, five "semaneros" (weekly rotating committee members) meet at the BCR. The group consists of two buyer representatives, two seller representatives, and one corredor (broker) representative.[^2]
- The semaneros set the price based on information about **the previous day's trades**, meaning whoever fixes by pizarra never transacts at the current day's market price but rather at the prior day's reference.[^3][^4]
- Covers five grains: soja (soybean), maíz (corn), trigo (wheat), girasol (sunflower), and sorgo (sorghum).[^2]
- Only transactions that qualify as "disponible" — available for immediate delivery (within 30 days), cash payment against delivery — are considered. Costs from extended delivery terms or interest on deferred payments are excluded to keep the price "clean".[^2]

**Data inputs for the pizarra calculation:**

- **Operator declarations:** Any BCR member can declare to the CAC the operations they executed the previous day.[^2]
- **MATba/ROFEX data:** Since March 2013, the pizarra incorporates 10% of the volume traded in futures positions with physical delivery option (the portion considered free of speculation and representing genuine buyer-seller transactions).[^5][^2]
- **SIOGranos platform data:** Information from the national mandatory grain operations registry is also used.[^2]

**Programmatic access — BCR API GIX:**

The BCR provides a REST API called "API GIX" for programmatic access to pizarra prices. The key details for ERP integration are:[^6]

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Base URL (Production) | `https://api.bcr.com.ar/gix/index.html` |
| Base URL (UAT) | `https://apiuat.bcr.com.ar/gix/index.html` |
| Authentication | Bearer token via `/Login` endpoint using `api_key` and `secret` |
| Token expiry | 24 hours |
| Endpoint — Prices | `/PreciosCamara?idGrano={id}&fechaConcertacionDesde=YYYY-MM-DD&fechaConcertacionHasta=YYYY-MM-DD` |

**Grain IDs for API:**

|---|---|
| Trigo (Wheat) | 1 |
| Maíz (Corn) | 2 |

The API response includes the pizarra price in ARS/ton, the USD equivalent, the BNA exchange rate used, and a movement indicator (1 = up, 2 = down, 3 = unchanged relative to the prior pizarra).[^6]

**Other access channels:** The CAC also publishes pizarra prices via WhatsApp (+54 9 3415 47-2192) and a chatbot (+54 9 3412 17-5778). The website `cac.bcr.com.ar/es/precios-de-pizarra` displays current values expressed in ARS/ton for available delivery, cash payment, FOB truck/wagon at Rosario zone.[^7][^8]

### MATba/ROFEX Futures

MATba Rofex (now branded as A3 Mercados) operates the Argentine futures and options exchange for agricultural products. Acopiadores use futures prices in two fundamental ways: as a hedging tool and as a price-fixing reference in "a fijar por futuros" contracts.[^9][^10]

**Key contract specifications:**

- Standard futures contracts: 100 tons per contract for soja, maíz, trigo, girasol, sorgo, and cebada, denominated in USD/ton.[^10]
- Mini contracts: 10 tons per contract with financial settlement (no physical delivery), designed for broader market participation.[^11]
- Contracts are available with physical delivery and with financial settlement (cash-settled against the pizarra price, e.g., the ISR — Índice Soja Rosafé).[^10]

**How acopiadores use futures:**

When an acopiador buys grain "a fijar" from a producer and then sells it "a precio" (at a fixed price) to an exporter, the acopiador is effectively short in the physical market. To hedge this exposure, the acopiador would buy futures contracts (cobertura compradora) to offset the risk that prices rise before the producer fixes. The acopiador should compute their **posición expuesta neta** (net exposed position) daily across all operations — compras a fijar, ventas a precio, canje operations, and physical stock — and hedge only the net exposure.[^9]

### Basis ("Base") Calculation

The basis is the difference between the local cash price and the futures reference price. In international commodity markets, the standard formula is: **Basis = Cash Price − Futures Price**.[^12][^13]

In Argentina, the basis reflects multiple factors beyond simple logistics:[^14]

- **Export taxes (derechos de exportación):** 33% for soja, 12% for maíz (as of recent campaigns), which structurally compress local prices below CBOT.
- **Logistics:** Flete (trucking), port handling, river depth (Paraná draft issues), storage costs.
- **FX dynamics:** Gap between official and parallel exchange rates, devaluation expectations.
- **Seasonal factors:** Harvest pressure in March-May, counterseasonal tightness.
- **Quality differentials:** Protein, humidity, damaged grains.

MATba/ROFEX also offers a **Contrato de Futuros de Base** — a standardized basis futures contract where the underlying is the difference between the MATba futures settlement price and the local price for a specific delivery point. This is denominated in USD/ton and settles for multiple delivery months.[^15]

For the ERP, the practical formula used in daily operations is typically expressed as:

> **Local Price (USD/ton) = MATba/ROFEX Future − Basis Discount (or + Basis Premium)**

Or equivalently, referencing CBOT:

> **FOB Price = CBOT + FOB basis premium/discount**
> **FAS Price = FOB Price − Export Taxes − Fobbing Costs**

### CAC Reference Prices vs. Other Cámaras

While Rosario is the primary national reference, other Cámaras Arbitrales exist in Buenos Aires, Córdoba, Bahía Blanca, and Quequén. Bahía Blanca, for instance, often quotes prices as a premium or discount to the Rosario pizarra, particularly for soja and maíz. The Bolsa de Cereales de Buenos Aires also publishes orientative prices from multiple Cámaras.[^16][^17][^18]

### SIOGranos

SIOGranos is the national mandatory electronic platform for registering all grain purchase/sale and canje operations. It was established in 2014/2015 by joint resolution of the Secretaría de Agricultura and the CNV. It provides publicly available price references based on weighted averages of declared operations, offering an alternative data source alongside the pizarra. All RUCA-registered operators must declare their operations through this system.[^19][^20][^21][^22][^23]

## Contract Types for Acopiadores

### Disponible (Spot)

The simplest modality: buyer and seller agree on price, quality, and delivery conditions. Payment is cash against delivery with assured unloading slot ("cupo asegurado"). This is the "most natural" operation — all standard conditions are agreed upon, and the grain transfers immediately. The spot price includes an implicit discount for delivery space when slots are scarce.[^24]

### Forward (Precio Hecho / Cash Forward)

A forward contract fixes the price today for future delivery. The price risk disappears, but it is replaced by counterparty risk (riesgo de incumplimiento contractual) since these are bilateral, unguaranteed agreements.[^9]

**Documentation:** Formalized through a "boleto de compraventa a precio hecho" (purchase/sale ticket at fixed price), which specifies seller and buyer CUIT, grain type, quantity (kg or truck-load), price in ARS or USD per 100 kg, delivery period, payment terms, quality grade ("Grado 2 y demás condiciones cámara" is standard), and delivery destination. These contracts must be registered with ARCA (formerly AFIP) via the "Registro Fiscal de Operadores de Granos" system within 8 calendar days of issuance, using forms F8081 (compraventa contract) or F8082 (oferta de entrega).[^25][^26]

Boletos are also registered (or pre-registered) with the local Bolsa through the "Confirma" digital platform, where seller/buyer CUIT and corredor information are recorded.[^27][^28]

### A Fijar (Price To-Be-Fixed)

This is the dominant modality in Argentine grain trade — estimated at 20% to 40% of the total crop, and for soja, an even higher share. The seller delivers grain to the buyer, and all terms (quality, quantity, delivery) are agreed, but the **price is left open** for the seller to fix at a later date within a contractually agreed deadline.[^29][^17][^30]

**What triggers the price fixing (fijación):**

- **By seller's decision:** The seller (producer or acopiador) communicates to the buyer their wish to "fix" the price. The communication must generally occur before the market opens on the reference day.[^17]
- **By deadline expiry:** If the seller does not exercise the right to fix before the agreed deadline, the price is automatically fixed at the deadline date's reference value. For contracts exceeding 1,000 tons without specific volume limits, the seller may not fix more than 20% of the total daily.[^17]

**Price-fixing modalities:**

| Modality | Description |
|---|---|
| Pizarra ciega | Seller fixes before knowing the day's pizarra (before ~10:30 AM). Most common[^17]. |
| Pizarra tuerta | Seller fixes before the market opens or 30 min before CBOT close, whichever is first[^17]. |
| Pizarra vista | Seller fixes after seeing the pizarra. Rare, as it disadvantages the buyer[^17]. |
| Precio de mercado | Seller fixes at the buyer's market price during the trading day. Growing in use[^17]. |
| Por futuros (MATba/ROFEX) | Both buyer and seller independently fix through futures positions[^17][^31]. |

**Documents generated:**

- A contract ("contrato a fijar") is issued at grain delivery, specifying all terms except price.
- Upon each partial or total fijación, a **fijación order** is recorded.
- The final **Formulario C-1116 B** (Compraventa-Liquidación) is generated once the price is fixed, documenting the transaction with all retenciones (tax withholdings) and deductions.[^32][^33]

**PAF Digital Garantizado (MATba/ROFEX):** A newer instrument approved by the CNV where "a fijar" operations are registered on the Confirma platform and guaranteed by MATba/ROFEX. The buyer deposits 110% of grain value as collateral, and both parties can fix independently via futures. This was developed partly in response to the Vicentin default crisis to provide counterparty security.[^29][^27]

### Canje (Barter)

In a canje operation, the producer or acopiador acquires inputs (fertilizers, agrochemicals, seeds, machinery, fuel) and pays by delivering grain instead of cash.[^34][^35]

**How the exchange ratio is determined:**

- The equivalence is typically expressed as "X tons of grain per Y units of input" (relación de canje / relación insumo-producto).
- The ratio is calculated by dividing the input price (in USD or ARS) by the grain price (usually the current futures price or available price at the time of agreement).[^36]
- These ratios are tracked and published by firms like Az Group, which monitor how many tons of soja, maíz, or trigo are needed to buy a given quantity of fertilizer, fuel, or pesticide. The ratios change daily with grain and input prices.[^36]

**How it works operationally:**

- The acopiador issues a **Liquidación de Venta de Granos (LSG)** for the grain delivered. This is compensated against the **Factura de Compra** for the inputs acquired. No cash changes hands — the invoices are netted.[^35][^34]
- This avoids the Impuesto a las Transferencias Bancarias (bank transaction tax) and can reduce Ingresos Brutos withholdings, since there is no cash flow through banks.[^34]

**Risk for the acopiador:** If a canje is agreed at a certain grain-equivalent price and grain prices decline before delivery, the acopiador receives grain worth less than the credit extended. The acopiador is therefore "long" in the physical market during this period.[^9]

### Minimum Price / Price Floor Contracts

While not a formal "named" contract type in Argentine regulations, minimum price mechanisms exist through the options market. A producer or acopiador can establish a price floor by **purchasing put options** on MATba/ROFEX. This guarantees a minimum sale price (the strike price minus the premium) while preserving upside if prices rise. The BCR publication on acopio explicitly discusses this as a hedging strategy for acopiadores who are exposed to price risk on their physical positions.[^9]

Some larger acopiadores or exporters may offer proprietary "precio mínimo" contracts to producers as a commercial tool, combining an "a fijar" with an embedded put option. The specific terms vary by operator and are not standardized.

## How an Acopiador Buys Grain

An acopiador can operate in two distinct roles, and the ERP must support both:[^37][^38]

### Compraventa (Outright Purchase)

The acopiador buys the grain from the producer, taking ownership. This is documented through:[^33][^32]

1. **Carta de Porte / CTG:** The physical transport document and electronic traceability code that accompanies the grain from farm to plant.
2. **Formulario C-1116 A (Certificado de Depósito Intransferible):** Issued upon receipt at the plant, documenting the type, volume, and quality of grain received, along with applicable service tariffs (secado, zarandeo, almacenaje). This is always issued regardless of whether the sale is compraventa or consignación.[^32]
3. **Formulario C-1116 B (Compraventa-Liquidación):** This is the core purchase document. It records the purchase price, grain grade, quality adjustments, payment terms, and all applicable tax withholdings (IVA, IIBB, Ganancias under SISA/ARCA rules). It must be issued electronically since March 2013 per RG 3419.[^39][^33][^32]

### Depósito / Consignación

The acopiador receives and stores the grain on behalf of the producer without purchasing it. The producer retains ownership. Two outcomes are then possible:[^37]

- The acopiador (depositario) itself buys the grain later from the producer — issuing the C-1116 B at that point.
- The acopiador sells the grain to a third party (exporter, industry) **on behalf of the producer** — acting as consignatario and issuing a **Formulario C-1116 C (Mandato/Consignación-Liquidación)**, which is similar to the C-1116 B but reflects a consignment sale where the acopiador earns a commission rather than a margin.[^37][^32]

### Payment Timeline

Payment terms are variable and are a key part of the commercial negotiation:

- **Cash against delivery (contado contra entrega):** Payment upon grain receipt and quality determination.
- **Partial upfront + balance at term:** Common in forward and a fijar contracts. The boleto may specify, e.g., a percentage as partial payment and the balance at 30 days from final liquidation.[^25]
- **Deferred payment:** The C-1116 B must be issued within 72 hours of the last discharge. Settlement then follows per contracted terms. In "a fijar" contracts, payment occurs upon fijación — the producer may take months to fix, effectively using the acopiador as a savings account that pays the grain price at the moment of fixing minus accumulated storage costs.[^30][^37]

### Formulario C-1116 RT (Retiros y Transferencias)

If grain deposited under a C-1116 A is transferred to another location or withdrawn by the producer without a sale, the **C-1116 RT** document is issued. This covers all movements of grain that do not involve a transfer of ownership documented by a C-1116 B or C.[^32]

## How an Acopiador Sells Grain

### Typical Buyers

The acopiador's downstream clients include:[^40][^38]

- **Exportadores:** The largest buyers (Cargill, Bunge, COFCO, Dreyfus, Viterra/Glencore, AGD, ACA, etc.). They originate grain through their own acopio networks and through independent acopiadores. Approximately 45% of internal grain commercialization flows through the acopio system.[^38]
- **Industria:** Oilseed crushers (aceiteras), flour mills (molinos harineros), feed manufacturers, ethanol plants.
- **Otros acopios / cooperativas:** Secondary trading between storage operators.

### Role of Corredores (Brokers)

Corredores are intermediaries that connect sellers (acopiadores, cooperativas) with buyers (exporters, industry). Argentina has approximately 287 registered grain brokerage firms, with the highest concentration in Rosario (~41% of national total) and Buenos Aires (~50%).[^41]

**Key characteristics:**

- Corredores **never take ownership** of the grain. They act as facilitators, earning a variable commission from one or both parties.[^41]
- They subscribe the boleto de compraventa in the physical grain market and may handle payments on behalf of the seller.[^41]
- They provide market intelligence, logistics coordination, financing facilitation, and commercial strategy advice.[^41]
- In operations with a corredor, the corredor issues the **Liquidación Secundaria de Granos (LSG)**. Without a corredor, the selling acopiador emits the LSG directly.[^42]
- Corredores must be registered in the RUCA (Registro Único de la Cadena Agroalimentaria).[^41]

### Sales Documentation

When an acopiador sells grain (secondary operation), the following documents are generated:[^39][^42]

| Document | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Boleto de compraventa (or contrato) | Formalizes the sale terms. Registered on Confirma / SIOGranos. |
| Carta de Porte + CTG | Transport document for physical dispatch from plant. |
| LSG (Liquidación Secundaria de Granos) | Settlement document for secondary sales. Issued by corredor (if involved) or acopiador. |
| Factura (Invoice) | Standard commercial invoice for the sale. |
| SIOGranos declaration | Mandatory registration of the operation within regulatory deadlines. |

For primary operations (first sale from producer), the C-1116 B is the key document. For secondary operations (acopio-to-exporter), the LSG replaces the C-1116 B.[^43][^42]

## Currency Considerations

### Denomination of Grain Prices

Grain prices in Argentina exist in a dual-denomination environment:[^44][^45]

- **Futures prices (MATba/ROFEX):** Quoted in USD/ton for standard delivery contracts. Peso-denominated futures also exist.
- **Pizarra prices (CAC):** Published in ARS/ton as the primary reference. Since recent reforms, the pizarra also shows a USD equivalent calculated using the BNA divisas comprador (buyer) rate.[^8][^6]
- **Contracts:** Can be denominated in either USD or ARS. However, **payment to the producer is always made in pesos** — even for USD-denominated contracts.[^44]
- **FOB reference prices:** Published in USD/ton by the Secretaría de Agricultura based on exporter declarations.[^46]

### Exchange Rate for Settlement

The official regulation (Disposición 9/2019) establishes that contracts denominated in USD must be settled using the **tipo de cambio comprador del Banco de la Nación Argentina (BNA) para divisas** on the business day immediately preceding the effective payment date.[^47]

In practice, this means:

| Rate Type | Usage |
|---|---|
| BNA Divisas Comprador | Official settlement rate for grain contracts per regulation[^47]. Also used by CAC for pizarra USD conversion[^6]. |
| BNA Billetes Compra/Venta | Retail banknote rate, not typically used for grain settlements. |
| CCL / MEP | Parallel financial rates. Not legally applicable to grain settlements, but acopiadores may use them for internal valuation or financial planning. |

The BCR API GIX `/Cotizacion` endpoint returns both the BNA billetes and divisas rates (compra and venta), making it possible for an ERP to automate the FX conversion.[^6]

### Practical Impact

Since Argentina's exchange rate regime has undergone multiple modifications (including the "dólar soja" programs in 2022-2023, and the crawling-peg/band system under the current administration), the effective USD value of grain operations can vary significantly depending on when and how the conversion occurs. An ERP should store both the ARS contract price and the applicable USD equivalent (with the exchange rate used and its source) for every transaction, as this is essential for profitability analysis and for matching against USD-denominated hedging positions on MATba/ROFEX.[^48][^49]
