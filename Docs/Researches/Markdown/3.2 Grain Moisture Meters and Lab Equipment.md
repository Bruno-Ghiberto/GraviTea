# Grain Quality Analysis Equipment Used by Acopiadores in Argentina

## Executive Summary

Argentine acopiadores rely on a well-defined set of laboratory instruments for grain quality assessment during reception. The core equipment revolves around moisture meters (from Delver, DICKEY-john, and Motomco), test weight (peso hectolítrico) measurement, and mechanical sieves for dockage determination. Near-infrared (NIR) protein/oil analyzers — primarily from FOSS and Perten — remain largely confined to medium-to-large operations and port terminals due to their cost. Most instruments in use today have digital output ports (USB, RS-232, and increasingly Ethernet/LAN), making direct ERP integration technically feasible — though manual data entry remains the norm at small acopios.

***

## Grain Moisture Meters: Brands and Models

### Delver (Argentine Manufacturer)

Delver S.A. is an Argentine manufacturer with strong market penetration among small and mid-sized acopiadores. Their main models are:

- **HD-1021-USB** — A fully portable moisture meter with built-in scale and USB 1.0 connectivity. It measures humidity and peso hectolítrico for 120+ materials, with precision of ±0.3% at 12% moisture. Delver explicitly markets its USB software as enabling "transfer of measurement results to a management system" (sistema de gerenciamiento), making it suitable for grain storage plants.[^1]
- **HD-1023-ECO** — An automatic benchtop unit with Dig-RF digital radio-frequency technology, built-in thermal printer, and USB 1.0 port. Designed for port terminals and acopio plants, it achieves ±0.2% precision. The integrated printer produces tickets for cartas de porte or sample bags. The Dig-RF technology improves long-term calibration stability against temperature drift — a critical factor during harvest campaigns.[^2][^3]

### DICKEY-john (USA, Distributed in Argentina)

DICKEY-john is the dominant brand in larger acopio operations, port terminals, and exporters throughout Argentina and South America. Distributed by AG-Tech SRL in Marcos Juárez, Córdoba, and serviced by TecnoCientifica.[^4][^5]

- **GAC 2100BSA** — The established workhorse, described as "the equipment adopted by the majority of exporters, port terminals, large acopios, and a large part of the grain trade chain in Argentina and South America". It features fully automated operation, up to 64 calibrations in memory, and two RS-232 serial output ports (DB25M) for printer and external computer/modem connection.[^5][^6][^7]
- **GAC 2500-INTL** — The next-generation model using 149 MHz UGMA (Unified Grain Moisture Algorithm) technology. It offers a color touchscreen interface, unlimited calibrations, and significantly expanded connectivity: 4 USB ports, 1 USB-B for PC connection, 1 LAN/Ethernet port, and 1 RS-232 serial port. It supports data export in CSV and Excel formats and includes a network setup option for centralized data management.[^8][^9]
- **Mini GAC 2500** — A portable handheld analyzer with UGMA technology, Bluetooth capability for sending data directly to iOS or Android devices, and USB connectivity.[^10][^11]

### Motomco (Brazil)

Motomco is widely used in Brazil and has presence in Mercosur countries. Their equipment is typically found in larger classification rooms.

- **999-ES** — A semi-automatic unit measuring moisture, temperature, peso hectolítrico, impurities, and damage. It features RS-232 serial (DB9), USB-B, and USB for pendrive. Stores up to 100,000 measurements and includes a touchscreen and integrated printer.[^12][^13]
- **999-ESI** — The fully automatic industrial version adds Ethernet and Wi-Fi connectivity alongside RS-232 and USB, designed for continuous high-throughput operation in classification rooms.[^14]

### Others

- **Steinlite SB900** — Supports 500+ calibrations for specialty grains and nuts, primarily distributed in Mexico and Chile. Less common in Argentina's grain corridor.[^15]
- **Perten AM 5200-A** — Widely used in the US grain trade (UGMA/NTEP certified), with Ethernet and USB connectivity. Some presence in South America through PerkinElmer distribution.[^16][^17]

### Proven ERP/Scale System Integrations

Real-world integration between grain moisture testers and management software already exists in the North American market — providing a clear blueprint for Argentine ERP developers:

- **Agvance Grain** (SSI/Cultura Technologies) connects to the DICKEY-john GAC 2500 via COM port (RS-232), automatically reading moisture and test weight values into scale tickets. The output format is set to CSV01 with CRLF line termination.[^20]
- **Agvance** also integrates with the **Perten AM 5200-A** over Ethernet using UDP protocol, with the moisture tester transmitting to the scale PC's IP address.[^16]
- **Fairbanks Scales** integrated the Perten AM 5200-A via serial cable, capturing moisture, test weight, temperature, and sample ID directly into transaction records.[^21]
- The **Steinlite SL95** interfaces with Agvance via RS-232 COM port, transmitting CSV-formatted results including moisture, test weight, grain ID, and timestamps.[^22]

These integrations demonstrate that the protocol and data format requirements are well-documented and relatively straightforward — typically serial CSV output over RS-232 or UDP packets over Ethernet.

## NIR Analyzers for Protein and Oil Content

### FOSS (Distributed by Scancotec in Argentina)

FOSS instruments are the global standard for grain analysis in commercial trade:[^23][^24]

- **Infratec NOVA** (current generation) — The flagship grain analyzer using transmittance NIR in the 400–1100 nm range. It measures moisture, protein, oil, test weight, starch, wet gluten, fiber, and ash in under 60 seconds with no sample preparation. It features a 10-inch capacitive touchscreen, Ethernet connectivity, and 3 USB ports. Results can be sent directly to a PC/LIMS and to a printer. The **FossConnect™** cloud networking platform enables remote instrument management, configuration, and data centralization.[^25][^18][^26]
- **Infratec 1241** — The long-established predecessor, still widely in service. Uses transmittance NIR (570–1100 nm) with a modular design including optional flour module and test weight module. Over 10,000 units have been installed globally.[^27][^28]
- **Infratec Sofia** — A lower-cost, mobile NIR analyzer specifically designed to "bring the value of precision grain analysis to reception warehouses of all sizes." Standardized against the Infratec 1241 database, it measures moisture, protein, and oil with protein predictability within 0.1%. It operates from -4°C to +40°C, making it suitable for outdoor and unheated facilities.[^29][^30]
- **NIRS DA1650** — A robust benchtop reflectance NIR (1100–1650 nm) primarily aimed at oilseed crush operations, feed mills, and animal nutrition. It comes precalibrated with ANN global calibrations and features IP65 dust/waterproofing and Mosaic networking software.[^31][^32]

### Perten/PerkinElmer (Distributed by Granotec Argentina)

Granotec Argentina distributes Perten instruments and markets them alongside gluten testing equipment (Glutomatic, Falling Number):[^33]

- **Inframatic 9500** — A high-precision benchtop NIR analyzer (570–1100 nm, scanning monochromator) for grain and flour. It features 4 USB ports + 1 Ethernet (RJ45) and a 12-inch color touchscreen. Measures moisture, protein, oil, starch, wet gluten, and Zeleny index in under 50 seconds.[^34][^19]
- **Inframatic 8800** — A portable battery-operated NIR analyzer (850–1050 nm, diode array) with built-in GPS for field-level protein and moisture mapping. Designed for use on trucks, harvesters, and silos with up to 2 hours of battery life.[^35][^33]

### Who Uses NIR in Argentina?

NIR protein analyzers represent a significant capital investment. Based on industry patterns and the INTA survey of 86 acopio plants across Argentina's main grain region:[^36][^37]

- **Port terminals and exporters** — Routinely equipped with FOSS Infratec or Perten IM 9500 for grading and segregation by protein content (especially for wheat destined for export markets with protein premiums).
- **Large acopios and cooperatives** — Increasingly adopting NIR for premium grain segregation and value capture, particularly those handling wheat and soybean.
- **Small acopios** — The INTA study found that small plants "presented significant limitations in their current equipment." NIR adoption at this tier is minimal, though the FOSS Infratec Sofia was specifically designed to lower the entry barrier for smaller grain reception facilities.[^30][^38]

## Typical Laboratory Setup at a Small Acopio

A small acopiador's lab (sala de recibo or laboratorio de calado) follows a standardized workflow for grain reception based on Argentine commercial norms and the standards described in regional best-practice guides:[^39][^40][^41]

### Essential Equipment (Always Present)

1. **Caladores (grain probes)** — Stainless steel sampling probes (e.g., 10-compartment S10) used to extract representative samples from trucks.[^39]
2. **Divisor/cuarteador de muestras (Boerner divider)** — To homogenize and quarter-divide bulk samples into test portions.[^39]
3. **Moisture meter** — Always present, typically a Delver HD-1021-USB or HD-1023-ECO for smaller operations, or a DICKEY-john GAC 2100 for medium-sized ones.[^5][^1]
4. **Peso hectolítrico determination** — Either via a traditional Schopper balance (¼ liter capacity) with conversion tables, or increasingly through the moisture meter itself if it has a PH function (both the Delver HD-1023 and GAC 2100/2500 include this).[^42][^5]
5. **Zarandas (sieves)** — Standard-mesh sieves (e.g., 4.76 mm for wheat) for determining dockage (materia extraña/merma). The sample is passed through sieves and impurities are weighed.[^41][^39]
6. **Visual inspection equipment** — Bandeja (tray), lupa (magnifying glass), and occasionally a diaphanoscope for inspecting damaged, heated, or insect-infested grains.[^40]

### Optional / Larger Operations

7. **NIR protein/oil analyzer** — FOSS Infratec Sofia (entry-level) or Infratec NOVA/Perten IM 9500 for full protein, oil, and wet gluten analysis. Only in medium-to-large operations or cooperatives that segregate grain by quality.[^30]
8. **Falling Number system** — For alpha-amylase determination in wheat (sprouting risk). Present in operations supplying flour mills.[^33]
9. **Glutomatic system** — For wet gluten content and Gluten Index measurement.[^33]

## ERP Integration: Feasibility and Realistic Approach

### Technical Feasibility

From a hardware perspective, direct instrument-to-ERP integration is entirely feasible. The GAC 2500-INTL already supports LAN connectivity, CSV data streaming over RS-232, and USB data export. The FOSS Infratec instruments support direct LIMS data transmission over Ethernet. Even the older GAC 2100 can send data to a PC via its dual RS-232 ports.[^6][^18][^9]

For instruments with only RS-232 output, inexpensive serial-to-Ethernet converters (serial device servers) can bridge the connection to a local network, creating virtual COM ports accessible by any PC on the LAN.[^43]

The typical data flow for integration would be:

- **Instrument** → outputs CSV string (moisture %, test weight, temperature, grain type, timestamp)
- **RS-232/USB/Ethernet** → physical or virtual serial connection to a PC or directly to the network
- **Middleware/listener service** → a lightweight application that reads the serial stream or UDP packet and pushes data to the ERP via API
- **ERP** → receives quality data and associates it with the active scale ticket or incoming shipment

### Realistic Assessment for Small Acopiadores

Despite the technical capability, the realistic picture for small acopios in Argentina is nuanced:

- **Manual entry remains dominant** — Most small acopiadores still rely on handwritten lab tickets or manual data entry into their management systems. The INTA study highlighted that small plants have "significant equipment limitations" both in capacity and technology adoption.[^38]
- **Cost sensitivity** — Small acopios operate on thin margins. While a Delver HD-1021-USB costs relatively little, the effort to develop and maintain custom integration software may not be justified for low-volume operations.
- **Intermediate approach** — A pragmatic strategy for an ERP designed for small acopiadores would be to offer **optional instrument connectivity as an upgrade path** while defaulting to a well-designed manual entry interface. The ERP could include a simple serial listener module that reads CSV data from any RS-232-equipped instrument (Delver, DICKEY-john, Motomco) and auto-populates the quality fields.
- **USB data export as a bridge** — Even without real-time integration, most modern instruments support USB pendrive data export. The ERP could include a CSV import function that reads batch results from a pendrive, matching measurements to load records by timestamp or sample ID.

### Integration Prioritization

For an ERP targeting Argentine acopiadores, the recommended integration approach would be:

1. **First priority** — Robust manual entry with validation rules (e.g., moisture ranges per grain type, PH bounds) to minimize errors.
2. **Second priority** — CSV file import from USB pendrive (compatible with Delver, GAC 2500, Motomco data exports).
3. **Third priority** — Serial port listener for real-time data capture from RS-232 instruments (GAC 2100, GAC 2500, Motomco 999).
4. **Long-term** — Ethernet/network integration for facilities with FOSS Infratec or GAC 2500 on LAN.
