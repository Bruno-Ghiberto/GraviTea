# Trade Associations, Events & Networking Channels for Acopiadores de Granos in Argentina

## Executive Summary

Argentina's approximately 1,000 independent grain storage and trading companies (*acopiadores*) handle around 40% of the country's interior grain storage infrastructure—an investment exceeding US$3 billion. They serve roughly 70% of the nation's producers, making them a critical but concentrated market. This report maps the institutional ecosystem, industry events, media landscape, and regional clusters that define how to reach and engage this sector, with particular attention to the Villa María–Marcos Juárez–Bell Ville corridor in Córdoba.[^1][^2]

***

## Trade Associations and Chambers

### Federación de Centros y Entidades Gremiales de Acopiadores de Cereales

The **Federación de Acopiadores** is the single most important institution for reaching the acopiador market. It is the national second-degree entity (federation of regional associations) founded in **1946**, headquartered at Corrientes 119/127 PB, Buenos Aires.[^3][^2]

- **President**: Fernando Rivara (in office since 2014, reelected for 2025–2026)[^3]
- **Website**: [www.acopiadores.com](http://www.acopiadores.com)[^4]
- **Membership**: 18 regional *Centros y Sociedades* representing **more than 1,000 acopiador companies** nationwide[^5][^3]
- **Key role**: Institutional representation before government, organizer of the flagship *A Todo Trigo* and *Agrotendencias* congresses, professional advisory services, and press/communications arm via SAVIA Comunicación[^2][^6]
- **Press contact**: SAVIA Comunicación, 011-4545-7734[^7]

The Federación is the primary gateway to the entire acopiador sector. Any B2B marketing or partnership strategy should begin here.

### Bolsas de Cereales and Bolsas de Comercio

These exchanges serve as institutional anchors for grain trade, providing price discovery, arbitration, laboratories, and training—all functions that acopiadores rely on daily.

- **Bolsa de Cereales de Buenos Aires** (founded 1854): The country's oldest business entity. Publishes crop estimates, runs the *Congreso de Perspectivas Agrícolas*, and offers extensive training (Futuros y Opciones, Negocios y Contratos del Agro).[^14][^15][^16]
- **Bolsa de Comercio de Rosario (BCR)**: Argentina's largest grain exchange. Houses the *Cámara Arbitral de Cereales de Rosario* (founded 1899), the nation's oldest arbitral body. Runs specialized courses on grain quality and arbitration.[^17][^18][^19]
- **Bolsa y Cámara de Cereales de Córdoba (BCCBA)**: Founded 1966. Acts as Tribunal Arbitral, operates a certified grain laboratory, publishes the *pizarra* (reference prices), and hosts technical courses including the "*Manejo Administrativo del Acopio*" course taught by Federación de Acopiadores staff. The Sociedad de Acopiadores de Córdoba is a member entity.[^20][^21][^22]
- **Bolsa de Cereales de Bahía Blanca** and **Bolsa de Cereales de Entre Ríos** round out the regional exchange network.[^23]

### Cámaras Arbitrales de Cereales

These bodies are critical to the daily operations of acopiadores—they set pizarra prices, resolve commercial disputes, and certify grain quality.

- **Cámara Arbitral de la Bolsa de Cereales de Buenos Aires** (founded 1905): A plurisectoral body with **3 acopiador seats** among its 22 members. Functions include arbitration, quality determination, and price setting.[^24][^25]
- **Cámara Arbitral de Cereales de Rosario** (founded 1899): Operates within the BCR. Acopiadores from the Sociedad Gremial de Rosario serve as members.[^26][^24]
- A national network of Cámaras Arbitrales operates under a uniform regulatory framework (Decree 1918/81), with additional bodies in Bahía Blanca, Santa Fe, and other cities.[^27][^19]

### CONINAGRO and the Cooperative Sector

**CONINAGRO** (Confederación Intercooperativa Agropecuaria) groups over 350 cooperatives and 150,000+ producers. While not specifically for independent acopiadores, the cooperative acopio sector is significant: approximately **155 cooperatives** operate **476 grain storage plants** across the country. Major cooperative operators include:[^28][^29]

- **ACA (Asociación de Cooperativas Argentinas)**: Originates and trades over 23 million tonnes/year—roughly one-sixth of national production.[^30]
- **AFA (Agricultores Federados Argentinos)**: A major second-degree cooperative.
- **CAFER** (Entre Ríos) and **UCAL** (Chaco): Important regional cooperative federations.[^28]

CONINAGRO is relevant for reaching *cooperative acopios* but is not the channel for independent acopiador companies, which are served by the Federación.

### Other Institutional Bodies

- **CIARA-CEC** (Cámara de la Industria Aceitera / Centro Exportador de Cereales): Represents major exporters (Cargill, Bunge, COFCO, LDC, etc.) who are buyers from acopiadores, not acopiadores themselves.[^31][^32]
- **Consejo Agroindustrial Argentino (CAA)**: A broad coalition of 60+ ag industry chambers. Includes Bolsas de Cereales, CIARA-CEC, Acsoja, Maizar, Argentrigo, and Asagir, but the Federación de Acopiadores is not a listed direct member.[^33][^23]
- **APOSGRAN (Asociación Argentina de Poscosecha de Granos)**: Founded 1985 in Rosario, housed in the BCR. A technical non-profit focused on postharvest training and capacity building. Runs the *JORNATEC* (Jornadas Técnicas en Poscosecha de Granos) and courses for plant operators, encargados, and recibidores. Highly relevant for reaching acopio plant-level personnel.[^34][^35]

## Industry Events

### Acopiador-Specific Events

#### A Todo Trigo — THE Flagship Acopiador Congress

The single most important event for reaching acopiadores. Organized by the **Federación de Acopiadores** since 2004, it has grown into the largest wheat and winter crops congress in the Spanish-speaking world.[^36][^1]

- **2026 edition**: May 14–15, Hotel Sheraton, Mar del Plata[^1]
- **Attendance**: ~1,500 in-person + ~2,000 virtual participants[^1]
- **Format**: 60+ expert speakers across 3 simultaneous tracks (agronomy, markets/policy, technology)[^37][^36]
- **Audience**: Acopiador company owners, managers, producers, input suppliers, government officials, Bolsa de Cereales analysts[^1]
- **Why it matters**: This is *the* event where the Federación's membership concentrates. Every major input supplier, tech company, and service provider targeting acopiadores sponsors or exhibits here. It combines a strong commercial dimension with top-tier content featuring government secretaries, INTA researchers, and market analysts.[^38][^36]
- **Contact for sponsorship**: SEMA events (eventos@sema.com.ar); Cristián Favereau (cfavereau@sema.com.ar, +54 9 11 4538 2570)[^1]

#### Agrotendencias

A secondary congress also organized by the **Federación de Acopiadores**, held at the **Bolsa de Cereales de Buenos Aires**. Focuses on macroeconomic and policy analysis relevant to the grain sector. Smaller and more policy-oriented than A Todo Trigo, but attended by decision-makers in the acopio chain.[^6]

#### Grano SAC — Expo Feria Internacional Poscosecha

The premier event specifically focused on **postharvest operations**—the core technical domain of grain acopio. Organized by APOSGRAN with over **40 years of trajectory**.[^39]

- **2025 edition**: November 5–6, Hotel Ariston, Rosario[^40][^39]
- **Content**: Silo bolsa technology, grain drying and aeration, pest control, regulatory compliance, cubicaje (volume measurement), new storage technologies[^39]
- **Audience**: Plant managers, technical personnel, equipment suppliers, INTA researchers
- **Relevance**: Extremely high for any company selling storage infrastructure, pest control, monitoring equipment, or postharvest services to acopiadores[^39]

#### APOSGRAN JORNATEC (Jornadas Técnicas en Poscosecha de Granos)

Periodic technical workshops run by APOSGRAN at the BCR in Rosario, targeting plant-level operators, recibidores, and encargados de planta. These are more intimate, hands-on training sessions—ideal for product demonstrations and technical selling.[^34]

### Major Agricultural Expos (Broad Audience, Acopiadores Present)

| Event | Date (typical) | Location | Relevance for Acopiadores |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ExpoAgro** | March (2026: March 10–13) | San Nicolás, Buenos Aires | 700+ exhibitors, 220,000+ visitors. Broad audience; ACA and cooperatives have strong presence with acopio-related exhibits. Good for brand visibility but diluted targeting[^41][^42] |
| **AgroActiva** | June (2025: June 4–7) | Armstrong, Santa Fe | 270,000+ visitors. Strong presence of Bolsas de Cereales de Buenos Aires and Córdoba. Heart of Santa Fe grain country—many local acopiadores attend[^43][^44][^45] |

Both ExpoAgro and AgroActiva are valuable for broad brand-building but lack the focused audience concentration of A Todo Trigo or Grano SAC.

### Chain-Level Congresses

- **Congreso Maizar**: Organized by the maize value chain association. Multi-stakeholder (producers, industry, exporters). Acopiadores may attend but are not the core audience.[^46]
- **Acsoja / Argentrigo / Asagir**: The four major crop chains (soy, wheat, sunflower, maize) hold joint press conferences at ExpoAgro and individual events. They engage acopiadores indirectly as part of the broader chain.[^47]
- **Congreso de Perspectivas Agrícolas** (Bolsa de Cereales de Buenos Aires): Annual event with crop production estimates and economic outlook. Attended by commercial operators including acopiadores.[^48]

### Technical Training (High-Value Touchpoints)

- **BCR courses**: "Calidad y Arbitraje en el Comercio de Granos"—a multi-day practical course covering grain standards, arbitration procedures, and laboratory analysis. Acopiador staff regularly attend.[^18][^17]
- **BCCBA courses**: "Manejo Administrativo del Acopio" taught by Federación staff (Ing. Armando Casalins). Covers cost management, RUCA regulations, commercial documentation.[^20]
- **Bolsa de Cereales de Buenos Aires**: "Futuros y Opciones Agrícolas" and "Negocios y Contratos del Agro"—important for acopiador commercial teams.[^15][^16]
- **INTA Balcarce Postharvest Program**: Hands-on courses on grain management, aeration system design, moisture control, and SLAM (grain management strategy). Taught by world-class researchers including Ing. Ricardo Bartosik.[^49][^50]

### Specialized Postharvest Content

- **APOSGRAN magazine** (*Revista APOSGRAN*): Published by the Asociación Argentina de Poscosecha de Granos. Technical articles on grain storage, drying, pest control, and regulatory compliance. Distributed to APOSGRAN members and available at postharvest events.[^57]
- **INTA postharvest publications**: The INTA Balcarce group produces the reference *Manual de Buenas Prácticas en Poscosecha de Granos*, authored by Bartosik and Abadía, widely used as a training resource in acopio plants.[^49]
- **Revista Chacra**: Features periodic coverage of postharvest diagnostics and storage technology, often with INTA sourcing.[^58]

### Institutional Circulars

The **Sociedad de Acopiadores de Córdoba** distributes regular digital circulars to its members covering market intelligence, regulatory updates, and event announcements. These circulars are archived on their website (acopiadorescba.com) and represent a trusted internal communication channel for Córdoba-based acopiadores.[^9][^59]

### YouTube and Social Media

No single YouTube channel is dedicated exclusively to acopio operations. However, relevant video content exists across:

- **Agrofy News Live**: Streaming market analysis and interviews[^60]
- **Bolsa de Cereales de Buenos Aires** and **BCR** YouTube channels: Event recordings and educational content
- **Bichos de Campo**: Strong social media presence with agro-influencer-style content reaching field-level audiences[^61][^55]
- Various INTA channels covering postharvest technology

## Regional Acopiador Clusters

### National Distribution

Argentina's grain postharvest system comprises approximately **4,150 storage plants** with an aggregate capacity of ~71 million tonnes. The five core provinces—**Buenos Aires, Santa Fe, Córdoba, La Pampa, and Entre Ríos**—account for **90% of grain production and 88% of all acopios**. RUCA records show **2,151 plants** registered under the *Acopiador Consignatario* category.[^62][^63][^58]

The 16 largest acopio companies alone operate 406 plants, representing 18% of the total registered base—meaning the vast majority of the industry consists of **small and medium family-owned enterprises** that are the Federación's core constituency.[^63][^30]

### The Villa María – Marcos Juárez – Bell Ville Corridor (Southeastern Córdoba)

This is the heart of Córdoba's grain belt and a dense cluster of acopiador operations. The Sociedad de Acopiadores de Córdoba member list (updated March 2025) reveals significant membership in this exact corridor:[^10]

- **Villa María**: ALEMAR S.A., CAON ATILIO J Y CAON MIGUEL A S.H., HECTOR A. BERTONE S.A., JJST S.A.S., VILLA MARIA CEREALES S.R.L.[^10]
- **Bell Ville**: JOSE DEL RE S.A.[^10]
- **Marcos Juárez area** (Cruz Alta, General Roca, Noetinger, Inriville, Camilo Aldao): AGL S.R.L. (Cruz Alta), ALFREDO S. MONDINO CEREALES S.R.L. (General Roca), DIVISION AGROPECUARIA S.A. (Noetinger), ENRIQUE BERDINI Y CIA. SRL (Inriville), ECHANIZ HERMANOS S.A. and MUGICA Y CIA. S.A. (Camilo Aldao)[^10]
- **Surrounding area**: Corral de Bustos, Leones, Las Varillas, Guatimozín, James Craik, Ucacha, La Carlota, and others have multiple members[^10]

Additionally, the **Cooperativa Agropecuaria General Paz de Marcos Juárez** is a major presence with six acopio plants totaling 230,000 tonnes of capacity, serving ~600 active producing firms.[^64]

The **Bolsa y Cámara de Cereales de Córdoba** serves this entire region with price information, laboratory services, arbitration, and training programs. Their training events—especially the acopio management courses held in Córdoba city—draw attendees from across this corridor.[^21][^65][^20]

### Other Key Regional Clusters

- **Northern Buenos Aires (Pergamino zone)**: Base of the Sociedad de Cerealistas del Norte, historically one of the founding clusters of the Federación.[^11]
- **Southeastern Buenos Aires (Necochea-Quequén, Tres Arroyos, Bahía Blanca)**: Three separate centros de acopiadores with deep institutional history. The Centro de Acopiadores Zona Puerto Quequén covers 9 districts with 60+ companies, with strong ties to the export port.[^66][^12]
- **Rosario hinterland (C-S Santa Fe, E Córdoba)**: The Sociedad Gremial de Rosario's ~120 socios cover this zone. In 2011, they created the *Cooperativa Agropecuaria de Acopiadores Federados*, combining 131 socios with 250 plants and 3.5M tonnes of installed capacity.[^67][^8]
- **La Pampa**: The Centro in General Pico covers a fast-growing agricultural frontier.[^13]
- **Entre Ríos**: The Asociación de Acopiadores has its own institutional structure based in Paraná.[^3]
- **Chaco / NOA**: Emerging but smaller; the Centro de Acopiadores del Chaco is based in Las Breñas.[^4]

### Local Networks and Meetups

Formal "meetups" for acopiadores are rare outside institutional channels. The Federación conducts **periodic working-commission meetings** that bring together representatives from all regional centros. The Sociedad Gremial de Rosario holds zonal meetings and periodic visits to member plants. The BCCBA regularly convenes its members—including the Sociedad de Acopiadores de Córdoba—for training and institutional events. Informal networking happens extensively at *A Todo Trigo*, ExpoAgro, AgroActiva, and within Bolsa de Cereales social events.[^65][^26][^21]

## Strategic Recommendations: Best Channels to Reach Acopiadores

### Tier 1 — Highest Impact, Most Targeted

1. **Federación de Acopiadores de Cereales** — Direct institutional relationship. Gate opens to 1,000+ companies.
2. **A Todo Trigo congress (May 2026, Mar del Plata)** — Sponsorship or exhibition puts you face-to-face with the core audience.
3. **Sociedad de Acopiadores de Granos de Córdoba** — For the Villa María corridor specifically. Their circular distribution and events reach your immediate geography.
4. **Grano SAC / Expo Poscosecha (Rosario, November)** — For any product/service related to storage operations.

### Tier 2 — Strong Reach, Broader Audience

5. **BCCBA training programs** — Sponsoring or co-organizing *Manejo Administrativo del Acopio* courses.
6. **Sociedad Gremial de Acopiadores de Rosario** — 120 socios in the richest grain zone.
7. **APOSGRAN** — For plant-level technical engagement.
8. **ExpoAgro** and **AgroActiva** — Brand visibility with the broader ag sector including acopiadores.

### Tier 3 — Media and Content

9. **Infocampo, Agrofy News, TodoAgro** — Digital advertising and sponsored content.
10. **Cámaras Arbitrales courses** — Co-sponsorship or integration with BCR/BCBA training programs.
11. **Institutional circulars of regional Sociedades** — Advertising in member communications.
