# **The Argentine Grain Weighing Ticket (Romaneo): Structural, Legal, and Operational Framework within the Acopio System**

The commercialization of agricultural commodities in the Argentine Republic operates within a highly sophisticated, rigidly controlled logistical, commercial, and regulatory environment. At the absolute epicenter of this physical-to-digital supply chain lies the weighing and receiving ticket, almost universally referred to within the industry as the *romaneo*. For the Argentine *acopiador* (grain elevator operator or primary collector), the execution, documentation, and systemic reporting of the romaneo is not merely a routine operational necessity; it constitutes a strict fiscal and legal obligation that definitively dictates the tradable volume, commercial quality, and underlying financial value of the producer's harvest.

Given that an estimated seventy percent of agricultural land in Argentina is operated under lease agreements or third-party cultivation contracts, the precise tracking of grain ownership, origin, and quality is fundamental to the structural integrity of the nation's agrarian economy.1 The physical transfer of grain from the harvester in the field to the receiving hopper at the elevator triggers a cascade of fiscal and commercial events. The romaneo serves as the absolute ground-truth document that captures the volumetric reality and the qualitative condition of the physical asset. In doing so, it closes the logistical loop opened by the transit documentation and officially initiates the financial settlement process. The precise methodologies employed to capture this data, the mathematical formulas used to adjust the raw weights, and the systemic integrations required to report these findings to the state form a complex web of compliance that defines modern Argentine agribusiness.

## **Nomenclature and Typological Distinctions**

Within the Argentine agricultural sector, the document generated at the exact point of grain reception goes by several interchangeable colloquial and formal names. While often used synonymously by producers, truck drivers, and plant operators, each term carries slight operational, historical, and legal distinctions that require clarification to understand the document's multifaceted role.

| Terminology | Operational and Legal Distinction |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Romaneo / Boleta de Romaneo** | This is the most prevalent and universally recognized standard industry term. The word *romaneo* historically refers to the physical act of weighing bulk goods on a steelyard balance (una romana). However, in the context of modern agribusiness, the term encompasses the complete, integrated receipt document containing both the physical scale weights and the highly detailed laboratory quality parameters.3 It is the holistic record of the receiving event. |
| **Boleta de Recepción** | This nomenclature is frequently utilized within internal operational manuals, standard operating procedures (SOPs), and quality management systems. It highlights the document's primary function as formal proof of receipt and custody transfer by the *acopio* facility, focusing on the logistical handover rather than just the metrological aspects.4 |
| **Tique de Balanza** | Strictly speaking, the *tique de balanza* (weighing ticket) is the immediate, often raw printed output directly from the electronic scale indicators. Historical regulatory frameworks often refer to this specific printout, but the raw ticket itself only forms the baseline metrological foundation of the final, comprehensive romaneo, lacking the vital laboratory analysis.5 |
| **Certificado de Calidad** | This term is primarily deployed when the document is explicitly formatted to present the laboratory analysis to external arbitration boards, such as the *Cámara Arbitral de Cereales*, or to commercial partners to resolve disputes over grain condition. |
| **Tarjeta de Descarga** | An older, highly operational term often referring to the physical card that accompanied a truck through the yard, tracking its passage from the scale, to the sampling probe, to the unloading pit, and back to the tare scale. This term has largely been rendered obsolete by integrated digital ERP systems. |

It is of absolute paramount importance to distinguish the *romaneo* from the *Liquidación Primaria de Granos* (LPG). A common misconception among external observers is conflating the physical receipt of goods with the financial invoice. The romaneo is the operational, metrological, and physical receipt generated at the exact moment the truck unloads its cargo at the facility.3 Conversely, the LPG is the legally binding electronic fiscal invoice, strictly regulated by the *Agencia de Recaudación y Control Aduanero* (ARCA, the state revenue service formerly known as AFIP) under General Resolution 3419/2012. The LPG utilizes the finalized data established by the romaneo—specifically the adjusted net weight and quality bonuses or penalties—to execute the financial settlement, calculate the respective value-added tax (IVA), and apply mandatory federal and provincial tax withholdings.6 The romaneo is the physical truth; the LPG is the fiscal consequence.

## **The Relational Mechanics: Romaneo, CTG, and the Carta de Porte Electrónica**

To comprehend the structure of the romaneo, one must first understand the strict sequence of events that permits its creation. The generation of a romaneo is not an isolated event; it is the culmination of a highly regulated logistical transit process governed by ARCA. The relationship between the romaneo, the Carta de Porte Electrónica (CPE), and the Código de Trazabilidad de Granos (CTG) is rigidly enforced as a one-to-one-to-one correlation per physical truckload.

The Carta de Porte Electrónica is the mandatory digital document that authorizes the physical transit of bulk grain across Argentine territory via automotor (truck) or railway.8 When a producer wishes to move grain from a silobag in the field to an *acopio*, they must access the ARCA portal and request a CPE. During this request, the producer declares the origin, the destination plant, the specific grain type, and, crucially, an *estimated* weight of the cargo, as precise field scales are rarely available.9 Upon successful generation, ARCA issues the CPE along with an embedded Código de Trazabilidad de Granos (CTG), which serves as the active, verifiable authorization code for that specific journey.8

Therefore, the relational dynamic dictates that there is exactly one romaneo per Carta de Porte Electrónica, per CTG, per truck. It is categorically impossible under current ARCA regulations to issue a single romaneo covering multiple trucks, nor can a single truck generate multiple primary romaneos for a single unified cargo hold.8 The workflow progresses through highly specific systemic gateways:

First, the truck arrives at the *acopio* facility. The security or scale operator accesses the ARCA web service—or utilizes the SMS validation system via the shortcode 2347—to verify that the CPE is in an "Activa" (Active) state and has not exceeded its strict five-day validity period.8 Upon verification, the facility executes a systemic *Confirmación de Arribo* (Confirmation of Arrival), which alerts the federal tax authority that the truck has reached its declared destination.8

Following the physical weighing, sampling, and unloading processes—which culminate in the generation of the internal romaneo—the destination *acopio* must execute the final and most critical regulatory step: the *Confirmación Definitiva* (Definitive Confirmation).8 This systemic action is the exact intersection where the operational reality of the romaneo overrides the logistical estimation of the CPE. ARCA protocols explicitly dictate that during the *Confirmación Definitiva*, the destination facility is required to modify the "kilograms received" field.9 The operator inputs the exact *Peso Neto* (Net Weight) derived from the freshly generated romaneo, thereby overwriting the producer's initial estimated field weight. This action legally closes the CPE, extinguishes the CTG, and officially transfers the physical responsibility and custody of the exact grain volume to the *acopiador*.7 Once this confirmation is finalized, the CPE cannot be annulled, ensuring absolute integrity in the state's tracking of agricultural commodities.7

## **Comprehensive Structural Architecture of the Romaneo**

The informational structure of a standard Argentine romaneo is exhaustively detailed, reflecting the dual demands of the commercial market—arbitrated by entities like the *Cámara Arbitral de Cereales* of the Bolsa de Comercio de Rosario (BCR)—and the fiscal surveillance mechanisms of ARCA. While the graphical user interfaces of different software platforms vary, the underlying data schema is universally standardized across the nation to facilitate algorithmic reporting, taxation, and commercial equity.

The myriad fields populating a complete and legally compliant romaneo are categorized into logistical identification, volumetric assessment, qualitative metrics, and applied physical and commercial deductions.

### **1\. Header, Identification, and Traceability Data**

The upper section of the romaneo serves to establish the legal entities involved in the transaction, define the temporal reality of the event, and permanently link the physical grain to its authorizing transit documentation. This creates an unbroken chain of custody from the point of harvest to the point of storage.

| Identification Field | Comprehensive Description and Regulatory Function |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Document Number and Date** | A unique, sequential alphanumeric control number generated automatically by the *acopio's* Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) system (e.g., Albor Campo, Physis, Finnegans GO Agro). This is accompanied by the exact calendar date and the precise timestamps of the truck's entry onto the gross weighbridge and its exit from the tare weighbridge.12 |
| **Producer Data (Remitente Comercial)** | The legal identification of the entity delivering the grain. This mandates the inclusion of the legal registered name (Razón Social), the unique Fiscal Identification Code (CUIT), and the registered fiscal domicile.15 In complex agricultural operations involving sharecropping, pools de siembra, or leased land (which constitute 70% of Argentine production), this ensures the correct entity is credited for the physical stock.1 |
| **Acopio Data (Destinatario / CUIT Destino)** | The legal identification of the receiving elevator facility. Crucially, this must include the specific plant authorization number (Número de planta de descarga) registered within the *Sistema de Información Simplificado Agrícola* (SISA), which replaced the older RUCA registry requirement.17 |
| **CPE and CTG Reference Numbers** | The unique tracking number of the Carta de Porte Electrónica and its embedded Código de Trazabilidad de Granos.8 The inclusion of these numbers on the printed or digital romaneo is an absolute legal mandate to prove the grain arrived under lawful authorization. |
| **Grain Type and Campaign Year** | The specific agricultural commodity being delivered, which must be identified by standardized national AFIP/ARCA codes to ensure database interoperability (e.g., Code 19 for Corn, Code 23 for Soybeans, Code 15 for Bread Wheat, Code 22 for Sorghum).3 This is accompanied by the agricultural campaign year (e.g., 2024/2025) to properly segregate historical stocks from fresh harvests. |
| **Truck and Transport Data** | Comprehensive identification of the logistical vector. This includes the distinct license plates (patentes) of the truck cab (chasis) and the trailing unit (acoplado), the full name and national identity document (DNI) of the driver, and the CUIT of the transport company (Transportista).8 This data is cross-referenced against national transport registries to ensure compliance with maximum weight limits per axle.18 |

### **2\. Volumetric and Scale Data**

The metrological core of the romaneo captures the raw physical mass of the delivery. The strict procedures surrounding this data dictate a sequential double-weighing mechanism on calibrated, industrial weighbridges to eliminate manual estimation or fraud.

The process begins when the fully loaded truck drives onto the primary scale. The system captures the **Peso Bruto (Gross Weight)**, which represents the combined absolute mass of the vehicle, its fuel, the driver, and the agricultural cargo.3 Following this initial measurement, the truck proceeds into the facility's logistical circuit, stops at the pneumatic sampling station, and eventually positions itself over the *tolva de recepción* (receiving hopper) to discharge the grain via gravity or hydraulic lifts.19

Once entirely empty, the truck loops back to the scale infrastructure—often a dedicated secondary scale to prevent logistical bottlenecks—to capture the **Tara (Tare Weight)**.19 The ERP software instantly calculates the mathematical difference between the Peso Bruto and the Tara, yielding the raw **Peso Neto (Net Weight)**.3 This Peso Neto represents the absolute metrological mass of the physical material delivered, before any qualitative analysis reveals how much of that mass is actually viable commercial grain versus water, dirt, or foreign matter.

### **3\. Qualitative Analysis Metrics**

Immediately following the capture of the gross weight, a representative sample of the cargo is systematically extracted from the truck bed using automated pneumatic probes (caladores pneumáticos) that penetrate to the bottom of the load to ensure a homogenous representation. This sample is transported to the facility's internal laboratory, where it is subjected to rigorous testing.

The grain is evaluated against the strict national commercial standards dictated by the Secretariat of Agriculture and aggressively arbitrated by entities such as the *Cámara Arbitral de Cereales* (CAC) of the Bolsa de Comercio de Rosario (BCR).20 The results of these tests are permanently inscribed onto the romaneo and fundamentally dictate whether the grain receives the base market quotation, earns a commercial premium (bonificación), or suffers severe volumetric shrinkages and monetary penalties (rebajas).

| Quality Parameter | Metrological Definition, Commercial Impact, and BCR Standard Integration |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Humedad (%)** | The moisture content is the most critical parameter on the romaneo. Standard base tolerances are highly specific: 14.5% for Corn, 14.0% for Wheat and Sorghum, and 13.5% for Soybeans.20 Excess moisture triggers severe volumetric shrinkage deductions (merma de secado) and mandatory monetary drying tariffs to bring the grain down to a safe storage equilibrium.20 |
| **Peso Hectolítrico (kg/hl)** | The specific test weight of the grain, representing its density. This is crucial for Wheat and Corn. Higher test weights indicate denser, healthier, and higher-yielding grain for milling. For example, BCR Norma XX dictates that Wheat Grade 1 requires a minimum of 79 kg/hl (yielding a 1.5% commercial bonus), while Grade 3 permits 73 kg/hl (resulting in a 1% penalty).20 |
| **Zarandeo / Cuerpos Extraños (%)** | The percentage of foreign matter—such as dirt, dust, empty pods, stems, stones, and non-toxic weed seeds—separated using standardized mechanical laboratory sieves.23 Material exceeding the base tolerance results in a direct volumetric reduction known as *merma de zarandeo*.3 |
| **Granos Dañados (%)** | The proportion of kernels exhibiting physical, fungal, heat, or weather-induced damage (e.g., fermented, sprouted, heat-damaged, or rotten grains).23 High percentages significantly downgrade the overall lot quality, potentially classifying the entire truckload as *Fuera de Estándar* (Off-Standard), which allows the buyer to apply severe discretionary discounts or reject the load entirely.20 |
| **Granos Quebrados (%)** | Broken, chipped, or fragmented grains.23 These are penalized because broken kernels are highly susceptible to fungal infections and rapid pest infestation during long-term silo storage, and they reduce the extraction yield in milling operations.20 |
| **Granos Picados (%)** | The percentage of grains visibly damaged by insect boring, tunneling, or feeding. This is closely monitored and recorded on the romaneo to prevent the introduction of active insect populations into clean silo infrastructures.23 |
| **Proteína (%)** | Measured specifically for Bread Wheat, the standard base protein level is established at 11%. Values tested above 11% earn a progressive 2% commercial bonus, incentivizing high-quality cultivation. Conversely, values below 11% suffer aggressive penalties: a 2% penalty for levels between 10-11%, escalating to a 4% penalty for levels below 9%.20 |
| **Chamico (Seeds/100g)** | The presence of the toxic weed seed *Datura ferox* (Chamico) is heavily penalized due to its severe health risks in animal and human consumption chains. For commodities like Sorghum, the presence of 5-10 seeds triggers a 1.5% deduction, escalating dramatically to a 5% penalty if 100-200 seeds are detected per kilogram.20 |
| **Olores Objetables / Amohosados** | A qualitative olfactory and visual assessment of commercially objectionable odors (e.g., sour, musty, petroleum) or visible mold. The laboratory marks this as 'Conforme' (Acceptable) or applies flat, non-negotiable percentage discounts of 0.5%, 1%, 1.5%, or 2% based on severity.23 |

### **4\. Deductions (Mermas) and Final Reconciled Weight**

The most critical functional and mathematical purpose of the romaneo is the algorithmic conversion of the raw *Peso Neto* into the **Peso Neto Conforme** (Final Adjusted Weight). The raw net weight is a physical truth, but it is not a commercial truth. To achieve commercial equity, the raw weight must be subjected to a series of physical and commercial deductions known within the Argentine market as *mermas*. The application of these mermas is hardcoded into the ERP systems based on the laboratory results and BCR regulations.

**Merma de Secado (Drying Shrinkage):** If the *Humedad* percentage determined by the laboratory exceeds the base commercial standard for that specific grain, the facility must artificially dry the grain to prevent spoilage. **Merma de Zarandeo (Cleaning Shrinkage):** This deduction accounts for the physical removal of foreign matter, dirt, and unmarketable broken grains that exceed the allowed commercial tolerances. The laboratory determines the percentage of excess waste, and this percentage is translated into physical kilograms deducted from the raw net weight, accompanied by a monetary cleaning tariff (Tarifa Zaranda).3

**Merma Volátil (Volatile Shrinkage) / Merma de Recibo:** Unlike moisture or foreign matter, which are directly measured, volatile shrinkage is a standardized, non-negotiable deduction applied across the board to account for invisible, unavoidable physical losses during the violent mechanical unloading process. This covers the mass lost to suspended dust plumes, pulverized vegetative matter, and microscopic grain abrasion that blows away in the wind or is extracted by the facility's aspiration systems. The standard recognized deduction is generally 0.3% of the total weight for cereals (wheat, corn) and 0.5% for oilseeds (soybeans, sunflower).22

**Merma por Manipuleo (Handling Shrinkage):** A secondary standard deduction applied to account for the physical degradation, breakage, and loss of grain as it is violently moved through the facility's vast network of bucket elevators, conveyor belts, mechanical sieves, and dryers. Standard agreed-upon rates are 0.10% for Wheat, 0.20% for Sunflower, and 0.25% for Corn and Soybeans.22

After all of these percentage-based *mermas* are mathematically calculated and converted into specific kilograms, they are aggregated and subtracted from the raw Peso Neto. The resulting mathematical figure is the **Peso Neto Conforme** (Final Adjusted Commercial Weight).22

The *Peso Neto Conforme* represents the ultimate commercial climax of the romaneo. This specific figure becomes the definitive, legally recognized volume of the agricultural asset. It is this exact quantity of kilograms that is officially credited to the producer's *Cuenta Corriente Granaria* (grain current account) held with the *acopio*, and it is the sole metric utilized subsequently to calculate the total financial payout in the electronic Liquidación Primaria de Granos.14

### **5\. Final Operational and Authorization Data**

The final section of the romaneo document solidifies the internal logistics and provides legal authorization for the transaction.

* **Silo/Celda Assignment:** An internal logistical routing code indicating the exact physical destination of the unloaded grain. It specifies whether the grain was deposited directly into a vertical concrete silo, a horizontal storage cell (celda), or if its high moisture content required it to be routed immediately to the wet holding bins prior to entering the dryer (secadora).19 This is essential for internal traceability and quality segregation.  
* **Signatures and Digital Confirmations:** Historically, the printed romaneo required the wet physical signatures of the facility's certified scale operator (*recibidor de granos*) and the truck driver, who acts as the legally authorized proxy for the producer and the transport company.5 In the contemporary digitized landscape, while printed tickets are still signed and archived for immediate physical proof, the definitive authorizations are executed via secure digital confirmations, cryptographic timestamps, and user-permission logging within the *acopio's* centralized ERP environment.

## **Format Standardization and AgTech Software Integration**

A common inquiry regarding the Argentine system is whether the federal government mandates a strict, unalterable printed template for the romaneo. The answer is nuanced: there is no single, state-mandated graphical or visual format for the printed physical ticket. Each *acopiador* or cooperative is completely free to design the visual layout, branding, and printing format of their romaneos according to their operational preferences.

However, the underlying data schema, the naming conventions of the variables, and the specific fields required are absolutely and rigidly standardized by necessity. This requirement for profound standardization stems from the reality that the data captured on the romaneo cannot exist in a vacuum; it must immediately, seamlessly, and algorithmically feed into highly regulated national tax databases via complex Web Services APIs (Application Programming Interfaces).11

The Argentine AgTech sector has evolved sophisticated Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems to manage this immense data flow. Platforms such as Albor Campo, Physis Gestión Acopio, Finnegans GO Agro, and Algoritmo utilize the standardized data parameters of the romaneo to generate the physical tickets for the truck drivers while simultaneously executing automated, invisible background compliance tasks.12

For instance, systems like Finnegans incorporate advanced features such as "Bot de granos" and external document integration hubs named "Deals".28 Because the agricultural supply chain involves multiple disparate actors sending romaneos and quality certificates back and forth, these automated bots ingest digital romaneos from third-party elevators, use optical character recognition and data mapping to normalize the fields, and sync the adjusted weights and quality penalties directly into the producer's core accounting ledger without requiring manual data entry.28

Similarly, platforms like Physis and Albor explicitly market their automated connectivity with ARCA.13 When the scale operator finalizes the romaneo on their terminal, the software compiles the data into a highly specific XML (eXtensible Markup Language) format wrapped in a SOAP (Simple Object Access Protocol) envelope (e.g., \<S:Envelope\>, ns2:TipoResp).26 It transmits this payload directly to ARCA's servers to validate the Carta de Porte, execute the definitive confirmation, and update the national stock registries in real-time.13 Consequently, while a romaneo printed by a local cooperative in the province of Córdoba may look visually entirely distinct from one issued by a multinational port exporter in Rosario, the structural content, the mathematical application of the *mermas*, and the rigid quality nomenclatures remain absolutely identical to satisfy the relentless demands of the state's algorithms.

## **Regulatory Reporting and the State Apparatus**

The romaneo is unequivocally not a mere internal operational record relegated to a filing cabinet; its digital manifestation is a heavily scrutinized regulatory instrument of the Argentine state. The data generated on the scale and in the laboratory must be reported to the federal tax authority as part of a continuous, unbreakable fiscal compliance loop aimed at combating tax evasion and controlling the nation's primary source of foreign currency reserves.

Under the framework of General Resolution 3593/2014, which was subsequently updated, expanded, and substituted by RG 5689/2025, the Argentine state mandates a strict "Régimen de Registración Sistémica de Movimientos y Existencias de Granos" (Systemic Registration Regime of Grain Movements and Stocks).30 Any operator registered within the SISA framework who maintains physical storage plants is legally obligated to systemically report every single physical intake, transfer, and output of grain.18

The generation of the romaneo acts as the triggering transaction for this federal requirement. The regulations explicitly dictate that any movement or storage of grain must be preceded by a formal systemic registration that identifies the exact plant, the document number (e.g., the romaneo number), the precise grain type, the kilograms involved, and the harvest campaign.7 When the *acopiador* finalizes the romaneo, the ERP system transmits this exact data payload to ARCA's portal, instantly updating the facility's centralized physical stock ledger.30

ARCA's algorithms continuously cross-reference this incoming romaneo data against the plant's declared physical storage capacity and the surrounding region's historical agricultural yields, flagging anomalies indicative of black-market grain entering the formal system.6 The consequences for failing to report romaneos accurately or timely are severe. Should ARCA detect inconsistencies or a failure to properly report the physical receipts, the agency possesses the immediate regulatory authority to block the *acopio* from utilizing the system. This includes halting their ability to request new Cartas de Porte Electrónicas, suspending the assignment of CTGs, and freezing their ability to issue the Liquidación Primaria de Granos, effectively paralyzing the facility's entire commercial operation until the data is rectified.7

## **Legal Bindingness and Commercial Dispute Resolution**

While the electronic Liquidación Primaria de Granos (LPG) stands as the sole legally recognized invoice for national tax, VAT, and fiscal withholding purposes 6, the romaneo holds immense legal gravity in its own right. It is a legally binding primary commercial voucher (*comprobante de respaldo*) that definitively establishes the physical reality and material condition of the transaction.5

The legal weight of the romaneo becomes paramount in scenarios involving high-stakes commercial disputes regarding grain quality and financial valuation. In the Argentine commercial system, both the delivering party (the producer or their broker) and the receiving party (the *acopio* or port exporter) must be in mutual agreement regarding the quality metrics printed on the romaneo. If a producer receives a romaneo and vehemently disputes the moisture content, the foreign matter percentage, or the insect damage penalties assigned by the *acopio's* internal laboratory, they possess the fundamental legal right to reject the findings and demand an independent, binding arbitration.21

This arbitration process is governed by specialized institutions such as the *Cámara Arbitral de Cereales* (CAC) of the Bolsa de Comercio de Rosario.21 The CAC operates under a widely agreed-upon set of industry bylaws known as the "Reglas y Usos del Comercio de Granos" (Rules and Customs of the Grain Trade), which explicitly dictate how contractual relations are managed and how technical, metrological discrepancies are resolved swiftly to maintain supply chain fluidity.33

In the event of a dispute triggered by a contested romaneo, a sealed "muestra lacrada" (lacquered/sealed physical grain sample)—which must be systematically extracted and preserved in a tamper-evident container at the exact moment the romaneo is issued—is submitted to the BCR's independent, highly calibrated laboratories for blind testing.21 The CAC acts as the ultimate technical arbiter, conducting the necessary tests (such as utilizing advanced NIR technology for rapid moisture determination) and issuing a formal resolution with the full legal effect of an arbitral award (*laudo arbitral*).21

The findings contained within this arbitral award legally supersede the original laboratory results printed on the disputed romaneo. Consequently, the *acopiador* is legally forced to recalculate the applicable *mermas* and reissue a corrected *Peso Neto Conforme* based entirely on the arbitrator's final parameters, which then dictates the final financial settlement.21

Furthermore, beyond immediate commercial disputes, broader historical and environmental legislation mandates the long-term preservation of these commercial grain records. While modern records are entirely digitized within cloud-based ERPs, the granular data encapsulated within the romaneo is subject to retrospective auditing by provincial and federal regulatory bodies. These audits serve to verify compliance with a matrix of environmental, phytosanitary, and fiscal laws—such as verifying that a shipment containing highly toxic weed seeds like *Chamico* was properly identified, documented, penalized, and managed, or ensuring that plant capacities adhere to provincial environmental protection regimes (such as Law 9855 in Córdoba).20

The romaneo, therefore, transcends its identity as a mere physical receipt. It stands as the indisputable operational truth, the bedrock of fiscal compliance, and the foundational legal instrument that enables the vast, complex machinery of the Argentine grain trade to function with transparency, commercial equity, and absolute regulatory adherence.
