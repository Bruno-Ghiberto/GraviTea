# Producer Current Accounts at Argentine Grain Storage Operators: Complete ERP Design Reference

## Executive Summary

The *cuenta corriente del productor* at an Argentine acopiador is a **dual-ledger system** that simultaneously tracks **physical grain balances in kilograms** and **monetary balances in pesos**. The grain account records deposits (entries), withdrawals (deliveries/sales), and deductions (mermas, services charged in kind), while the monetary account captures the financial flows generated when grain is sold or when cash transactions occur. Understanding this dual nature is fundamental to ERP design: grain enters the system valued in physical units at zero monetary value (or "a fijar"), and monetary value is crystallized only at the moment of sale or price-fixing.[^1][^2][^3]

This report covers every transaction type flowing through these accounts, the supporting official documents, regulatory requirements, valuation mechanics, reporting obligations, delivery orders, and barter (canje) operations.

***

## Overview of the Grain Commercial Circuit

The *etapa primaria* (primary stage) of grain commercialization in Argentina establishes the relationship between **producers** and **acopiadores** (storage operators) or cooperatives. The acopiador acts as a **depositario** (bailee) of grain — legally, grain deposited remains the property of the producer until sold. The acopiador provides storage, conditioning, conservation, and commercialization services, and may also supply agricultural inputs and financing.[^2][^1]

Upon delivery, grain loses its physical identity (it is mixed in silos), but the producer retains ownership of an equivalent quantity and quality. This is captured through the electronic documentation system mandated by AFIP (Argentina's tax authority), specifically the **Certificación Electrónica de Granos (CEG)** for deposits, retiros, and transfers, and the **Liquidación Primaria de Granos (LPG)** for sales.[^4][^5][^1]

***

## Credit Entries (A Favor del Productor)

### Grain Deposits (Entregas de Mercadería)

When grain arrives from the field, the flow is:

1. **Carta de Porte Electrónica (CPE)**: Issued before transport, containing shipper, destination, grain type, and gross kilos. Must include the CTG (Código de Trazabilidad de Granos) obtained via AFIP.[^1]
2. **Ticket Balanza / Romaneo**: Generated at reception, recording gross weight, humidity percentage, impurities, and other quality parameters.[^2][^1]
3. **Certificación Electrónica de Granos (CEG)**: The official electronic certificate consolidating one or more romaneos. It must be issued and closed within **30 calendar days** from the date of the first carta de porte. It records net kilos after all mermas and deductions, service tariffs, and quality data.[^1]

**At what value is grain deposited?** Grain is deposited at **zero monetary value** — only physical units (net kilograms) are credited to the producer's kilos account. No monetary amount is assigned until a sale occurs (LPG issuance) or a price is "fixed" on grain held *a fijar*. The CEG establishes the tariff schedule but does not bill storage (since duration is unknown).[^3][^2][^1]

### Sale Proceeds (Producto de Ventas)

When the producer sells grain, the acopiador issues an **LPG (Liquidación Primaria de Granos)** electronically via AFIP's web service. The net amount payable to the producer — after deducting services, commissions, freight, and taxes — is credited to the monetary account. A single CEG may generate **multiple LPGs** (partial sales).[^6][^4][^2][^1]

### Cash Advances (Anticipos)

The acopiador may advance cash to the producer against future grain deliveries or pending sales. These are recorded as monetary credits in the current account. They require a supporting invoice and are later offset against LPG proceeds. Advances typically require that the grain backing the advance has already been delivered and that all documentation is in order.[^7][^8]

### Price Adjustments and Quality Bonuses

- **Bonificaciones por calidad**: Price increases for grade 1 grain (1% bonus for wheat/corn), high protein content in wheat (2% per point above 11% base), or special quality attributes like high oleic in sunflower.[^2][^1]
- **Ajustes de precio**: When a producer who delivered grain "a fijar" (without a fixed price) subsequently fixes the price, a new LPG is issued at the fixed price, generating a credit entry.[^2]

### Other Credit Items

- **Credit notes** for service adjustments or overcharges
- **Interest earned** on overdue balances owed by the acopiador (uncommon but possible)
- **Fuera de precio** (out-of-price bonus): An additional per-ton payment agreed in the purchase contract, settled in the final liquidation[^3]

## Debit Entries (A Favor del Acopiador)

### Paritaria (General Handling Fee)

This charge covers the cost of using the acopiador's mechanical installations — **norias** (bucket elevators), **tolvas de recibo** (receiving hoppers), **extractors**, etc.. It is charged only when grain physically enters the silo facility; if grain goes directly to final destination (port, factory), paritaria is typically not charged.[^1]

- **Calculation**: Tariff ($/ton) × gross kilos delivered[^1]
- **Billing moment**: In the CEG or via a separate service invoice[^3]

### Secado (Drying)

Charged when grain moisture exceeds the base standard (14% for wheat and corn, 13% for soy, 11% for sunflower). Drying is the highest-cost conditioning service.[^1]

- **Calculation methods** (from ERP documentation):[^3]
  - **Basic drying**: A tariff for a first humidity range (e.g., 14.6%–17% for corn), plus a per-point-excess surcharge for higher moisture
  - **Decimal point drying**: Charges fractional humidity (e.g., 17.2% charges only two tenths, not rounded up)
  - **Range-based drying**: Different tariffs per defined humidity brackets
- **Merma por secado (drying shrinkage)**: Calculated with the formula: \(%M = \frac{H_i - H_f}{100 - H_f} \times 100\) where \(H_i\) is initial humidity and \(H_f\) is the commercial base humidity. Official tables published by the Cámara Arbitral de Cereales de Rosario provide exact percentages for each tenth of a percent.[^9][^10][^11]

### Zarandeo (Cleaning/Screening)

Applied when impurities, foreign matter, or broken grains exceed commercial standards.[^2][^1]

- **Calculation**: The system checks if the receipt has a zarandeo percentage assigned; if so, a fixed tariff per ton is charged[^3]
- **Merma por zarandeo**: The weight of material removed is deducted from the gross kilos[^9]
- **Merma por manipuleo (handling shrinkage)**: 0.10% for wheat, 0.25% for corn and soy, 0.20% for sunflower[^1]

### Almacenaje (Storage)

Storage is billed **after the fact**, since the duration of storage is unknown at the time of deposit.[^1]

- **Typical structure**: A **free period** of 60–90 days post-harvest (negotiable), then a **monthly percentage** applied to the grain's market value. A reference rate of ~0.8% of grain value per month was observed in BCR case studies.[^3][^1]
- **Calculation in ERP**: The system computes the number of days from reception to sale/withdrawal, applies the monthly rate, and generates the charge in the LPG or a separate service invoice.[^3]

### Fumigación (Fumigation)

Charged when insect infestation requires treatment.[^1]

- **Calculation**: The system flags receipts marked as "fumigated" and applies the fumigation tariff[^3]
- **Billing**: In the CEG or separately

### Comisión del Acopiador (Commission)

This is the acopiador's intermediation fee on grain sales. It is **freely negotiable** between parties.

- **Typical range**: **1.5% to 2.5%** on the sale value, though some operations reach 4%[^2][^1]
- **Calculation basis**: Applied as a percentage of the gross sale amount (Precio de Referencia × kilos). In some ERP setups, it is calculated as *Porcentaje Bruto* — a percentage of the total including IVA.[^3]
- **Cooperatives** may also charge a *capitalización* (mandatory share subscription) in addition to or instead of a commission[^1]

### Flete (Freight)

Two types appear on the producer's account:

| Type | Description | Billed on |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| **Flete corto** (short haul) | Field to acopio plant | Gross kilos transported; in CEG[^1] |
| **Flete largo** (long haul) | Acopio to port/factory/buyer | Net kilos sold; in LPG[^1] |

Tariffs are freely negotiated; reference rates are published by CATAC or FADEEAC.[^12][^1]

### Merma Volátil (Volatile Shrinkage)

A fixed percentage deducted for natural weight loss (dust, leaves, minor material loss). Applied **last**, after all other mermas.[^9]

| Grain Category | Rate |
|----------------|------|
| Cereals (wheat, corn, barley, oats, rye) | 0.30%[^9] |
| Oilseeds (soy, sunflower, flax) and sorghum | 0.50%[^9] |

### Withholding Taxes (Retenciones)

Acopiadores act as **withholding agents** for multiple taxes, all deducted from the producer's sale proceeds in the LPG:[^13][^4]

| Tax | Rate | Notes |
|-----|------|-------|
| **IVA (VAT)** | Grain sales taxed at 10.5%. Retention regime depends on SISA status: inscribed producers get partial reintegro (systematic refund)[^13] | Complex regime per RG 4310/2018; retention appears in LPG and must generate F.2005 certificate via SIRE[^4] |
| **Ganancias (Income Tax)** | 2% if registered in SISA; 8% without registration; up to 28% if unregistered in tax[^1] | Per RG 2118/06 and successors |
| **Ingresos Brutos (Gross Revenue Tax)** | Provincial; varies by jurisdiction | Applied as perception or retention depending on province |
| **Sellado (Stamp Tax)** | Provincial tax on registered contracts | Varies; some provinces apply ~0.5–1%[^1] |

### Other Charges

- **Cámara (Arbitration Chamber lab fees)**: Charges from the Cámara Arbitral de Cereales for quality analysis[^1]
- **Gastos de análisis (analysis expenses)**: Including *recupero de honorarios* — 50% of lab costs shared with producer per industry custom[^3]
- **Playa (staging area fees)**: Some acopiadores debit the producer for truck staging charges at port areas[^1]
- **Insurance**: Deposited grain must be insured with coverage appropriate to the operation. The acopiador typically takes out comprehensive insurance (covering fire, weather, theft, etc.) for all grain in storage, and the cost may be passed through to the producer.[^14][^15]

## Account Valuation

### Dual-Ledger Structure

The producer's account is maintained as **two parallel ledgers**:[^3]

| Ledger | Unit | Updated By |
|--------|------|------------|
| **Cuenta corriente en kilos** | Kilograms net | Automatic: grain entries (CEG), exits (sales/LPG, retiros, transfers), service deductions in kind[^3] |
| **Cuenta corriente en dinero** | Pesos (or pesos + USD) | Automatic: LPG proceeds, invoices for services, advances, payments, retentions[^3] |

Some ERP systems (like AgroAcopio) also track a **plant-level kilos account** that represents total physical stock across all producers, useful for reconciliation against physical inventory.[^3]

### When Is Grain Converted to Money?

The conversion occurs at **sale time**, specifically when the LPG is issued. For grain sold *a precio* (at market price), this happens at delivery. For grain sold *a fijar* (price to be determined), the conversion is deferred:[^2]

1. Producer delivers grain → kilos credited to account (no money yet)
2. Producer instructs acopiador to sell (or agrees to a price)
3. LPG is issued → kilos debited from kilos account; net monetary amount credited to pesos account[^2]

### Partial Sales

A single CEG can generate multiple LPGs, representing partial sales at different times and prices during the campaign. Each partial sale:[^1]

- Debits the proportional kilos from the kilos ledger
- Credits the net peso amount (after all deductions) to the monetary ledger
- Services like storage are recalculated proportionally for the period and volume involved

### Price-Fixing Events (Fijaciones)

For grain delivered *a fijar*, the producer can "fix" the price at any time during the agreed period. The price may be referenced to the Cámara Arbitral's **pizarra** (board price) for the relevant date. Each fijación generates its own LPG, partially or fully converting kilos to pesos.[^1]

## Statements and Reports

### Account Statements (Extractos de Cuenta Corriente)

The acopiador provides producers with:

- **Kilos account statement**: Shows opening balance, entries (by CEG), exits (by LPG, retiro, or transfer), mermas, and closing balance — broken down by grain type, campaign (e.g., 2024/25), and plant location[^16][^3]
- **Monetary account statement**: Shows debits (service charges, retentions), credits (LPG proceeds, advances), and running balance in pesos[^3]
- **Pending items reports**: CEGs pending liquidation, canjes pending, partial liquidations pending[^3]

### Frequency

Statements are typically provided:

- **On demand**: At the producer's request
- **At campaign close**: A final reconciliation
- **Monthly or periodic**: Depending on the acopiador's systems and the commercial relationship

### Required Information

According to the FACPCE CECyT Informe N° 34, disclosure should include:[^1]

- Grain type
- Quantity in quintals or metric tons
- Fair value less selling costs (per unit and total)
- A reconciliation showing: opening balance + receipts − sales ± other movements = closing balance

### Official Documents Generated

All documents are electronic and filed via AFIP web services:

| Document | Purpose | Issued By |
|----------|---------|-----------|
| **CEG (Certificación Electrónica de Granos)** | Backs grain deposit | Depositario (acopiador)[^5] |
| **LPG (Liquidación Primaria de Granos)** | Liquidation of sale to/through the acopiador | Buyer or consignee[^4] |
| **Certificación Primaria de Retiro** | Grain withdrawal by producer | Depositario[^5] |
| **Certificación Primaria de Transferencia** | Transfer of kilos between operators | Depositario[^5] |
| **Factura de Servicios** | Billing of conditioning/storage services | Acopiador[^3] |
| **F.2005 (SIRE)** | Tax retention certificate (IVA/Ganancias) | Retention agent via SIRE[^4] |

## Órdenes de Entrega (Delivery Orders)

### Mechanism

A producer may instruct the acopiador to deliver stored grain to a specific buyer (exporter, factory, another acopiador) without a monetary sale flowing through the acopiador's account. The process works as follows:[^5][^17]

1. **Producer instructs delivery**: Specifies buyer, grain type, quantity, and destination.
2. **Acopiador verifies balance**: The system checks the producer's available kilos balance (the retiro cannot exceed the saldo disponible backed by a CEG).[^17]
3. **Certificación Primaria de Retiro de Granos** is issued electronically. The system generates a CTG and Carta de Porte for the physical movement.
4. **Effect on current account**: Kilos are debited from the producer's kilos ledger. If the buyer issues an LPG directly to the producer, the monetary credit appears on the producer's external account (not at the acopiador). If the acopiador sells on behalf of the producer, an LPG flows through the acopiador's monetary account.[^17]

### Special Rules

- Only **one Carta de Porte per retiro** is permitted[^17]
- If the destination is another operator with a registered plant, the receiving operator must issue a new CEG[^17]
- Retiros require a pre-existing CEG; no retiro can be issued without stock backing[^17]

## Canje (Barter) Operations

### Structure

In a canje, the producer receives inputs (seeds, fertilizers, agrochemicals, fuel, machinery, services) from a **canjeador** (which may be the acopiador itself or a third-party input supplier) and pays by delivering grain.[^18][^19]

### Document Flow

The canje generates **two parallel document streams**:[^19]

| Document | Issued By | For |
|----------|-----------|-----|
| **Factura de venta (input sale invoice)** | Canjeador/input supplier | Sale of inputs to producer; typically in USD converted to pesos, or directly in pesos. Includes IVA at 21% (inputs) |
| **LPG (grain sale liquidation)** | Canjeador or broker | Purchase of grain from producer; IVA at 10.5%[^19] |

### Pricing

- **Input price**: Invoiced at the date of delivery/agreement. The invoice amount is expressed as a certain number of kilograms of grain × the grain price on that date.[^19]
- **Grain price**: At the time of actual grain delivery (which may be months later, at harvest), the canjeador issues an LPG at the **current market price**. A **nota de débito** is issued for the IVA on the original input invoice plus any price difference between the initial and final grain valuations.[^19]

### Canje Types

| Type | Description | Tax Treatment |
|------|-------------|---------------|
| **Canje total** | Full payment in grain; no cash exchanged | No IVA or Ganancias retentions (documents compensate). Canjeador charges IVA perception only[^19] |
| **Canje parcial** | Partial grain + partial cash | Retentions apply to the cash portion |

### Recording in the Current Account

In the producer's account at the acopiador:

1. **Debit (kilos)**: Grain delivered for the canje reduces the kilos balance
2. **Credit (pesos)**: LPG amount for the grain sold
3. **Debit (pesos)**: Invoice for inputs received
4. **Offset**: The LPG credit and the input invoice debit are **compensated** in the current account, ideally zeroing out[^19]

If there is a residual difference (due to rounding, price changes, or partial canje), the balance remains in the monetary account as a debit or credit.[^20]

## ERP Design Considerations

### Data Model Essentials

Based on this research, an ERP accounting module for an acopiador should implement:

- **Dual current account per producer**: One in kilos (by grain type, campaign, plant), one in pesos/USD
- **Transaction types**: Structured around the official documents (CEG, LPG, Retiro, Transferencia, Factura de Servicios)
- **Service contract template system**: Per-producer or per-grain service contracts defining tariffs for each service, calculation methods, and billing moments[^3]
- **Merma calculation engine**: Implementing official shrinkage tables (by humidity, cleaning, handling, and volatile loss) with the correct sequential application order[^9]
- **Tax retention engine**: Computing IVA, Ganancias, and IIBB retentions/perceptions per AFIP rules, SISA status, and provincial regulations[^4][^13]
- **Canje module**: Linking purchase invoices (inputs) with LPGs (grain), supporting total/partial compensation and handling the IVA differential (21% vs. 10.5%)[^19]
- **AFIP integration**: Web service interfaces for CEG/LPG/CTG electronic filing and SIRE retention certificates[^5][^4]

### Transaction Type Summary

| Transaction | Kilos Account Effect | Pesos Account Effect | Document |
|-------------|---------------------|---------------------|----------|
| Grain reception | + Net kilos | — | CEG |
| Sale (total or partial) | − Kilos sold | + Net proceeds | LPG |
| Retiro/delivery order | − Kilos withdrawn | — (or + if LPG from external buyer) | Cert. Retiro |
| Transfer to another operator | − Kilos transferred | — | Cert. Transferencia |
| Cash advance | — | + Advance amount | Factura anticipo |
| Paritaria, secado, zarandeo, fumigación | (may deduct merma kilos) | − Service charge | CEG or Factura Servicios |
| Almacenaje | — | − Storage charge | LPG or Factura Servicios |
| Comisión | — | − Commission amount | LPG |
| Tax retentions | — | − Retention amounts | LPG + F.2005 |
| Canje — grain delivery | − Kilos delivered | + LPG amount | LPG |
| Canje — input receipt | — | − Input invoice amount | Factura compra |
| Canje — compensation | — | Net offset | Orden de pago/compensación |
| Price fixing (fijación) | − Kilos fixed | + Fixed price × kilos | LPG |

## Regulatory Framework Summary

The key regulations governing this system include:

- **RG AFIP 3691/2014**: Electronic certification of deposit, retiro, and transfer of grains[^5]
- **RG AFIP 3419/2012**: Liquidación Primaria de Granos (LPG) electronic regime[^4]
- **RG AFIP 3690/2014**: Liquidación Secundaria de Granos[^5]
- **RG AFIP 4310/2018**: IVA retention/perception regime for grain operations, including SISA integration[^13]
- **RG AFIP 2118/2006**: Income tax retention regime for grain operations[^1]
- **RUCA (Registro Único de la Cadena Agroalimentaria)**: Mandatory registration for all grain operators[^21]
- **Código Civil y Comercial, Arts. 1319–1335**: Legal framework for mandato and consignación contracts governing the producer-acopiador relationship[^1]
- **RT 8, 17, 22 (FACPCE)**: Accounting standards for grain operations, biological assets, and disclosure requirements[^1]

***

## Conclusion

The producer current account at an Argentine acopiador is a sophisticated construct that bridges physical commodity management and financial accounting. The key architectural insight for ERP design is the **dual-ledger nature** — kilos and pesos run in parallel but are connected through well-defined events (sales, price fixings, service billings). Every transaction maps to an official electronic document filed with AFIP, making document-driven design essential. The system must handle complex merma calculations with sequential application, multiple tax retention regimes dependent on the producer's SISA status, and flexible service contracts that vary by grain type, campaign, and individual commercial agreements.
