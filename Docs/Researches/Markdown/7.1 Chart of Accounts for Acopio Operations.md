# Chart of Accounts for an Argentine Acopiador de Granos

## Executive Summary

There is no single official chart of accounts published by FACPCE or any professional body exclusively for grain storage operators (acopiadores) in Argentina. However, a practical, production-ready structure can be assembled from FACPCE technical resolutions (RT 9, RT 17, RT 22/46, RT 54), the detailed consignment accounting model published in *Consultor Agropecuario* by De Carli (2023), the Bolsa de Comercio de Rosario operational analysis, and AFIP/ARCA grain-trade regulations. This report provides a complete, hierarchical chart of accounts with account codes, organized by the six areas requested, and annotated with the regulatory and practical rationale behind each section.[^1][^2]

***

## 1. Revenue Accounts Specific to Acopio

An acopiador derives revenue from two fundamentally different business models that coexist: **service provision** (storage, drying, cleaning, fumigation, freight, analysis) and **grain trading** (buy/sell on own account). When acting as a consignatario, revenue comes from commissions and the *diferencia de conjunto* (net result of consignment operations).[^1]

### 1.2 Additional Revenue Lines Often Missed

- **Ingresos por diferencia de calidad** — when grain received exceeds the base quality standard, the acopiador may charge a quality discount or retain a quality premium.[^1]
- **Ingresos por venta de combustibles/lubricantes** — many acopiadores sell diesel (gasoil) to producers, which constitutes a separate revenue stream subject to distinct IIBB treatment in Córdoba (code 466123, 2.00%–3.25%).[^3]
- **Ingresos por canjes** — when input sales (seeds, agrochemicals) are settled in grain, the revenue from the input sale and the grain acquisition are recorded separately.[^1]

## 2. Cost of Goods Sold for Grain Trading

### 2.1 COGS Calculation

When the acopiador trades grain on its own account, the **Costo de Mercadería Vendida (CMV)** is calculated using the classic formula:[^2]

\[ \text{CMV} = \text{Existencia Inicial} + \text{Compras} - \text{Existencia Final} \]

Under the permanent inventory system (sistema de inventario permanente), each purchase is recorded at acquisition cost including the purchase price on the *Liquidación Primaria de Granos* (LPG), freight to plant, and non-recoverable taxes.[^1]

### 2.2 Costing Method: Weighted Average (Preferred)

- **LIFO is prohibited** under both NIC 2 (IAS 2) and Argentine professional standards (RT 17/RT 54).[^4][^5]
- **FIFO** and **weighted-average cost** are the permitted methods. For grain — a fungible commodity where individual lots lose identity once deposited in silos — **weighted-average cost is the predominant method** used in practice. Once grain enters the silo, it cannot be individually identified, making FIFO largely theoretical.[^6][^1]
- Fowler Newton notes that for fungible goods, the weighted-average method is the most practical because it assigns a single cost per unit to all identical items in stock.[^6]

### 2.3 Campaign-Year Inventory Valuation

Argentine grain operations follow crop-campaign cycles (e.g., soja campaign: April–March). At each balance-sheet date, own-grain inventory must be measured at the **lower of cost or net realizable value (VNR)**, following RT 17 section 5.5 (now RT 54, chapter 3, paragraphs 287–312):[^7][^5]

- **VNR** = Market price (pizarra price at the relevant exchange, e.g., Rosario CAC) minus estimated costs to sell (freight to port, commissions, paritaria, drying/cleaning if applicable).
- For commodities with an active, transparent market — which grains clearly have — the VNR measurement is reliable. When VNR > cost, the gain can be recognized immediately (mark-to-market). When VNR < cost, the loss must be recognized.[^7]
- RT 22 / RT 46 (Actividad Agropecuaria) specifically governs biological assets at fair value less costs to sell, but this applies to **producers** at the point of harvest. For an acopiador, grain held as inventory post-purchase is treated under the general **bienes de cambio** rules of RT 17 / RT 54.[^8][^9]

## 3. Inventory Accounts

### 3.1 Own Grain: Separate Accounts per Grain Type

Own-grain inventory must be broken out by grain type because each has different market prices, quality parameters, and seasonal dynamics. This is both an operational necessity and an accounting best practice.[^1]

| 1.3 | **BIENES DE CAMBIO** | |

### 3.2 Third-Party Grain: Off-Balance-Sheet (Cuentas de Orden)

Grain deposited by producers or already purchased by exporters/industrials awaiting dispatch is **not the property of the acopiador**. It is tracked through *cuentas de orden* (memorandum/off-balance-sheet accounts). Under Argentine accounting doctrine, these are disclosed in the notes to financial statements or as separate line items below the balance sheet.[^1]

| Code | Account Name | Notes |
|------|-------------|-------|
| 8.1 | **MANDATOS Y CONSIGNACIONES** | Cuentas de orden |

| 8.1.02 | Trigo en consignación – Entrega | Credit when grain delivered to buyer |
| 8.1.03–12 | (Same pair for Maíz, Soja, Girasol, Cebada, Avena) | |

The De Carli (2023) model shows the journal entry for an LPG (consignment receipt):[^1]

- **Debit**: Trigo en consignación recibo
- **Credit**: Trigo en consignación recibo (a saldar en la final)
- **Debit**: IVA – Crédito Fiscal
- **Credit**: Comisión / IVA – Débito Fiscal / Sellados y gastos de terceros / Retención Ganancias / Retención IIBB / Retención IVA / Deudas comerciales

### 3.3 Distinguishing Own vs. Third-Party Grain

The critical distinction in the chart of accounts is:

- **1.3.XX (Bienes de cambio)** — Only grain the acopiador owns (purchased via LPG01 compraventa). Appears on the balance sheet as a current asset.
- **8.1.XX (Cuentas de orden)** — Grain held on behalf of producers/buyers (received via consignment or deposit certificate). Does NOT appear as an asset or liability on the balance sheet.[^1]
- **Operaciones pendientes de liquidar (1.3.30 / 2.3.30)** — Active (asset) or passive (liability) position accounts per grain type, reflecting net uncommitted obligations from sales not yet invoiced or purchases not yet settled. If tonnes sold exceed tonnes bought, a liability is recognized for the pending purchase.[^1]

## 4. Tax-Related Accounts

Argentine grain trade is subject to an especially complex tax overlay. The acopiador acts simultaneously as taxpayer, withholding agent, and perception agent across national, provincial, and municipal taxes.

### 4.1 IVA (Value Added Tax)

Grain sales are subject to IVA at the reduced rate of **10.5%**. The IVA retention regime for grain (SISA) imposes withholding rates of 5%, 7%, or 8% on the total purchase price depending on the seller's SISA classification. Because the acopiador's value-added is only 3–4%, the 8% retention on the full invoice generates chronic *saldo a favor* (credit balances) that accumulate over time — a well-documented structural problem for the sector.[^10][^11]

### 4.3 Fondo Sojero (FOFESO)

The Fondo Federal Solidario, which redistributed 30% of soybean export duties to provinces and municipalities, was **derogated by Decree 756/2018** and no longer exists as a liability for acopiadores. It should not be included in a current chart of accounts.[^12]

### 4.4 Export Duties (Derechos de Exportación)

As of December 2025, permanent rates are: soja 24%, sub-products 22.5%, trigo/cebada 7.5%, maíz/sorgo 8.5%, girasol 4.5%. While these are legally borne by the exporter, they affect the FAS price that the acopiador receives and should be tracked if the entity exports directly.[^13]

## 5. Receivable and Payable Accounts

### 5.1 How Acopiador A/R and A/P Differ from a Typical Business

The acopiador's receivables and payables are tightly coupled with the **physical grain position**. Unlike a standard commercial business:[^1]

- **Settlement may occur in kind**: a producer may deliver grain instead of cash to settle a debt for inputs or financing, requiring recognition of both the grain receipt and the debt extinguishment.
- **"A fijar" (to-fix-price) operations** create contingent obligations: the producer delivers grain without setting a price; the acopiador owes whatever the price will be when the producer decides to fix it. This creates a liability whose amount is unknown until the fixing date.[^2]
- **Net position tracking** is critical: at any moment the acopiador may be "long" (comprado) or "short" (vendido) in a given grain, and the receivable/payable structure must reflect this.[^1]

### 5.3 "Productores – Cuentas Corrientes" as a Central Account

This is the most distinctive account in grain-trade accounting. Each producer has a running-balance sub-ledger that accumulates:[^1]

- Credits: grain deliveries (valued at purchase price or "a fijar"), reimbursement of services.
- Debits: input advances (canjes), cash advances, storage/conditioning fees, tax withholdings, commission charges.

The net balance may be positive (amount owed to the producer) or negative (advance owed by the producer). It functions as an integrated current-account that combines commercial and financial relationships in one ledger — fundamentally different from the simple A/P of a retail or manufacturing business.

## 6. Official or Recommended Chart of Accounts

### 6.1 FACPCE Position

**No FACPCE technical resolution publishes a chart of accounts specific to acopiadores.** The relevant FACPCE norms are:[^5][^14][^8]

| Resolution | Scope | Relevance to Acopiador |
|-----------|-------|----------------------|
| RT 8 | General financial statement presentation | Overall structure (assets, liabilities, equity, results) |
| RT 9 | Presentation for commercial/industrial/service entities | Classification of bienes de cambio, receivables, payables[^14] |
| RT 17 / RT 54 (NUA) | Measurement rules (bienes de cambio, VNR, cost) | Inventory valuation criteria[^5] |
| RT 22 / RT 46 | Actividad Agropecuaria | Primarily for producers (biological assets, harvest-point valuation); of limited direct relevance to pure acopiadores[^8][^9] |

The FACPCE Informe N° 25 (Área Contabilidad) provides worked examples for agropecuaria entities, but these focus on biological-asset producers rather than storage/trading operators.[^15]

In March 2026, FACPCE announced **COSEAGRO**, a new software platform for agropecuaria entities presented at Expoagro. This may eventually include standardized chart-of-accounts templates, but documentation is not yet publicly available.[^16]

### 6.2 Argentine Academic Textbooks

- **Fowler Newton** (*Contabilidad Básica*, *Cuestiones Contables Fundamentales*, 6th ed., 2020) provides extensive treatment of bienes de cambio measurement for fungible goods using weighted-average cost assignment, and VNR for commodities with active markets. He does not publish an acopiador-specific chart of accounts.[^17][^6]
- **Pahlen Acuña** and **García** are referenced in the FACPCE agropecuaria literature but likewise address general agropecuaria accounting rather than the acopio-specific structure.
- The most specific published source is **De Carli, Florencia** (2023), *"El Acopio de Granos,"* in *Consultor Agropecuario* (Editorial Errepar), which provides the consignment account listing and journal entries cited throughout this report.[^1]

### 6.3 AFIP / ARCA Requirements

The *Registro Sistémico de Movimientos y Existencias de Granos* (AFIP) requires electronic reconciliation of grain movements with the acopiador's accounting records. RG 3419 (LPG), RG 3690 (LSG), and RG 3691 (certification of deposit/withdrawal/transfer) — as amended by RG 5687/2025 — define the documentary backbone that the chart of accounts must support.[^18][^1]

## Key Implementation Recommendations

- **Grain-type sub-accounts** are essential throughout revenue, COGS, inventory, consignment, and position accounts. The minimum set should cover: Trigo, Maíz, Soja, Girasol, Cebada, Avena, and an "Otros" catchall.
- **"A precio" vs. "a fijar" tracking** should be handled as an attribute or sub-ledger dimension on the payable account (2.2.01 vs. 2.2.03), not as separate account numbers, to avoid chart-of-accounts proliferation.
- **Position reporting** (comprado/vendido per grain, per day) is a critical operational control that can be built from account balances in 1.3, 2.2, 2.3, and 8.1.[^1]
- **Provincial IIBB sub-accounts** should be opened for every province where the acopiador has a Convenio Multilateral nexus, as aliquots vary significantly (e.g., Córdoba grain trading at 0.50% vs. almacenaje at 4.75%).[^3]
- **Adjustment for inflation (ajuste por inflación)** must be applied to the full chart of accounts per RT 6 / RT 54 in periods of high inflation, which Argentina has experienced continuously since 2018.
