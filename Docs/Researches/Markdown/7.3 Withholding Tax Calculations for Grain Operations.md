# Withholding tax rules on grain purchase/sale in Argentina (IVA, Ganancias, IIBB, SUSS) for ERP automation – 2025‑2026

## 1. Scope and main sources

This report summarizes the regimes of **withholding (retenciones) and collection (percepciones)** applicable to **grain purchase and sale operations** in Argentina as of 2025‑2026, focusing on acopiadores and other intermediaries who liquidate primary grain transactions.

Key primary sources used:

- **AFIP – IVA on grains:** historical RG 2300 and its predecessors, and the current SISA‑based regime documented by AFIP/ARCA under the heading “Régimen de retención del IVA – Operaciones de granos y semillas en proceso de certificación (cereales y oleaginosas) y legumbres secas”, which specifies the current percentages by SISA state and situation of the seller.[^1][^2]
- **AFIP – Ganancias on grains:** RG 4325 (Impuesto a las Ganancias – Comercialización de granos no destinados a la siembra y legumbres secas – Régimen de retención), which replaced RG 2118 and expressly ties the regime to SISA scoring.[^3][^4][^5]
- **AFIP – Ganancias general:** RG 830 and its amendments, especially RG 4525, which updated the “montos no sujetos a retención” for the general regime; plus professional interpretations confirming that those amounts continue to be used where the specific grain regime does not apply.[^6][^7]
- **AFIP/ARCA – SISA:** official explanations of SISA states and obligations of agents of retention, and professional summaries (Blog del Contador) of how RG 4325 and 4326 interact with SISA.[^2][^8]
- **Liquidación Primaria de Granos (LPG):** RG 3419 (and later amendments) establishing the electronic LPG as mandatory documentation of primary grain operations and clarifying that the LPG, together with F.2005 in SIRE, functions as the certificate of retention for IVA and Ganancias.[^9][^10][^11]
- **SIRE / F.2005:** RG 3726 and RG 4523, plus AFIP/Consejo circulars explaining that for LPG to be valid as retention support, the F.2005 certificate must be generated in SIRE.[^12][^13][^14]
- **IIBB provincial regimes:** provincial DGR/API/ARBA/ATER/DGR‑LP rules and professional summaries for Córdoba, Santa Fe, Buenos Aires, Entre Ríos and La Pampa, including: Córdoba RN 36/19 (padrones de alícuotas), Santa Fe RG 15/1997 as amended by RG 37/2025 and its new PARP padrón, La Pampa RG DGR 54/07 (Anexo XI – Producción primaria – cereales y oleaginosas), and ARBA’s “Régimen de Recaudación por Sujeto” and SIRCAR/SIRCREB documentation.[^15][^16][^17][^18][^19][^20][^21][^22]
- **SUSS:** RG 1784 and related material, plus professional instructions explaining scope and alícuota of the general SUSS retention regime.[^23][^24]

Where 2025‑2026 updates exist (e.g., Santa Fe RG 37/2025 for IIBB, ARBA FAQs updated 2026, SIRCREB/SIRCAR and provincial news), they are explicitly mentioned.[^17][^18][^25][^21]


## 2. IVA withholding on grain purchases (successor to RG 2300)

### 2.1. Legal framework and current regime

Historically, **RG 2300** regulated IVA retentions on grain commercialization, linked to the old Registro Fiscal de Operadores de Granos. With the introduction of **SISA**, AFIP replaced and re‑framed grain‑specific regimes through RG 4310, 4325, 4326 and related resolutions.[^26][^27][^28]

The **current IVA withholding regime for grains** is documented by AFIP/ARCA under the SISA section “Régimen de retención del IVA – Operaciones de granos y semillas en proceso de certificación (cereales y oleaginosas) y legumbres secas – excepto arroz”. This regime applies to:[^29][^2]

- Sales of **grains and seeds in process of certification (cereales y oleaginosas)** and **legumbres secas** not intended for sowing, except rice.[^2]
- Sellers who are **Responsables Inscriptos in IVA** and who are registered and scored within **SISA**.[^29][^2]
- Primary and subsequent commercialization, provided the operation is reached by IVA.

Operations where the seller is **monotributista** or **IVA‑exempt** are not covered by this special regime and therefore do **not suffer IVA retention under it**.[^8][^29]


### 2.2. IVA withholding rates by SISA status and seller type

According to the SISA/AFIP table for IVA grain retention:[^2]

| Seller situation (SISA / AFIP) | IVA retention rate |
| --- | --- |
| SISA State 1 (RIESGO BAJO) – grain producer or operator | **5%** of taxable IVA base |
| SISA State 2 (RIESGO MEDIO) – grain producer or operator | **8%** of taxable IVA base |
| SISA State 3 (RIESGO ALTO) – grain producer or operator | **10,5%** of taxable IVA base |
| Non‑registered in SISA / suspended / excluded (but IVA RI) | **16%** of taxable IVA base |
| Registrants with “sin operar” / inhabilitado status | **16%** of taxable IVA base and other sanctions may apply |
| Seller monotributista or IVA‑exempt | **0%** (regime not applicable) |

- For **operations with rice**, AFIP maintains a separate table; in practice, most acopiadores handle cereals/oleaginosas and legumbres secas, for which the table above is relevant.[^2]
- The **5% rate for State 1** is typically **fully refundable** by AFIP to producers through a systematic credit/reintegro mechanism, which is why State 1 implies no net IVA cost if the producer is compliant.[^27][^29]

As of early **2026**, there are **no published AFIP norms changing these IVA grain rates**; updates since 2024 have focused on general IVA/Ganancias retention/perception thresholds for other regimes, not on the grain‑specific SISA table.[^30]


### 2.3. IVA withholding base and calculation rules

AFIP’s SISA IVA regime and professional commentary establish the following base rules:[^31][^2]

- **Base imponible:**
  - Start from the **taxable amount for IVA on the sale of grains**, i.e. the price net of any discounts or bonuses, **excluding IVA itself**.
  - From that base, **deduct only charges and expenses that share the same VAT rate and are passed on to the seller**, such as certain commissions, flete subject to the same alícuota, etc., if invoiced in the same document and with IVA discriminated.[^31]
  - Do **not** deduct federal or provincial withholdings (Ganancias, IIBB, SUSS) or municipal fees when calculating the base for IVA retention.[^31]

- **Moment of calculation:**
  - The retention is calculated **per payment**, including partial payments and adjustments, taking the **LPG or invoice amount associated to that payment** and applying the corresponding rate.[^32][^12]

- **Minimum threshold (monto no sujeto a retención):**
  - Unlike some generic IVA regimes, the **grain IVA regime does not establish an explicit monetary “monto no sujeto a retención”** in the current SISA‑based rules; retention applies in principle from the first peso of base, except for operational de minimis rules that may exist in the SIRE system (importes mínimos de retención genéricos).[^13][^32]
  - Professional sources that summarise AFIP’s 2024–2025 updates to IVA/Ganancias retention thresholds indicate changes for **card and e‑commerce regimes**, but do not list a specific threshold for grain IVA retention, confirming that the original structure remains.[^30]

ERP implication: implement **no specific IVA grain threshold**, but respect any **system‑level minimum retention amount** configured in SIRE/SICORE if AFIP later introduces one.


### 2.4. Treatment of exempt operations

- **Exempt or non‑taxable grain operations** (e.g., exports covered by export exemptions, or operations where the seller is IVA‑exempt) fall **outside the special IVA retention regime** because no IVA is liquidated on the transaction and/or the seller is not IVA RI.
- Blog del Contador’s analysis of the SISA reforms confirms that the **IVA perception/retention mechanisms do not apply when the provider is monotributista**, which in practice covers most small primary producers who have opted for the simplified regime.[^8]

ERP implication: if seller’s IVA status is **Monotributo or Exento**, **do not compute IVA retention under the SISA grain regime**; only compute Ganancias/IIBB where applicable.


## 3. Income Tax (Ganancias) withholding on grain purchases

### 3.1. Specific grain regime (RG 4325) vs. general RG 830

The **main regime for Ganancias retention on grain commercialization** is **RG 4325/2018**, which **replaces RG 2118** and is explicitly tied to SISA scoring.[^4][^5][^3]

Key points:[^5][^3]

- RG 4325 implements a **specific regime of Ganancias retention** for sales of **granos no destinados a la siembra (cereales y oleaginosas) y legumbres secas**.
- The regime applies to **each payment made for the sale**, including adjustments, interests, etc., documented in the LPG or invoice.
- It **excludes operations covered by the general RG 830 regime**; Blog del Contador explicitly notes that “las operaciones comprendidas quedan excluidas del régimen de retención establecido en la RG 830”.[^8]
- It also provides that the regime **does not apply when the provider is monotributista**.[^8]

Therefore, for **grain purchases by acopiadores, exporters and industries** in 2025‑2026, **RG 4325 is the primary Ganancias regime**; **RG 830 only applies residually** for non‑grain transactions or exceptional cases.


### 3.2. RG 4325 – Rates by SISA state and registration

RG 4325 sets different Ganancias withholding rates depending on the SISA state and whether the seller is registered as a grain producer/operator.[^3][^26]

From RG 4325 and professional summaries:[^33][^26][^3]

| Seller situation | Ganancias withholding rate |
| --- | --- |
| SISA State 1 (RIESGO BAJO) – registered producer / operator | **0%** (no Ganancias retention) |
| SISA State 2 (RIESGO MEDIO) – registered producer / operator | **2%** of base subject to retention |
| SISA State 3 (RIESGO ALTO) – registered producer / operator | **15%** of base subject to retention |
| Seller not registered in SISA (no inscripción o baja) | **30%** of base subject to retention |
| Seller monotributista (RS) | **0%** – regime not applicable (express exclusion) |

- The **0% rate for State 1** is one of the key SISA incentives: compliant producers in State 1 do **not suffer Ganancias retention** on grain sales.[^27]
- For **State 2 and 3**, RG 4325 provides that the retentions are **direct payments on account of the seller’s annual Ganancias liability**.[^3]

As of 2025‑2026, there is **no public evidence of changes to these RG 4325 rates**; later AFIP updates focus on SISA scoring criteria and general Ganancias thresholds, not on the grain-specific percentages.[^33]


### 3.3. RG 4325 – Base imponible and minimum threshold

RG 4325 defines the **monto no sujeto a retención and mínimum retention amount** as follows (Art. 11 and related articles, as summarised by professional sources):[^34][^26][^3]

- **Monto no sujeto a retención (per LPG/payment):** **142.400 pesos**.
  - Retention applies only on the **excess over 142.400** per payment/document for the operations covered.
- **Importe mínimo de retención:** professional comments indicate that the **minimum actual Ganancias retention amount** follows the **general thresholds updated by RG 4525**, which for **“enajenación de bienes muebles y bienes de cambio”** set a **“monto no sujeto” of 224.000 pesos and a minimum retention of 240 pesos** for the general regime; however, for RG 4325 operations, the specific **142.400 base continues to be used unless AFIP modifies it, and subsequent updates have not explicitly changed it.**[^7][^6]

In practice, applied rules used by tax practitioners in 2025–2026 are:[^26][^7][^3]

- If the **net amount (without IVA) of the LPG/invoice** does **not exceed 142.400**, no Ganancias retention is applied under RG 4325.
- If it exceeds 142.400, the appropriate percentage (2%, 15% or 30%) is applied **on the full net amount**, not just the excess, as long as the resulting retention surpasses the **general minimum retention amount** (currently around **240 pesos** by reference to RG 4525 and confirmed by professional guidance that these minimums remain in force).[^6][^7]

ERP implication: parameterise **142.400** as the **RG 4325 specific “monto no sujeto a retención”** and a **generic minimum retention amount (~240)** consistent with the general Ganancias regime, and keep both configurable for future AFIP updates.


### 3.4. Treatment of monotributistas and non‑registered sellers

Blog del Contador and earlier RG 2118 text (still reflected in RG 4325) clarify that the **grain Ganancias regime does not apply when the provider is monotributista**, i.e. adheres to the Régimen Simplificado.[^35][^8]

- **Monotributistas:**
  - **No Ganancias retention** under RG 4325.
  - No RG 830 retention either, since the income is already taxed via Monotributo.[^8]

- **Non‑registered or uninscribed sellers (no CUIT or not registered in SISA/AFIP):**
  - Subject to the **30% rate** on the RG 4325 base, after considering the 142.400 threshold, as long as they are clearly identified and the operation is linked to grain sales.[^3]
  - In practice, acopiadores avoid operating with completely non‑registered persons because of this heavy withholding and compliance risk.


### 3.5. Operations involving canje (payment in kind)

RG 4325 and RG 4326, together with SISA documentation, address **canje operations** where payment of goods or services is made with grains.[^2][^8]

Key rules for ERP implementation:

- **Canje grains for goods/services (IVA and Ganancias):**
  - RG 4326 modifies IVA perception rules for **operations of sale of things, services or works, cancelled with delivery of grains**, except rice and certain legumbres.[^8]
  - For **Ganancias**, the **retention regime of RG 4325 still applies to the grain component**: the value of the grains delivered is treated as the **amount subject to Ganancias retention**, even though no cash is paid.[^3]

- **Base and timing:**
  - Determine the **market value of the grains** (price agreed in the canje, consistent with LPG or canje contract) **net of IVA** as the base.[^2]
  - Apply the corresponding RG 4325 rate (0%, 2%, 15%, 30%) depending on SISA state.
  - The retention is considered **“practicada” at the moment of imputing the value of the grains** to cancel the obligation for the goods or services.

- **Practical effect:**
  - As there is **no cash outflow**, the retention usually generates a **credit in favour of AFIP**, which is settled via SIRE/Ganancias or offsets against other obligations.

ERP implication: treat canje as if **cash had been paid** equal to the **value of the grains**; compute Ganancias retention on that notional cash amount and record a **Ganancias withholding payable account**, even though the seller receives grains instead of money.


### 3.6. When RG 830 applies

For **non‑grain operations** (e.g., purchase of fixed assets, services unrelated to grain commercialization) RG 830 and its amendments (e.g., RG 4525) continue to apply.

- For **“enajenación de bienes muebles y bienes de cambio”** under RG 830, the **monto no sujeto a retención** was updated by RG 4525 to **224.000 pesos**, with a **2% rate** over the excess and a **minimum retention of 240 pesos**; professional Q&A confirms that these bounds remain in force and are used to decide whether to retain when paying for movable goods (outside the special grain regime).[^7][^6]

ERP implication: keep **two Ganancias modules**:

- **Grain module (RG 4325)** tied to **SISA** for cereal/oleaginous/legume sales.
- **General module (RG 830)** for all other goods/services, using RG 4525‑updated thresholds.


## 4. IIBB withholding regimes by province

### 4.1. General characteristics and limitations

Unlike AFIP regimes, **Ingresos Brutos is a provincial tax**, and each province defines its **own retention/perception regimes**, often using **padrones of alícuotas by CUIT** and/or **SIRCAR/SIRCREB** systems.[^16][^36][^37]

Important implications for ERP design:

- There is **no single national IIBB rate** for grain operations; each province (Córdoba, Santa Fe, Buenos Aires, Entre Ríos, La Pampa, etc.) applies its own **combination of padrones, minimum thresholds and rates**.
- For many regimes, the **exact rate for a given counterparty and period comes from a machine‑readable padrón (CSV/TXT)** which must be imported regularly into the ERP.[^18][^36][^38][^16]
- In several provinces, **primary production of grains is exempt at the local level**, so a “pure” producer who only has primary production and sells FOB to an acopiador may have **0% IIBB**, but still be subject to retenciones/percepciones if not properly classified in padrones.[^39][^40]

The following subsections summarise, for each requested province, the **legal basis, typical base and thresholds**, and how padrones interact with grain operations.


### 4.2. Córdoba (DGR)

#### 4.2.1. Applicable regime and padrones

Córdoba regulates its IIBB retentions and perceptions through several norms; for practical ERP purposes, the key one is **Resolución Normativa DGR 36/19**, which implements **padrones of alícuotas** for retention and perception regimes, with **monthly publication**.[^41][^16]

- RN 36/19 establishes that DGR will publish **padrones of alícuotas** for each regime **on the 22nd of each month or next working day**, to be used for operations in the following period.[^16]
- Agents (including acopiadores) must consult these padrones electronically and apply the **alícuota assigned to each CUIT** for IIBB retention and perception.

There is **no single fixed IIBB rate for grain operations**; the rate is **CUIT‑ and regime‑specific**.

#### 4.2.2. Base and minimum

While RN 36/19 focuses on padrones, Cordoba’s Código Tributario and complementary instructions specify that:[^41]

- **Base:** typically the **net amount of the operation excluding IVA**, similar to the general rule for IIBB retentions.
- **Minimum threshold:** minimums for application of retentions/perceptions are set in the combination of **Código Tributario, Ley Impositiva Anual and DGR instructions**; they are **not grain‑specific** and often change via annual tax laws.

Given that **RN 36/19 delegates the effective rate and often the minimum to the padrón**, ERP implementation should treat **both IIBB rate and “monto mínimo” as data imported from the padrón**, not as hard‑coded constants.


### 4.3. Santa Fe (API)

#### 4.3.1. Regime and 2025 reform (RG API 37/2025)

Santa Fe’s IIBB retention/perception regime is based on **RG 15/1997 (text ordered by RG 18/2014)**, substantially amended by **RG API 37/2025**.[^42][^17]

RG API 37/2025 introduces:[^17][^18]

- A new **Padrón de Alícuotas de Retención/Percepción (PARP)**, published **monthly**, which assigns **23 groups of alícuotas** by taxpayer based on activity, compliance and other factors.
- A requirement that agents (including grain buyers designated as such) **consult and apply the PARP** before liquidating operations.

RG 37/2025 also sets **default rates and thresholds** where no padrón data exist:[^18]

- **If the subject is not included in the PARP and does not evidence inscription:**
  - **Retentions:** apply **5%**.
  - **Perceptions:** apply **6%**.
- **Montos mínimos actualizados (from 2025):**
  - **Retenciones:** do not apply when **each payment does not exceed 650.000 pesos** in the general cases listed in RG 37/2025 (certain incisos of Art. 1 and 2).
  - For specific cases (e.g., regime on certain services) a lower minimum **180.000 pesos per payment** applies.
  - **Percepciones:** do not apply when the **base of each operation does not exceed 360.000 pesos**, except for **acquisitions of meats and poultry products**, where the **minimum per operation is 650.000 pesos**, aggregating daily operations per subject.[^17][^18]

These thresholds were significantly increased by RG 37/2025, effective for 2025 and 2026, and are specifically cited in Santa Fe professional communications.[^18][^17]

#### 4.3.2. Base and primary production

- **Base imponible:** for most regimes, the **amount subject to IVA excluding IVA** (net price) is used as base for IIBB retention/perception.[^42]
- **Primary production:** Santa Fe’s Código Fiscal grants an exemption for certain **primary production activities** (Art. 213 inc. e’), including primary grain production, but the **application of exemptions in practice depends on correct inscription and classification**; Di Paolo’s analysis highlights the need to manage padrones carefully to avoid excessive upstream retentions on primary producers.[^39]

ERP implication: import **PARP padrones monthly (CSV/TXT)**, apply **padron‑specific alícuota and minimums**, and fall back to **5% / 6% rates with 650.000 / 360.000 thresholds** only when counterparty is not present and no valid exemption cert is provided.


### 4.4. Buenos Aires (ARBA – SIRCAR/SIRCREB)

#### 4.4.1. Regimes involved

In the Province of Buenos Aires, IIBB collection on grain‑related activities can be triggered by:

- **Régimen de Recaudación por Sujeto (ARBA):** padrones of **retention/perception alícuotas by CUIT** for general IIBB operations.[^21][^22]
- **SIRCAR:** a unified system for reporting IIBB retentions/perceptions across jurisdictions, used for extra‑bank operations.[^36][^37]
- **SIRCREB:** bank‑account‑based collection on **accredited amounts**, applied for Convenio Multilateral and some local taxpayers.[^19][^20]

#### 4.4.2. ARBA padrón – rates, base and minimums

ARBA’s **“Régimen de Recaudación por Sujeto”** documentation and FAQs (updated 2026) explain:[^22][^21]

- ARBA publishes **monthly padrones** that contain the **retention and perception rates (alícuotas)** applicable to **each CUIT**.
- Agents must download and import these padrones, or consult individual CUITs via ARBA’s web.
- For counterparties **not included in the padrón**, ARBA applies **subsidiary maximum alícuotas** defined in Disposición Normativa Serie B 01/04:
  - **Percepciones:** **8%** maximum.
  - **Retenciones:** **4%** maximum.
- In case of **technical problems preventing padrón consultation**, agents must apply temporary default rates:
  - **Percepciones:** **3%**.
  - **Retenciones:** **1,75%**.[^21]

**Base imponible:**

- As a rule, IIBB retention/perception in Buenos Aires is calculated on the **net taxable amount of the operation excluding IVA**; specific regimes may have exceptions, but this is the default.

**Minimum thresholds:**

- Minimums for applying retentions/perceptions under the general regime are set by **Ley Impositiva and ARBA norms** and change over time; however, ARBA’s current public focus is on **who must act as agent and at what billing threshold**, not on altering the basic operational minimums.
- Recent ARBA communications (early 2026) raised the **billing threshold for being designated as agent to 7.800 million pesos of annual income**, but this affects **who becomes agent**, not the per‑operation “monto mínimo” for retentions.[^43][^44]

ERP implication: treat **alícuota and any “mínimo de aplicación” as padrón‑driven**, not fixed; implement fallback rates **4% / 8%** (or **1,75% / 3%** if padrón unavailable) only when ARBA’s web services or local copies are not accessible.


#### 4.4.3. SIRCREB (multilateral treatment)

**SIRCREB** is a unified system for **IIBB recaudación on bank account credits** for Convenio Multilateral (and some local) taxpayers across multiple provinces, including Buenos Aires and CABA.[^45][^46]

Key technical aspects:[^20][^47][^19]

- **Base:** the **total amount credited** to bank accounts (in pesos or foreign currency) subject to SIRCREB.
- **Alícuota:**
  - The percentage applied by banks for each CUIT and period is calculated centrally by COMARB based on the **last CM03 declaration (bases imponibles by jurisdiction)** and the **alícuotas set by each province**, using a **weighted sum**; the final rate is rounded and allocated by jurisdiction.[^47][^19]
  - There is **no single fixed SIRCREB rate**; each CUIT‑period has its own percentage.
  - COMARB FAQ confirms that to know the applicable alícuota, the taxpayer must **consult SIRCREB with CUIT and period (mes/año)**.[^20]
- **Frequency:** padrones are typically generated **monthly**, after the last CM03 due date; some jurisdictions may adjust more frequently, but the banks apply a monthly alícuota per CUIT.[^19][^47]

ERP implication: for SIRCREB, **do not hard‑code rates**; instead, use **bank account statements (which show retention amount)** as input and map them into IIBB credits per jurisdiction. For forecasting, query **SIRCREB padrones** via COMARB’s or provincial interfaces.


### 4.5. Entre Ríos (ATER)

Entre Ríos uses several IIBB retention/perception regimes administered by **ATER** and integrated with SIRCAR; no **grain‑exclusive** IIBB regime exists, but primary production is often exempt under the provincial Código Fiscal.

Key references:

- **ATE R Res. 319/2016:** establishes and modifies regimes of IIBB retention/perception for specific sectors (fuel, etc.), describing the **base for retentions and obligations of agents**; it clarifies that agents must withhold on payments made to taxpayers registered in Entre Ríos when they are designated as agents.[^48][^49]
- ATER’s online services include **padrón of alícuotas and regimes**, accessed and reported via **SIRCAR**.[^50][^36]

In practice for grain operations:

- **Base:** net amount of the operation excluding IVA for payments to Entre Ríos IIBB taxpayers.
- **Rate:** determined from **ATER’s padrón of alícuotas (through SIRCAR)** for the supplier’s CUIT; there is no uniform grain rate.
- **Minimum:** per‑operation minimums exist and are updated periodically via ATER norms; they are not grain‑specific and must be parameterised as padrón or configuration data.

ERP implication: treat **IIBB retention in Entre Ríos as fully padrón‑driven** (SIRCAR) and avoid hard‑coding percentages; maintain mapping of **subject’s jurisdiction and CM** location to decide whether and when to apply ATER regimes.


### 4.6. La Pampa (DGR)

La Pampa has one of the clearest **grain‑specific IIBB retention regimes** in **RG DGR 54/07**, with several annexes; for grains, **Anexo XI – “Producción primaria – cereales, oleaginosas y productos frutihortícolas”** is key.[^15]

#### 4.6.1. Subjects and operations covered

Anexo XI designates as **agents of retention**:

- Acopiadores, industries, cooperatives, producer associations and other entities that **acquire or intermediate in the commercialization of cereals, oleaginosas and frutihortícolas**.[^15]
- In the case of cereals and oleaginosas, agents must also be registered in the **Registro Fiscal de Operadores de Granos (AFIP RG 2300 and successors)**.[^28][^15]

#### 4.6.2. Base and percentages

The annex establishes:[^15]

- **Base imponible:**
  - As per Anexo I, IIBB retentions are calculated on the **net amount of the operation excluding IVA** when the seller is IVA RI and IVA is discriminated; otherwise on the **total amount**.[^15]
- **Application percentages on base:**
  - **100% of base** for operations in La Pampa with production from La Pampa.
  - **85% of base** for operations with La Pampa production sold in another jurisdiction.
  - **15% of base** for operations in La Pampa with production originated in other jurisdictions.[^15]

The actual **IIBB tax rate to be applied on that base is not fixed in the RG**, but is the **one set annually by the Ley Impositiva Anual** for the corresponding activity; RG 54/07 only defines the **portion of base** to which the rate applies.[^15]

#### 4.6.3. Minimums and other features

- RG 54/07’s **grain annex does not state an explicit monetary “monto mínimo”** for retentions in Anexo XI; in contrast, other annexes (e.g., Anexo II and IX) do set monetary thresholds (1.000 and 5.600 pesos) for other regimes.[^15]
- Therefore, for grain operations, **any operation assigned to La Pampa jurisdiction and subject to IIBB is in principle fully subject to retention**, unless the seller has a **“Constancia de No Retención/Percepción”** or an exemption.[^15]

ERP implication: implement the **100% / 85% / 15% base adjustment** and apply the **IIBB rate from the Ley Impositiva for the relevant year**, with only generic minimums if the provincial tax law introduces any; also support **Constancias de No Retención** as overrides.


### 4.7. SIRCREB multilateral treatment (summary)

As already noted in 4.4.3, **SIRCREB** is centralised by COMARB and affects grains only **indirectly**, because primary production is often exempt but intermediaries (acopiadores, traders, industries) are usually **Convenio Multilateral taxpayers**.

From COMARB and professional sources:[^47][^19][^20]

- **Base:** all bank account credits subject to the regime.
- **Rate:** no static rate; calculated per CUIT‑period from CM03 and provincial alícuotas; must be **queried from SIRCREB padrones** or deduced from bank statements.
- **Jurisdictional allocation:** retention amount is split among provinces according to their share in CM03.

ERP implication: treat SIRCREB as **bank‑driven withholding imported from bank statements**, not as something computed on a per‑invoice basis in the ERP; allocate retentions to jurisdictions according to COMARB data when available.


## 5. SUSS / Seguridad Social retention

### 5.1. General SUSS retention (RG 1784) and scope

AFIP’s **RG 1784** established a **general regime of retention for SUSS contributions** (contribuciones patronales) applicable to **payments made to certain providers of goods, services and works gravadas by IVA**, where the payer acts as agent of retention.[^51][^23]

- The regime applies to **sales of movable goods, locaciones de obra, locaciones de cosas and prestaciones de servicios**, subject to IVA, with certain exclusions and thresholds.[^23]
- The **standard SUSS retention rate is 1% of the base imponible**, according to RG 1784 as amended by RG 2069 and subsequent instructions.[^24]
- Retention is **not applied** if the **calculated amount is below 400 pesos**, which implies an **effective invoice threshold of 48.400 pesos** (for 1% rate).[^24]

### 5.2. Application to grain operations

SUSS retention **does not apply to the purchase of grains from producers documented via LPG**, because:[^51][^23]

- The regime focuses on **contribuciones patronales for employers**, not on the income tax of the seller.
- The **trigger is the payment for goods/services by entities designated as agents** under RG 1784, in contexts such as construction, cleaning, security, tabacco industry, etc., rather than agricultural primary production.[^23][^51]

However:

- **Ancillary services** purchased by acopiadores – e.g., **fletes, construction, cleaning services** – **may be subject to SUSS retention** if the provider and operation fit the RG 1784 criteria and the payer is a designated agent.[^24]

ERP implication: implement **SUSS retention separately from the grain LPG module**:

- **Do not apply SUSS on LPG grain purchases per se**.
- Apply SUSS only to **invoices for services/goods** that fall under RG 1784 where your client is an agent of retention.


## 6. Special considerations for ERP implementation

### 6.1. Liquidación Primaria de Granos (LPG) vs. invoice

RG 3419 established a **special electronic LPG** as the **mandatory document for primary grain operations involving producers**, replacing paper forms C1116 B/C.[^52][^53][^9]

Key points:[^14][^10][^12]

- LPG **documents the transfer of ownership** of grains in primary operations and serves as the **reference document for IVA and Ganancias withholding**.
- RG 3419 and later AFIP communications clarify that:
  - The **LPG confirmed in the AFIP system, together with the F.2005 certificate generated in SIRE**, constitutes the **“comprobante justificativo de retención”** for IVA and Ganancias under RG 4310 and RG 4325.[^10][^12][^14]
  - For operations where the buyer is an **exporter** or where the LPG is **issued by a corredor included in SISA** in certain conditions, **the LPG alone is not considered a retention certificate**; in those cases only the **F.2005** is valid.[^14]

For **non‑primary operations** (e.g., sales among intermediaries where both are traders and no producer participates), standard **electronic invoices** (facturas) may be used, and IVA/Ganancias retentions are documented exclusively via **F.2005/F.2006 in SIRE/SICORE**.

ERP implication:

- For **LPG‑based operations**, integrate directly with AFIP’s LPG web service or at least capture all LPG fields (COE number, SISA state, kilos, price, discounts) and link them to SIRE’s **F.2005 certificates**.
- For **invoice‑based operations**, map AFIP e‑invoice data to the same tax engine but without LPG‑specific logic.


### 6.2. Order of calculating retentions in a 1116/LPG

Neither RG 4310 nor RG 4325 explicitly imposes an **order between federal and provincial retentions**; they simply define **their own bases**. In practice and in professional practice notes on LPG composition, the following implicit order is used only for calculation convenience:[^54][^32][^31][^3]

1. **Gross value of grains** (kilos × price) – net of trade discounts.
2. **Commercial deductions** (commissions, fletes, gastos) grouped by IVA rate.
3. Calculation of **IVA débito** on the taxable base.
4. Calculation of **IVA retention** on the IVA base according to SISA state.[^31][^2]
5. Calculation of **Ganancias retention** on the **net amount before IVA and before any retentions**, subject to RG 4325 thresholds.[^3]
6. Calculation of **IIBB retention/perception** based on provincial rules (net of IVA), usually independent of previous steps.[^16][^18][^15]
7. Application of any **SUSS or other special retentions** on specific service lines, not on LPG grain lines.

Important: **retentions do not cascade over one another**; each regime uses its own base (gross or net) but does **not subtract other retentions** already calculated.

ERP implication: design the LPG layout so that all retentions are **calculated in parallel** from the appropriate bases, but presented in a logical order (IVA, Ganancias, IIBB, others) for clarity.


### 6.3. Certificates of exclusion and reduced‑rate certificates

#### 6.3.1. AFIP certificates (IVA and Ganancias)

AFIP issues **certificados de exclusión o reducción de alícuotas** for certain retention regimes.

- **Ganancias:** RG 830 (general regime) allows taxpayers to request **exclusion or reduction** certificates where retentions would systematically exceed their annual liability; however, **RG 4325 for grains is a special regime and generally does not accept RG 830 certificates to avoid its own retentions**.[^6][^3]
- **IVA:** AFIP also issues **exclusion certificates** for some IVA retention/perception regimes (e.g., RG 2854, card systems), but the **grain IVA SISA regime is essentially governed by SISA state**, not by separate exclusion certificates; the de facto way to obtain lower impact is to maintain **SISA State 1**.

ERP implication: record AFIP **certificates of exclusion only for those regimes that actually recognise them** (mostly general RG 830 and some IVA perceptions), not for the **SISA‑based grain regimes**, where SISA state is the controlling variable.

#### 6.3.2. Provincial IIBB certificates

Each province issues **Constancias de No Retención/No Percepción o atenuación de alícuotas** for IIBB.

Examples:

- **Córdoba:** RN 6/2009 modifies the **“Certificado de No Retención”** mechanism, requiring taxpayers to notify changes and allowing DGR to suspend or revoke certificates.[^55]
- **La Pampa:** RG 54/07 explicitly exempts agents from retaining/perceiving when the taxpayer presents a **“Constancia de No Retención/Percepción”** issued by DGR; operations must still be reported in the DJ.[^15]
- **ARBA (Buenos Aires):** RN 64/2010 (and 26/2025) regulate **exclusion or reduction of alícuotas** for taxpayers with persistent IIBB credits; applications and approvals are processed digitally and reflected in ARBA padrones.[^56]
- **AGIP (CABA):** AGIP implements **perfiles de riesgo** (Res. 479/2025) and corresponding alícuotas diferenciales for retention/perception; while not a “constancia” per se, risk‑based padrones control the effective rate; CABA also issues specific exclusion certificates for SIRCREB (Res. 211/2020).[^57][^45]
- **Santa Fe (API):** API uses **PARP** and other padrones; updated norms set when and how a taxpayer may obtain **reduced or zero alícuota** in the padrón.[^58][^18]

ERP implication: model **provincial IIBB certificates** as **attributes of the taxpayer** (by CUIT and jurisdiction) that can **override padrón alícuotas or set them to 0%**; include **validity dates** and link them to the correct regime (retention, perception, SIRCREB, etc.).


### 6.4. Handling reduced‑rate situations (SISA states and IIBB padrones)

#### 6.4.1. SISA reduced rates

- **SISA State 1** corresponds effectively to **0% Ganancias** and the **lowest IVA retention (5%) with full refund**, so ERP should treat **State 1 as the default “reduced” case** for federal regimes.[^27][^2]
- SISA state can change periodically; the SISA portal and ARCA documentation specify that SISA performs **periodic recalification based on compliance**, and producers/operators must be checked online **at the moment of retention**.[^29][^2]

ERP implication: at the moment of issuing LPG/invoice, **query SISA (or import its padron) to know the current state**, and only then determine Ganancias/IVA rates; store the **state and timestamp** for audit.

#### 6.4.2. IIBB reduced rates

- Provinces implement **reduced IIBB alícuotas** mainly via **padrones and profiles** (e.g., Santa Fe PARP groups, ARBA “Régimen de recaudación por sujeto”, AGIP risk profiles, La Pampa constancias, etc.).[^57][^21][^16][^18]
- Some provinces explicitly allow **reduction or exclusion** when a taxpayer demonstrates **persistent IIBB credit balances** (e.g., ARBA RN 64/2010, modified by RN 26/2025).[^56]

ERP implication: allow **per‑CUIT, per‑jurisdiction overriding of IIBB alícuotas** based on padron downloads or manual entries; when a **reduced‑rate certificate** is present, adjust **both rate and minimum threshold** accordingly.


### 6.5. Machine‑readable padrones for ARBA, AGIP, API Santa Fe

#### 6.5.1. ARBA (Buenos Aires)

ARBA FAQs and professional ERP guides confirm that:[^59][^60][^22][^21]

- ARBA publishes **monthly padrones of alícuotas** for its **“Régimen de Recaudación por Sujeto”**, which agents can **download from the web** or query by individual CUIT.
- Padrones are provided as **text files (TXT) or CSV**, often **compressed (ZIP)**.
- ERP vendors commonly import these padrones and maintain **monthly versions** to automatically apply the correct alícuota to each CUIT.[^60][^59]

#### 6.5.2. API Santa Fe

API’s **RG 37/2025** defines the **PARP padrón** and requires monthly publication with at least **five working days of advance**, and professional ERP documentation shows that:[^38][^17][^18]

- PARP padrones are distributed in **CSV‑like text formats**, suitable for import.
- ERP systems ingest PADRON files for **current, previous and next periods**, updating client/supplier alícuotas automatically.[^38]

#### 6.5.3. AGIP (CABA) and SIRCREB/SIRCAR

- AGIP implements SIRCREB for CABA through norms like **Res. 211/2020**, with **padrones available through COMARB/SIFERE** and downloadable using **Clave Fiscal**; files are usually **TXT with comma‑separated fields**, also used by SIRCAR.[^61][^45][^36]
- COMARB and SIRCAR documentation indicate that **DDJJ files and padrones** are handled as **TXT/CSV, optionally compressed**, with one record per operation or CUIT.[^37][^36]

ERP implication: implement a **generic padrón‑import subsystem** capable of reading **TXT/CSV (with configurable record layouts)** for ARBA, API Santa Fe, AGIP and other provincial agencies, storing **alícuotas and minimums by CUIT, jurisdiction, regime and validity month**.
