# **Technical and Regulatory Framework for Primary Grain Liquidations in Argentina: ERP Architecture and Fiscal Compliance (2024-2026)**

The commercialization of agricultural commodities in Argentina is governed by one of the most technologically advanced, highly regulated, and rapidly evolving fiscal frameworks in the global agribusiness sector. At the epicenter of this complex ecosystem is the *Liquidación Primaria de Granos* (LPG), an electronic settlement document that simultaneously acts as a binding commercial invoice, a terminal logistics tracking endpoint, and a comprehensive tax withholding certificate. For software engineering teams tasked with developing Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems for grain storage operators (*acopiadores*), mastering this framework requires a dual fluency in Byzantine Argentine tax law and the intricate web service architectures deployed by the *Agencia de Recaudación y Control Aduanero* (ARCA), the entity formerly known as AFIP.

This exhaustive research report delineates the complete legal, fiscal, and technical specifications necessary to architect a compliant, robust, and automated ERP module for the agricultural sector, spanning the regulatory landscape of 2024 through 2026\. By exploring the precise taxonomy of ARCA Forms 1116, the rigorous mathematics of quality deductions, the state-machine logic required for the *Sistema de Información Simplificado Agrícola* (SISA), the multifaceted provincial withholding regimes (*Ingresos Brutos*), and the precise SOAP-based interactions with the WSLPG API, this document serves as the definitive architectural blueprint for agribusiness software development in Argentina.

## **1\. The Legislative Continuum: From RG 3419 to ARCA 2026**

To architect a resilient software system, engineers must first understand the legislative scaffolding that dictates the underlying data models. The electronic primary grain liquidation system does not exist in a vacuum; rather, it is the culmination of a decade-long drive by the Argentine state toward absolute fiscal traceability, aiming to eliminate informality in the multi-billion-dollar grain export pipeline.

### **1.1 The Genesis of Electronic Traceability: General Resolution 3419/2012**

The cornerstone of the contemporary digital regime was established by AFIP General Resolution (RG) 3419/2012.1 Prior to this resolution, grain transactions were settled using pre-printed physical forms subject to manual auditing. RG 3419/2012 mandated the definitive shift to a strictly electronic regime for the commercialization of all grains, oilseeds, and dry legumes not destined for sowing.1

This regulatory shift fundamentally altered the operational reality of the *acopiador*. It introduced the mandatory requirement of synchronous API communication, stipulating that a transaction is completely invalid without the obtainment of a *Código de Operación Electrónico* (COE) directly from the tax authority's servers.2 Furthermore, RG 3419 established that all actors within the supply chain—ranging from the primary producers and transport logistics providers to the brokers and *acopiadores*—must be registered and actively enabled in the *Registro Único de Operadores de la Cadena Agroalimentaria* (RUCA).1 For ERP systems, this created the foundational requirement of maintaining strict master data governance, ensuring that the *Clave Única de Identificación Tributaria* (CUIT) of any party involved in a transaction is continuously validated against the RUCA and ARCA registries before any payload is generated.

### **1.2 Evolution and the Transition to ARCA (2024-2026)**

Throughout late 2024 and continuing into the 2025-2026 fiscal periods, the administrative transition from AFIP to the newly designated ARCA brought profound updates to the regulatory corpus.3 The state's objective during this period shifted from basic digitalization to algorithmic cross-referencing, linking physical transport data inextricably to financial settlements.

General Resolution 5558/2024, which entered into force in September 2024, significantly tightened the logistical traceability parameters.1 It explicitly linked the issuance of electronic liquidations to previously approved transport documents, ensuring that no grain could be financially settled if its physical movement had not been previously authorized by the state.

A critical turning point for compliance software was the subsequent promulgation of ARCA General Resolution 5687/2025. Effective May 6, 2025, this resolution drastically altered the operational timeline, establishing a strict twenty-day calendar deadline for the issuance of the primary certification following the definitive confirmation of the electronic *Carta de Porte* (Waybill).4 This temporal constraint forced ERP developers to implement sophisticated asynchronous monitoring systems to prevent regulatory gridlock. Furthermore, concurrent resolutions aimed at streamlining the tax burden, such as RG 5824/2026, introduced automated integrations linking these liquidations directly to pre-filled Value Added Tax (IVA) and Income Tax (*Ganancias*) declarations within the ARCA portals.6 This means that any mathematical discrepancy generated by a third-party ERP immediately triggers an automated fiscal alert at the federal level, demanding absolute precision in the software's calculation engine.

## **2\. Document Taxonomy: The 1116 Ecosystem**

The Argentine grain market relies on a highly specific taxonomy of electronic documents, collectively known within the industry as the "1116 Family." An ERP tailored for an independent *acopiador* must implement dedicated data models, specific user interface views, and disparate validation rules for each form type, as they represent fundamentally different legal, commercial, and fiscal acts.8 Understanding which party issues and which party receives these forms is critical for establishing the correct accounts payable, accounts receivable, and inventory ownership ledgers within the software.

### **2.1 Form 1116-A: Certificate of Deposit and Brokerage Settlement**

The Form 1116-A serves a dual purpose depending on the precise commercial context of the operation, but it primarily functions as a *Liquidación del Corredor* (Broker Settlement) or a formal Certificate of Deposit.

* **Commercial Context:** The agricultural supply chain often involves deferred pricing. When a primary producer harvests their crop, they may lack the on-farm silo capacity to store it. They transport the grain to an *acopiador* for physical storage without immediately establishing a sale price, a practice known as fixing the grain in deposit. In this scenario, the 1116-A is issued to legally acknowledge the custody of the specific tonnage and its quality parameters.9 Alternatively, if a broker (*corredor*) mediates a transaction, this form is used to liquidate their specific brokerage commission.  
* **ERP Implications for the Acopiador:** From a systems architecture perspective, the ERP must track the physical inventory under a 1116-A as a strict liability—this is third-party grain held in custody. The system must segregate this tonnage from the *acopiador's* proprietary assets. Crucially, a 1116-A acting purely as a certificate of deposit does not trigger the complex matrix of tax withholdings, because no monetary exchange for the underlying asset has occurred. The system only calculates the storage tariffs and potential broker fees.

### **2.2 Form 1116-C: Primary Purchase (Liquidación de Compra)**

Form 1116-C is the most critical and complex document for an independent *acopiador*. It represents the *Liquidación Primaria*—the direct, primary purchase of grain from the original producer.8

* **Commercial Context:** The *acopiador* is the active issuer of this form, presenting it to the producer. The 1116-C acts simultaneously as the legal commercial invoice for the producer's sale, the official receipt of the goods by the *acopiador*, and the federally recognized withholding certificate for all applicable national and provincial taxes.9  
* **ERP Implications for the Acopiador:** The ERP must execute its most computationally expensive operations when generating a 1116-C. The software must transition the grain in the database from "third-party custody" to "proprietary inventory." It must calculate the gross price, execute non-linear subtractions for quality deductions (*mermas*), apply service charges for drying and cleaning, calculate the complex IVA and *Ganancias* withholdings based on the producer's real-time SISA tier, query provincial databases for *Ingresos Brutos* rates, and ultimately request the COE via the WSLPG.10 This form creates a definitive Account Payable to the producer.

### **2.3 Form 1116-B: Secondary Sale (Liquidación de Venta)**

Form 1116-B represents a secondary market operation. It is utilized when an intermediary or *acopiador* sells the previously acquired proprietary grain to a final domestic buyer, such as an exporter, a crushing plant (*aceitera*), or a milling facility.8

* **Commercial Context:** Having successfully purchased the grain via a 1116-C, the *acopiador* now acts as the seller. In standard market dynamics, it is usually the buyer (the massive exporter) who issues the 1116-B to the *acopiador*, though the *acopiador* can theoretically issue it if the buyer has not already done so.9  
* **ERP Implications for the Acopiador:** When the *acopiador* receives a 1116-B, the tax logic of the ERP inverses. The *acopiador* is no longer the withholding agent; rather, they are the subject of withholdings applied by the exporter. The ERP must record the 1116-B as an Account Receivable. More importantly, the software must carefully parse the taxes withheld by the exporter and log them into the general ledger as fiscal credits (*saldos a favor*). These credits are vital for the *acopiador's* working capital, as they will be netted against the company's own monthly tax liabilities.11

| Form Designation | Issuer in an Acopiador Context | Primary Receiver | Core Commercial Function | Tax Withholding Dynamics | Inventory Impact for Acopiador |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Form 1116-A** | Acopiador / Broker | Producer | Deposit Acknowledgment / Brokerage Fee Settlement | Minimal (Applies only to Broker service fees) | Increases Third-Party Custody Inventory |
| **Form 1116-C** | Acopiador | Producer | Primary Purchase of Grain directly from the farm | High (Calculates and applies IVA, Ganancias, IIBB against producer) | Converts Custody Inventory to Proprietary Inventory |
| **Form 1116-B** | Exporter / Final Buyer | Acopiador | Secondary Sale of Grain to the export or industrial market | Applied by the Buyer against the Acopiador; recorded as Fiscal Credits | Decreases Proprietary Inventory |

## **3\. Architectural Payload: Mandatory Fields and Financial Structuring**

To successfully generate an electronic liquidation and obtain a COE, the ERP must construct a highly structured XML payload containing mandatory commercial, logistical, and fiscal data.9 The ARCA Web Service is unforgiving; the omission, malformation, or mathematical imbalance of any of these fields will result in a synchronous rejection, returning a specific error code.

### **3.1 Core Identity and Commercial Tracing Fields**

Every 1116-B and 1116-C must declare the unequivocal fiscal identity of the transacting parties using the 11-digit *Clave Única de Identificación Tributaria* (CUIT).12 The ERP database must ensure these CUITs are stripped of hyphens and perfectly formatted.

* **cuitComprador & cuitVendedor:** The fundamental buyer and seller identifiers. In an 1116-C, the *acopiador* is the cuitComprador and the producer is the cuitVendedor. The ERP must cross-reference these CUITs with the RUCA registry before transmission.  
* **cuitCorredor:** If a registered broker mediated the transaction, their CUIT must be included in this tag. If the operation was direct, the schema rules dictate whether this tag is omitted or passed as null.12  
* **codGrano & campania:** The system cannot use internal text strings for crop types (e.g., "Soja"). It must use a standardized ARCA integer code denoting the grain type (e.g., the specific integer for Soybean, Corn, or Wheat) and the harvest campaign year formatted as a string (e.g., "2526" representing the 2025/2026 harvest).12  
* **numeroContrato:** If the operation is tied to a forward contract previously registered in the official ARCA contract registry, the contract number must be supplied. When this tag is present, the ARCA servers perform a hard validation: the ERP must ensure that the CUITs and the grain code perfectly match the parameters of the registered contract, otherwise an error is returned.10

### **3.2 Quantitative and Quality Parameters**

The physical reality of the agricultural commodity must be mathematically represented in the payload. The ERP must process data originating from the weighbridge (bascula) and the quality laboratory.

* **Gross Weight (pesoBruto) & Net Weight (pesoNeto):** The system must record the total kilograms received. Precision is paramount; ARCA validates that the sum of the weights of the associated transport documents matches these totals.  
* **Quality Metrics:** Fields capturing moisture percentage (*humedad*), foreign matter (*zarandeo*), and broken grains must be recorded to justify the subsequent financial deductions.  
* **Contracted Price (precio):** The base price per ton agreed upon. This is usually expressed in Argentine Pesos, but if a foreign currency is used (e.g., USD), a declared exchange rate based on the Banco de la Nación Argentina must be included in the payload.13

### **3.3 The Financial Waterfall (Cascada de Liquidación)**

The financial payload of the 1116-C is not a simple multiplication of weight by price. It represents a strict waterfall of subtractions from the gross value, which the ERP must calculate with floating-point precision to avoid rounding errors that ARCA will reject.9

1. **Importe Bruto:** The initial calculation (Gross Weight × Contracted Price).  
2. **Deducciones (Deductions):** Subtractions for commercial discounts due to quality deficiencies (*Mermas*) and service charges levied by the *acopiador* (*Secada*, *Zarandeo*, *Flete*).  
3. **Subtotal:** Calculated strictly as Importe Bruto minus Deducciones.  
4. **Impuestos y Retenciones (Taxes and Withholdings):** The calculation of the base IVA on the grain, the standard IVA on the services provided, and the subsequent subtraction of all withholding taxes (*Retención IVA*, *Retención Ganancias*, *Retención IIBB*).  
5. **Importe Neto a Pagar (Net Amount Payable):** The final liquid cash value payable to the producer. This must equal the Subtotal, plus taxes, minus withholdings. This is the exact amount that will be transferred to the producer's standard bank account (CBU).9

## **4\. The Physics of Grain in ERP: Mermas and Quality Standardizations**

Agricultural commodities are living, breathing biological assets. Their weight and volume fluctuate heavily with environmental conditions. When a producer delivers grain directly from the harvester, it rarely matches the perfect commercial standard expected by exporters. Therefore, the ERP must feature a highly robust computation engine capable of executing non-linear deductions based on standardized tables managed by entities like the *Cámara Arbitral de Cereales* (e.g., the Bolsa de Comercio de Rosario or the Bolsa de Bahía Blanca).14

### **4.1 Merma por Humedad (Moisture Deduction)**

Grain delivered with moisture levels above the established commercial standard is subject to a *merma por secado* (shrinkage due to drying).15 This calculation is notoriously complex for junior developers because it is not a 1:1 linear deduction. Removing water through industrial dryers also reduces the physical volume and alters the weight density of the grain far beyond the mere weight of the water lost.

* **Mathematical Modeling:** The ERP must store and constantly update deduction matrices. For example, according to the standard tables for Soybeans, the base moisture standard is 13.5%. If the grain arrives at 14.5% moisture, the applied weight deduction is not 1.0%, but rather 1.72%.15 If it arrives at 16.0% moisture, the deduction escalates to 3.45%.15  
* **ERP Implementation Strategy:** Hardcoding these values into the source code is a severe architectural anti-pattern. The software should implement an administrative module where users can upload or update these threshold matrices via CSV or JSON, as the *Cámara Arbitral* occasionally revises these standard tables in response to evolving climatic realities.15

### **4.2 Merma por Manipuleo and Volátiles**

In addition to moisture, standard commercial practice within the Argentine market dictates mandatory deductions for the physical handling of the grain and the loss of volatile dust.

* **Manipuleo (Handling):** A flat addition of 0.25% is standardly applied to the calculated moisture deduction. This accounts for the physical loss of grain as it is moved through the augers, bucket elevators, and conveyors of the silo facility.15  
* **Volátil (Volatile Matter):** An additional 0.30% deduction is frequently calculated for the loss of volatile matter (dust and micro-particulates) during the aeration and storage process.18 The ERP must subtract these percentages directly from the gross weight before applying the financial price.

### **4.3 Secada and Zarandeo (Drying and Cleaning Tariffs)**

It is crucial to distinguish between a *merma* and a service tariff. While a *merma* reduces the payable physical weight of the grain, *secada* (the industrial drying process) and *zarandeo* (the mechanical sieving and cleaning of foreign matter) are financial service charges levied by the *acopiador* against the producer.

* **Technical Restraints in the Payload:** In the WSLPG XML payload, the financial charges for Secada and Zarandeo can be uniquely itemized per individual transport document (CTG) within the array.10 However, any other miscellaneous services must be summarized in a global serviciosOtros field.10 The total deduction formula strictly validated by ARCA is: Total \= (∑ importesSecado) \+ (∑ importesZarandeo) \+ serviciosOtros.10 If the ERP's internal subtotal does not perfectly match this aggregation, the API will reject the 1116-C.

## **5\. The WSLPG Ecosystem and SOAP Architecture**

The core technical hurdle in building an agricultural ERP is achieving seamless, fault-tolerant integration with the ARCA web services. Unlike standard retail or corporate billing which utilizes the generic WSFE or WSMTXCA APIs 19, the grain sector utilizes a dedicated, highly specialized service: the **Web Service Liquidación Primaria de Granos (WSLPG)**.20

### **5.1 WSDL Endpoints and Cryptographic Authentication**

The WSLPG operates on a standard Simple Object Access Protocol (SOAP) architecture. To communicate with the endpoints, the client application must first authenticate via the *Web Service de Autenticación y Autorización* (WSAA). The ERP must generate a cryptographic payload using an X.509 digital certificate assigned to the *acopiador's* CUIT, sign it, and exchange it for a temporary \<token\> and \<sign\> which must be injected into the header of every subsequent WSLPG request.10

The ERP architecture must support environment toggling between the following WSDL endpoints:

* **Homologation (Testing) Environment:** https://fwshomo.afip.gov.ar/wslpg/LpgService?wsdl.21 This environment requires pre-seeded test data provided by ARCA. Developers cannot simply use random CUITs; they must use specific testing CUITs to successfully simulate grain operations and avoid validation errors.22  
* **Production Environment:** https://serviciosjava.afip.gob.ar/wslpg/LpgService.22 (Note: While the institutional branding shifted from AFIP to ARCA in late 2024, legacy DNS routing under AFIP domains frequently persists in core production APIs through 2025 and 2026, requiring careful management of endpoint configurations).

### **5.2 Expansive API Methodology**

The WSLPG API offers a vast array of methods reflecting the complex lifecycle of agricultural contracts. Key methods exposed by the WSDL that the ERP must implement include 10:

* **autorizarLiquidacionPrimaria:** The primary method used to submit the 1116-C payload and obtain the electronic COE. Without the successful return of the COE, the liquidation has zero commercial, logistical, or fiscal validity.2  
* **ajustarLiquidacion:** Allows the modification of an already authorized liquidation via a credit or debit adjustment (*Ajuste*), essential for correcting post-settlement quality disputes.10  
* **anularLiquidacion:** Submits a request to completely void a liquidation that has already received a COE, typically used in cases of severe data entry errors prior to the financial cash settlement.10  
* **asociarLiquidacionAContrato:** Links a pre-existing primary liquidation to a formalized grain contract.10  
* **Query Methods (consultar...):** An expansive suite of GET-equivalent operations allowing the ERP to synchronize its internal state with ARCA. Methods exist to query liquidations by COE (lsgConsultarXCoeReq), by point of emission and order number, or to download parameter tables such as active campaigns, grain codes, enabled ports, and active deductibles.10

### **5.3 Exception Handling and Schema Validation**

The WSDL strictly enforces data types and lengths. When a request fails, the API responds with an arrayErrores object containing specific numeric codes and string descriptions detailing the business logic failure.10 For instance, a validation code of 1504 or 1858 indicates profound structural issues within the payload or mismatched fiscal states.10 Robust ERP systems must map these arcane ARCA error codes to user-friendly UI warnings, allowing the scale operator to correct the anomaly (e.g., mismatched CUITs, invalid SISA states, or mathematical imbalances between gross weight and deductions) without needing to parse raw XML.

## **6\. Logistics Integration: CTG and Cartas de Porte**

The issuance of an electronic primary liquidation is the final commercial step in a logistical chain that begins at the farm gate. The physical movement of grain across provincial borders is rigorously tracked via the electronic *Carta de Porte* and its accompanying *Código de Trazabilidad de Granos* (CTG).

### **6.1 Multi-to-One Relationships**

A common operational scenario involves a producer delivering multiple truckloads of soybeans over the course of a week, resulting in several individual *Cartas de Porte* and their respective CTGs. The ERP does not need to issue a separate, fragmented 1116-C for every single truck. The regulatory framework explicitly allows for a multi-to-one relationship: multiple CTGs can and should be aggregated into a single Form 1116-C to streamline the financial settlement.9

### **6.2 Data Reconciliation in the WSLPG Array**

When generating the XML payload for the WSLPG service, the ERP must provide an array containing the identifiers of all associated CTGs. The web service architecture is specifically designed to cross-reference these transport documents.12

* **Weight Validation Logic:** ARCA's servers will synchronously sum the weights of the individual CTGs declared in the state's transport database. The API will verify that this sum perfectly matches the total gross weight declared on the submitted 1116-C. A discrepancy of even a few kilograms will trigger an immediate rejection.  
* **Service Association:** As noted in the WSLPG version 1.21 documentation, specific post-harvest logistics services such as drying and cleaning can be mapped algorithmically to specific CTGs within the XML array, providing granular, truck-by-truck cost tracing for the producer's records.10

## **7\. The Taxation Engine: Federal and Provincial Retenciones**

The most sophisticated, computationally dense component of an agricultural ERP is the tax calculation engine. Argentina operates a deeply fragmented, multi-tiered withholding system encompassing federal value-added tax, federal income tax, and highly variable provincial gross income taxes. The ERP must act as an automated tax agent on behalf of the state.

### **7.1 Value Added Tax (IVA) and the SISA Regime (RG 2300\)**

The withholding of Value Added Tax on grains is governed by RG 2300 and is strictly modulated by the *Sistema de Información Simplificado Agrícola* (SISA).25 SISA is a real-time risk scoring matrix maintained by ARCA, designed to reward compliant taxpayers and punish evasive behavior. Before issuing an 1116-C, the ERP must query the producer's CUIT against the SISA database API to determine their risk tier (Estado 1, 2, or 3), which dynamically alters the withholding rate.25

* **Base IVA Rate:** The sale of standard agricultural grains is taxed at a reduced IVA rate of 10.5%.28  
* **Estado 1 (Low Risk):** For highly compliant producers, the *acopiador* withholds 5% of the gross value. ARCA automatically refunds this entire 5% directly to the producer's CBU within 45 days (though systemic delays of 35-40 days often occur in reality).25  
* **Estado 2 (Medium Risk):** The withholding jumps to 7%. The state refunds 6% to the producer, retaining 1%.25  
* **Estado 3 (High Risk):** A punitive withholding of 8% is applied. No automatic refund is issued; the producer must claim it through complex annual tax filings.25  
* **ERP Logic:** The system must calculate the base 10.5% IVA, subtract the SISA-determined withholding percentage, and allocate the remainder to the IVA Res AFIP 2300 field on the form, which represents the net IVA to be deposited directly into the producer's account.9

### **7.2 Impuesto a las Ganancias (Federal Income Tax \- RG 830\)**

Income tax withholding on grain sales is dictated by General Resolution 830\. Like IVA, the application of *Ganancias* is heavily influenced by the producer's SISA status.27

* **SISA Integration:** Producers categorized as "Estado 1" (Low Risk) are entirely exempt from *Ganancias* withholding on grain sales (0% aliquot).27 For those falling into Estado 2, a 2% rate is applied to the net taxable base.27  
* **General Rules:** For operators not covered by SISA exemptions, or acting outside standard primary parameters, the standard RG 830 rates apply (typically 28% for individuals and 25% for legal entities).30  
* **Minimum Non-Taxable Thresholds:** The regulation establishes minimum withholding amounts to prevent administrative friction on negligible sums. Historically, if the calculated retention was less than $240 ARS, it was discarded.29 However, due to hyperinflationary pressures through 2024 and 2025, ERP software must never hardcode these values. The ERP must treat all minimum thresholds (Monto no sujeto a retención) and minimum retention values as highly configurable environment variables that accountants can adjust dynamically.31

### **7.3 Impuesto sobre los Ingresos Brutos (Provincial IIBB)**

While IVA and *Ganancias* are federally standardized, the *Impuesto sobre los Ingresos Brutos* (IIBB) is administered independently by individual provinces. This requires the ERP to integrate with diverse regional tax APIs and download multiple monthly tax pads. The geographic origin of the grain, the fiscal domicile of the producer, and the location of the *acopio* trigger highly specific withholding rules.

* **Santa Fe (API Santa Fe):** In 2025 and moving into 2026, the province of Santa Fe modernized its IIBB landscape via RG 37/2025 and RG 28/2026, introducing the mandatory *Padrón de Alícuotas de Retención y Percepción* (PARP).33 An *acopiador* must act as a withholding agent if their revenues exceed $3,000,000,000 ARS in Santa Fe or $3,500,000,000 ARS nationally.33 The ERP must feature a module to download and parse the monthly PARP database. For producers not found in the PARP, the software must apply a punitive default retention rate of 5%.33 Furthermore, specific minimum non-taxable bases exist that the software must evaluate before applying the rate (e.g., $650,000 ARS for general operations).33  
* **Córdoba (Dirección General de Rentas):** Governed by the annual tax laws (such as Ley 11016 for 2025 and Ley 11090 for 2026), Rentas Córdoba requires the verification of a *Listado Único de Alícuotas* (LUA) certificate.35 The ERP must prompt the operator to verify this status. If a producer fails to provide a valid LUA, the ERP must default to a maximum withholding rate of 6.5%.37 Standard aliquots for compliant subjects typically range between 4.75% and 6.50% depending on the precise activity classification.36  
* **Buenos Aires (ARBA):** ARBA dictates retentions based on a centrally managed registry queried via CUIT and *Clave de Identificación Tributaria* (CIT).38 The software must ping ARBA's servers monthly, process the massive text files, and update the specific aliquot applied to each producer's profile to avoid under-withholding penalties.  
* **Entre Ríos (ATER):** Governed by shifting resolutions and decrees (such as Decree 301/2026), ATER establishes highly specific tax minimums and rates for primary and wholesale activities.39 For instance, certain activities trigger a minimum annual threshold of $144,000 ARS, while others demand $288,000 ARS, alongside specific percentage rates like 0.20% for particular wholesale distributions.40 The ERP must feature an ATER-specific configuration matrix to handle these distinct categorical minimums and align with the ATER monthly expiration calendar.42

## **8\. Operational Timelines and the 20-Day Rule**

In the pursuit of real-time fiscal control, ARCA enforces strict operational deadlines that the ERP software must proactively monitor. A system that relies purely on manual human memory to issue liquidations will inevitably fail compliance audits.

### **8.1 The Strict 20-Day Limit (RG 5687/2025)**

Introduced under the modifications of ARCA General Resolution 5687/2025 and strictly effective from May 6, 2025, the *acopiador* is legally bound to a narrow window of twenty (20) calendar days to issue the primary certification (the 1116-C).2

* **Start of the Clock:** Crucially for system design, the 20-day countdown begins not at the physical reception of the grain at the weighbridge, but upon the exact timestamp of the "confirmación definitiva" (definitive confirmation) of the electronic Carta de Porte within the ARCA systems.2  
* **ERP Architectural Implementation:** This temporal constraint requires the ERP to implement asynchronous cron jobs or background worker processes that continuously monitor the age of confirmed *Cartas de Porte* residing in the *acopio's* virtual inventory. The software must generate automated dashboard alerts for operators on day 15, and forcefully trigger draft liquidations before day 20\. Failure to issue the liquidation within this timeframe results in regulatory gridlock, triggering the suspension of the *acopiador's* RUCA registration and inviting heavy financial penalties under Law 11.683.2

### **8.2 System Outages and Idempotency**

Acknowledging that internet infrastructure in rural Argentina or ARCA's own servers may face downtime, the legislation (specifically Article 12 of the framework) stipulates that if systems are inoperative, the delayed procedures must be completed within 24 hours of the service's restoration.2 For ERP architects, this mandates the use of queue-based asynchronous processing and idempotent API requests. The system must ensure that a timed-out request for a COE does not accidentally result in duplicate liquidations when the network eventually recovers.

## **9\. Ecosystem Duality: Factura Electrónica vs. Formulario 1116**

A frequent source of architectural confusion in agricultural ERP design is the interaction between standard electronic invoices (*Facturas Electrónicas* A, B, or C) and the Formulario 1116 ecosystem. An ERP must support both, but with strict logical firewalls between them.

### **9.1 The 1116 as the Ultimate Commercial Invoice**

Under General Resolution 1415/2003 and its subsequent modifications, the *Liquidación Primaria de Granos* (Form 1116-C) legally replaces and acts as the ultimate official invoice for the transaction of the grain itself.2 The *acopiador* **does not** and **must not** issue a standard ARCA Factura A or B for the purchase of the grain. The 1116-C satisfies all VAT and Income Tax documentation requirements for the physical commodity and is the sole document required to legitimize the movement of funds to the producer.

### **9.2 The Necessary Role of the Factura Electrónica for Acopiadores**

However, a modern *acopiador* is a multifaceted business entity that provides goods and services extending far beyond the mere storage of grain. The ERP must seamlessly support standard invoicing (via WSMTXCA or WSFE APIs) alongside the WSLPG API for the following distinct operational scenarios 19:

1. **Direct Billing of Services:** If the *acopiador* provides freight transport, industrial drying, or long-term storage services that are *not* deducted directly from a grain settlement (for instance, if the producer wishes to pay for the services in cash to preserve their grain inventory), the *acopiador* must issue a standard Factura A to the producer.43  
2. **Sale of Agricultural Inputs (Canje):** The sale of seeds, fertilizers, and agrochemicals by the *acopiador* to the producer requires standard electronic invoicing.13 In a *canje* (barter) operation, the ERP must be able to offset the value of the Factura A for the fertilizer against the value of the 1116-C for the grain.  
3. **Duality of Deductions:** While services like *secada* and *zarandeo* can be deducted internally within the financial waterfall of the 1116-C (thus avoiding the need for a separate invoice), this is only permissible if the cost is subtracted from the grain value.10 The ERP logic must enforce a rule: if a service is billed on a Factura, it cannot be listed as a deduction on the 1116-C, and vice versa, preventing double taxation and duplicate VAT declarations.

## **10\. Conclusion and Architectural Imperatives**

Designing ERP software for the Argentine grain sector is fundamentally an exercise in modeling a high-velocity, highly regulated fiscal environment. The ecosystem surrounding the *Liquidaciones Primarias de Granos* (Forms 1116-A, 1116-B, and 1116-C) demands a software architecture that is simultaneously mathematically precise, capable of handling complex biological deduction tables, and highly adaptable to continuous regulatory shocks.

The transition from AFIP to ARCA throughout 2024-2026 has introduced compressed timelines, notably the strict 20-day limit for liquidation issuance dictated by RG 5687/2025. Software must transcend simple CRUD (Create, Read, Update, Delete) operations. It must embrace automated background monitoring, dynamic SISA API verifications, robust local caching of volatile provincial IIBB tax pads (such as Santa Fe's PARP or Córdoba's LUA), and fault-tolerant SOAP communications with the WSLPG. By treating tax rates, deduction thresholds, and WSDL endpoints as configurable microservices rather than hardcoded logic, software architects can ensure that *acopiadores* maintain compliance, secure their vital RUCA standing, and achieve frictionless commerce in the complex landscape of Argentine agribusiness.
