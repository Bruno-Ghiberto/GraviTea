# **Architectural and Regulatory Framework of the Sistema de Información Simplificado Agrícola (SISA): Comprehensive Guide for Agri-Business ERP Development**

## **Introduction: The Paradigm Shift in Agricultural Fiscal Control**

The agricultural sector in Argentina operates under one of the most rigorously enforced, technologically integrated, and systemically complex tax and regulatory regimes in the world. For software architects, database administrators, and developers tasked with engineering Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems tailored to the grain supply chain—specifically focusing on the operations of grain collectors, elevators, and aggregators known as *acopiadores*—a granular, uncompromising understanding of this framework is not merely a legal or compliance requirement. It serves as the foundational blueprint for the entirety of the software’s underlying business logic, database schema, and integration middleware.

Historically, the fiscal oversight of grain operators was managed through the Registro Fiscal de Operadores de Granos (RFOG), an older framework originally established and governed by the Administración Federal de Ingresos Públicos (AFIP) under General Resolution (RG) 2300\. This legacy registry relied heavily on segmented databases, asynchronous manual oversight, and a high volume of physical documentation, most notably the traditional Form 1116 used for primary and secondary grain settlements. However, a systemic paradigm shift occurred with the issuance of Joint General Resolution 4248/2018, promulgated collaboratively by the Ministry of Agroindustry, the National Service for Agri-Food Health and Quality (SENASA), the National Seed Institute (INASE), and AFIP.1 This sweeping resolution formally and permanently abrogated RG 2300, instituting in its place the Sistema de Información Simplificado Agrícola (SISA).1 SISA was designed from its inception to be a unified, systemic, and highly automated registry capable of cross-referencing production yields, physical stockpiling, and commercialization data in absolute real-time.

By the 2025-2026 fiscal periods, the institutional and regulatory landscape underwent further dramatic transformation, reflecting a broader modernization of the Argentine state apparatus. The most visible manifestation of this shift was the organizational restructuring of AFIP into the Agencia de Recaudación y Control Aduanero (ARCA). Under ARCA’s administration, the regulatory framework has been further modernized to aggressively eliminate legacy physical procedures, enforcing real-time systemic tracking of physical grain movements. A pivotal, architectural update in this modern era is General Resolution 5689/2025, which wholly replaced and updated the previous grain registration regimes, definitively abrogating the transitional RG 3593\.2

For an ERP system, the most critical architectural implication of RG 5689 is the strict, algorithmic enforcement of the "Inventory Precedence Rule." The legislation mandates that any movement, transfer, or commercialization of grains must logically and chronologically be preceded by an explicitly registered existence or a prior entry of the exact same grain type, volume, and harvest campaign in the corresponding physical plant.2 Failure to adhere to this chronological and quantitative precedence immediately restricts the operator's ability to execute systemic movements within the registry.2 Consequently, modern ERP software cannot function merely as a post-facto financial accounting tool or a disconnected inventory ledger. It must operate as a preemptive, real-time control system that maintains continuous two-way synchronization with ARCA’s central databases to prevent operational gridlock at the physical weighbridge or during the dispatch of transport vehicles.

## **Typology and Categorization of Operators in the SISA Ecosystem**

The SISA framework classifies participants in the agricultural supply chain into distinct, highly regulated categories based on their underlying economic activities, the nature of their physical infrastructure, and their specific role in the transaction lifecycle. An ERP must strictly map its Vendor, Customer, and internal Company Master Data to these exact taxonomies, as the assigned category directly dictates the systemic behavior of the software regarding allowable document issuance, required web service endpoints, and tax calculation logic.

The primary bifurcation within the SISA registry architecture distinguishes between "Productores" (Producers) and "Operadores" (Operators).4 Producers are defined as entities or individuals who physically cultivate and harvest grains on owned or leased land. They sell their own production and act as the primary originators of the grain supply chain. Operators, conversely, encompass all intermediaries, aggregators, processors, and exporters who purchase, store, transform, or broker these grains.4

Within the macro-category of Operators, the registry recognizes a highly specialized array of sub-classifications, including the *acopiador* (collector or rural elevator), the *corredor* (broker or commission agent), the *canjeador* (barter operator facilitating seed/chemical exchanges for grain), the *industrial* (processor, crusher, or miller), and the *exportador* (exporter).5 The specific obligations, tax treatment, and systemic permissions of each sub-category are detailed extensively in the SISA registry and were originally mapped and migrated from the defunct Registro Único de Operadores de la Cadena Agroindustrial (RUCA).7

An independent *acopiador* functions as the primary aggregator in the physical supply chain. Their business model revolves around purchasing grain from multiple independent producers, storing it in localized silo facilities, conditioning it through drying and cleaning processes, and subsequently selling aggregated volumes to exporters or industrial processors at port terminals. Under the SISA regime, an independent acopiador must register under the specific operator category designated for grain stockpiling, which inherently requires the possession and formal registration of physical infrastructure.7

A critical operational dependency for an acopiador is this physical plant registration. According to Joint General Resolution 5673 (promulgated by SAGyP and ARCA), operators utilizing physical plants for the storage, processing, and industrialization of grains and grain derivatives must register and obtain authorization for these facilities directly within the SISA portal.7 The application process for new plants, modifications to existing capacities, or reactivations of dormant facilities is subjected to a rigorous 45-day technical review period by the Secretariat of Agriculture, Livestock, and Fisheries.7 During this period, the National Directorate of Agro-Commercial Control holds the authority to request additional operational documentation.7

For ERP architecture and database design, this regulatory reality dictates that the software must support a multi-layered relational data model for the acopiador entity itself, as well as for its commercial counterparties. The ERP must record not merely the legal entity's national tax identification number (CUIT) but must also maintain a normalized table of the unique SISA plant identifiers associated with that specific entity. When executing a Liquidación Primaria de Granos (LPG) to settle a purchase, or when issuing an electronic Carta de Porte to authorize a truck's movement, the ERP must algorithmically inject the precise, ARCA-validated plant authorization code representing where the grain is physically located or where it is destined. Furthermore, if an operator purchases grains strictly for their own consumption without operating a commercial plant—a specific category modified by Resolution 69/2024 of the former Secretariat of Bioeconomy—they are exempt from certain systemic movement registries but must explicitly declare their self-consumption status in SISA, a variable the ERP must track to bypass unnecessary API validations.7

## **Algorithmic Implementation of Tax Withholding Matrices**

The core financial and legal responsibility of an ERP tailored for an acopiador is the autonomous, error-free calculation of tax withholdings during the financial settlement process. In the Argentine jurisdiction, acopiadores are legally deputized to act as mandated retention agents for both the Impuesto al Valor Agregado (IVA \- Value Added Tax) and the Impuesto a las Ganancias (Income Tax).8 The withholding rates are entirely dynamic; they are not static variables that can be hard-coded into the software. Instead, they are continuously determined by ARCA’s advanced risk profiling algorithms, which assign a specific "State" (Estado) to every registered entity based on their real-time compliance posture.

The SISA scoring system categorizes entities into three active risk states and one inactive state, which form the basis of all ERP financial logic.4 State 1 represents Low Risk (Bajo Riesgo) and rewards compliant taxpayers with minimal or zero withholdings. State 2 denotes Medium Risk (Mediano Riesgo), applying moderate retentions. State 3 indicates High Risk (Alto Riesgo), subjecting the taxpayer to heavy financial retentions. Finally, the "Inactivo" state applies to suspended, excluded, or unregistered entities, triggering absolute maximum punitive withholding rates.4

### **Value Added Tax (IVA) Withholding Logic**

The IVA withholding matrix requires the ERP to evaluate a combination of three distinct variables before executing the final mathematical calculation: the real-time SISA State of the seller, the specific role of the seller in the transaction (Producer versus Operator), and the specific botanical classification of the grain being transacted (Rice versus all other Cereals, Oilseeds, and Legumes).4

The baseline mathematical formulation for the withheld amount is calculated by applying the dynamically determined rate to the net sales price resulting from the corresponding settlement document. The rates to be applied by the ERP, as strictly stipulated by ARCA documentation 4, must be implemented according to the following matrix:

| Real-Time SISA State | Validated Seller Role | Specific Commodity Type | Applicable IVA Withholding Rate |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Estado 1** | Producer or Operator | Cereals, Oilseeds, Legumes (excl. Rice) | **5%** |
| **Estado 1** | Producer or Operator | Rice | **10%** |
| **Estado 2** | Operator | Cereals, Oilseeds, Legumes (excl. Rice) | **5%** |
| **Estado 2** | Producer | Cereals, Oilseeds, Legumes (excl. Rice) | **7%** |
| **Estado 2** | Operator | Rice | **10%** |
| **Estado 2** | Producer | Rice | **14%** |
| **Estado 3** | Producer or Operator | Cereals, Oilseeds, Legumes (excl. Rice) | **8%** |
| **Estado 3** | Producer or Operator | Rice | **16%** |
| **Inactivo / Excluded** | Producer or Operator | All Grain Types | **100% of applicable IVA** |

Several critical architectural considerations arise from this matrix. First, the regulatory framework explicitly dictates a principle of non-compensability. Acquirers, acting as retention agents, cannot under any circumstances compensate these withheld funds against their own fiscal tax credits; they must be deposited directly and entirely to ARCA's treasury.10 Therefore, the ERP's Financial and Treasury modules must be programmed to automatically segregate these retained funds into an untouchable payable liability account the millisecond the settlement is authorized.

Second, the ERP must possess specialized logic for operations intermediated by grain futures markets, commonly known as *mercados de cereales a término*. In these specific contractual structures, the withholding is determined not by applying the applicable rate to the gross transaction value, but specifically to the adjustment price (*precio de ajuste*).4 The software must differentiate between physical spot contracts and futures adjustments to apply the correct base for the retention calculation.

### **Income Tax (Impuesto a las Ganancias) Withholding Logic**

Simultaneous and parallel to the IVA calculation, the ERP engine must calculate the Impuesto a las Ganancias withholding. This specific regime applies broadly to the payment of all sales operations involving grains, seeds in the certification process, and dry legumes. Crucially, the mathematical base for this withholding is inclusive of any subsequent financial adjustments, accumulated interests, monetary updates, and other associated concepts consigned in the primary invoice or equivalent document.9 Furthermore, the legislation strictly mandates that this retention regime applies to all commissions, brokerages, or other remunerations derived from the activity of grain brokers (*corredores de granos*), regardless of whether they are invoiced together with the grain or separately.9

Unlike the IVA matrix, the Ganancias withholding rates are less concerned with the specific commodity type and are solely dependent on the combination of the counterparty's SISA state and their foundational tax inscription status.9 The ERP must query both data points to apply the correct rate:

| Counterparty SISA State and Taxpayer Status | Applicable Ganancias Withholding Rate |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Estado 1 (Low Risk)** | **0%** (Complete exemption from retention) |
| **Estado 2 (Medium Risk)** | **2%** |
| **Estado 3 (High Risk)** | **15%** |
| **Inactivos (Registered in tax, but Inactive in SISA)** | **28%** |
| **Unregistered (No formal tax inscription)** | Punitive rates apply; operations severely restricted. |

A vital architectural warning for ERP designers involves the operational status of the acopiador themselves. If an acopiador drops out of the SISA registry, becoming excluded, suspended, or inactive, they immediately lose their legal authorization and capacity to act as a retention agent for Impuesto a las Ganancias in the payment of primary grain purchase operations.8 Therefore, the ERP must continuously monitor the acopiador's *own* SISA status through internal API polling. If the system detects that the host company has suffered an exclusion, it must immediately and automatically disable the automated retention generation logic. Continuing to issue retentions while excluded results in the generation of legally invalid tax certificates, exposing the company to severe fiscal penalties. Furthermore, if the excluded acopiador utilizes the services of independent brokers to move their grain, the commissions paid by the acopiador to those brokers will suffer a punitive 15% retention instead of the standard 2% applicable to compliant operators.8 The ERP’s accounts payable module must foresee this margin compression.

## **Operational and Financial Repercussions of SISA Exclusion**

Operating outside the boundaries of the SISA registry—whether due to an initial failure to register, a systemic expulsion for non-compliance, or falling into the dreaded "Inactivo" state—triggers immediate and cascading commercial, financial, and logistical catastrophes. An ERP designed for the grain sector must transcend basic data entry; it must implement stringent internal alerts, predictive dashboards, and hard transactional locks to prevent the acopiador from inadvertently breaching compliance parameters that could lead to registry exclusion.

The most immediate and devastating consequence of an exclusion or suspension from the registry is the systemic restriction placed on the issuance of Electronic Waybills, officially termed the Cartas de Porte Electrónicas. The Carta de Porte is the indispensable digital document required to legally move physical grain on any public road in Argentina. When an operator is excluded, ARCA's systems impose draconian partial authorizations or absolute numerical limits on the quantity of Cartas de Porte the excluded operator can request.8 This algorithmic restriction effectively paralyses the logistical capacity of the acopiador, rendering them unable to transport their proprietary, paid-for stock from their rural silos to the export port terminals or industrial crushing plants, halting their revenue cycle entirely.8

Furthermore, the excluded acopiador faces severe, system-enforced limitations in acquiring and issuing the mandatory primary settlement documents. Historically known as Forms 1116/B or 1116/C, these are now handled entirely via the systemic Liquidación Primaria de Granos (LPG).8 To document primary purchase or consignment operations while excluded, the acopiador is forcibly relegated to utilizing third-party brokerage firms to intermediate and document the transactions on their behalf.8 This forced reliance on brokers strips the acopiador of their commercial independence and incurs heavy, entirely unnecessary commission costs that destroy operational margins.

Financially, an excluded operator is subjected to the absolute maximum withholding rates, resulting in rapid and severe cash flow strangulation. As detailed in the tax matrices, an entity excluded from SISA suffers a total retention of the Value Added Tax. Because the standard IVA rate for most primary grains is 10.5%, the 100% retention rate effectively strips the seller of all IVA fiscal credits at the precise point of sale, capturing the entire tax amount in ARCA's treasury immediately.4 Concurrently, the Income Tax withholding spikes violently from the standard 2% (or the 0% benefit enjoyed by State 1 operators) to an exorbitant 15% or 28%, depending on the specific nature of the infraction and the operator's underlying tax inscription status.8

The ERP's financial forecasting, treasury management, and cash-flow projection modules must be mathematically capable of modeling these punitive rates. If an acopiador is dealing with a producer or counterparty who has fallen into Estado 3 or the Inactivo state, the ERP must calculate the final payable amount accurately. Failure to do so could result in the acopiador overpaying the non-compliant producer, inadvertently assuming the producer's massive tax liability out of their own working capital.

## **The Paradigm of Continuous Certification and Compliance Lifecycles**

Unlike legacy licensing models that required a static, annual paper renewal, SISA is not a static registry; it is a highly dynamic, behavior-based ecosystem. The concept of "renewal" has been entirely replaced by "continuous systemic certification." The risk "State" or scoring assigned to a producer or operator is continuously recalculated by ARCA's background algorithms based on the entity's fiscal behavior, their debt profile, and their strict, chronological adherence to mandatory informative regimes. For an ERP, automating the tracking, validation, and submission of these informative regimes represents one of the highest-value features a developer can build.

The absolute cornerstone of SISA's continuous certification mechanism for producers—and for operators who also engage in primary production—is the "Módulo Información Productiva" (Productive Information Module). Entities are legally obligated to declare their planted surface area, their specific crop types, their geolocated land parcels, and their expected yields during highly specific statutory windows. Historically, these windows were rigidly defined as IP1 (running from September 1 to October 31\) and IP2 (running from December 1 to the end of February).11

However, regulatory updates have shifted these timelines to better reflect agronomic realities. Recent chronograms established that IP1 must be submitted from November 1 to April 30 of the following year, while IP2 must be submitted from March 1 to April 30\.12 Regardless of the specific seasonal dates—which the ERP must treat as highly configurable parameters capable of being updated dynamically via ARCA's web services or rapid manual configuration patches—the consequences of non-compliance are rigid and unforgiving. Total or partial failure to comply with the Productive Information regime explicitly and immediately blocks the systemic registration of all grain contracts for that entity.11 If the ERP attempts to submit a purchase contract to ARCA via the relevant web service for a producer who has missed their IP deadline, the transaction will be systematically rejected, halting the commercial operation and preventing the subsequent issuance of the LPG.

Recognizing the complex administrative burden placed on operators, and aiming to foster voluntary compliance rather than immediate punishment, ARCA introduced vital flexibilities to the scoring system via General Resolution 5748/2025.13 Under this updated framework, if ARCA's algorithms detect "formal breaches" (incumplimientos de tipo formal)—such as a late informative filing—the taxpayer is not immediately downgraded to a higher-risk state with punitive withholdings. Instead, they are algorithmically granted an additional grace period of 45 consecutive calendar days.13 This grace period is added to the original timeframe indicated in the induction communication issued by the agency, allowing the operator a window to regularize their situation before a new, penalized state is officially assigned.13

An advanced, sector-specific ERP must feature a proactive compliance dashboard that tracks these 45-day grace periods with precision. By continuously querying ARCA's APIs, the software can generate automated alerts to the acopiador's administrative staff, notifying them that a key supplier, a crucial counterparty, or the acopiador themselves is operating within a grace period. This allows for preemptive regularization before the scoring drop triggers catastrophic withholding rates and systemic contract blockages.

## **Web Service Orchestration and Cryptographic Middleware Architecture**

To operate seamlessly within the SISA environment and comply with ARCA's mandates, an ERP must be engineered with a highly robust middleware layer capable of orchestrating complex synchronous and asynchronous communications with the agency's Simple Object Access Protocol (SOAP) Web Services.14 The architecture demands stringent cryptographic security, rigorous XML schema validation, comprehensive error handling, and the ability to parse complex programmatic responses.

### **Authentication via the WSAA Framework**

Access to any of ARCA's business web services (known internally as Web Services de Negocio or WSN) is centralized and heavily gated through the Web Service de Autenticación y Autorización (WSAA).15 An ERP cannot simply transmit a static username, password, or API key to access fiscal data. Instead, it must execute a complex cryptographic handshake utilizing X.509 digital certificates issued directly by ARCA acting as the root Certifying Authority.16

The authentication workflow that the ERP's middleware must automate involves several precise steps. First, the ERP generates a Ticket de Requerimiento de Acceso (TRA), which is an XML structure specifying the target service the ERP wishes to access (for example, ws\_sr\_padron\_a4 for registry queries, or wsliquigranos for settlements). Second, the TRA is cryptographically signed using the acopiador's private key, generating a PKCS\#7 / CMS (Cryptographic Message Syntax) signature block conforming to strict S/MIME standards.16 Third, this signed payload is transmitted via HTTPS to the WSAA endpoint.

Upon successful validation of the digital certificate and the mathematical integrity of the signature, the WSAA returns a Ticket de Acceso (TA) in XML format. The ERP's XML parsing engine must extract two critical string values from this response: the \<token\> and the \<sign\>.17 Crucially, the TA is not permanent; it is valid for a strictly limited maximum duration of 12 hours.16 The ERP must therefore implement an intelligent caching strategy, storing the Token and Sign in secure memory or an encrypted database table, and reusing them for all subsequent business API calls until expiration. Once expired, the software must autonomously and transparently re-initiate the entire WSAA cryptographic handshake without interrupting the user's workflow.

### **Real-time Counterparty Validation: ws\_sr\_padron\_a4**

Before an acopiador executes any commercial operation—be it drafting a forward contract or weighing a truck at the scale—the ERP must dynamically validate the counterparty’s SISA state, active categories, and tax statuses. This is achieved by invoking the "Consulta a Padrón Alcance 4" web service (ws\_sr\_padron\_a4).17

The ERP middleware constructs a SOAP envelope calling the getPersona method.17 The XML payload requires the injected \<token\> and \<sign\> retrieved from the WSAA cache, the \<cuitRepresentada\> (representing the acopiador's own CUIT), and the \<idPersona\> (the target counterparty's CUIT).17

The XML response provided by ARCA is exhaustively detailed, containing the complete fiscal profile of the requested CUIT. The ERP's parsing engine must traverse this complex response to algorithmically map the counterparty's specific tax inscriptions (ensuring they are active in Value Added Tax and Income Tax), their authorized economic activities according to the official nomenclator, and most crucially, their current SISA State (1, 2, 3, or inactive). Because the SISA state dynamically and directly dictates the financial withholding rates 4, the ERP's architecture should mandate that this query is executed at the exact moment of contract creation, and again precisely before the final issuance of the settlement document. This dual-verification ensures that no scoring downgrades or registry exclusions have occurred in the interim period between contracting and physical delivery.

In the event of localized network failures or API outages on ARCA's servers, the ERP should provide administrators with access to manual validation fallbacks, such as the Consultas web portals, where authorized personnel can manually verify statuses using their Clave Fiscal, though the systemic integration remains the operational standard.20

### **Settlement Orchestration: The WSLiquiGranos Service**

The most mission-critical web service integration for an acopiador's ERP is the WSLiquiGranos (Liquidación Primaria y Secundaria de Granos).21 This highly complex service entirely replaces the legacy manual Form 1116, serving as the sole, legally authorized documentary backing for primary grain operations where the seller legally acts as an agricultural producer.22

The ERP must interface with this specific web service to obtain the Código de Operación Electrónico (COE), a unique, cryptographically generated alphanumeric string that definitively validates the settlement in the eyes of the state.21 The operational scope of WSLiquiGranos that the ERP must support encompasses several distinct methods 21:

1. **Autorizar Liquidación Primaria/Secundaria**: The ERP transmits the comprehensive settlement details—including buyer CUIT, seller CUIT, exact grain type codes, gross weight, shrinkage and quality deductions, and the mathematically calculated tax withholdings—to obtain the initial COE.  
2. **Ajustar Liquidación**: If a quality dispute arises post-delivery, or a market price adjustment is required, the ERP cannot simply delete or overwrite the local database record. It must invoke the systemic adjustment methods (Ajuste de Crédito or Ajuste de Débito) referencing the original COE. For instance, to completely revert an operation on a previously settled certificate, the ERP must automatically generate and transmit a "LPG Ajuste de Crédito" containing identical data to the original, thereby neutralizing it systemically.22  
3. **Anular Liquidación**: This method allows for the outright cancellation of a document with an assigned COE, but it is subject to exceedingly strict temporal constraints defined by ARCA.  
4. **Asociar a Contrato**: The ERP must seamlessly link the finalized settlement to a previously registered forward or spot grain contract, a necessary step for ensuring total physical and financial traceability within the SISA ecosystem.  
5. **Consultas Paramétricas**: To prevent schema validation errors, the ERP must periodically poll the web service's synchronization methods to update its internal database dictionaries regarding active grain codes, valid historical harvest campaigns, enabled port terminals, and certified deduction/retention types.21

## **Advanced Master Data Management and Database Schema Design**

To seamlessly integrate with the rigorous and uncompromising demands of SISA, ARCA's web services, and the mathematically complex withholding matrices, the underlying relational database (or document schema) of the ERP must be meticulously structured. A rudimentary or flattened Vendor/Customer table is entirely insufficient and will lead to systemic compliance failures.

Below is an exhaustive technical specification for the critical data entities an acopiador ERP must maintain to survive in this regulatory environment:

### **1\. Master Data: Business Contacts and Counterparties**

Every producer, broker, logistics provider, and buyer must be tracked with absolute temporal accuracy, acknowledging that a contact's compliance state changes over time.

* **Tax Identification (CUIT)**: This serves as the primary key for all API queries. The database logic must enforce local mod-11 validation algorithms to prevent user typographical errors before wasting network resources querying ARCA.  
* **SISA State (estado\_sisa)**: Stored as an Integer (1, 2, 3\) or Enum (Inactive, Excluded, Suspended).  
* **State Timestamp (fecha\_actualizacion\_sisa)**: A critical DateTime field for auditability. The ERP must definitively know *when* the state was last verified via ws\_sr\_padron\_a4. If the timestamp is older than a configurable threshold (e.g., 24 hours), the system should implement a hard lock, forcing a background API refresh before permitting any financial transaction or physical dispatch.  
* **Role Classification (rol\_sisa)**: Enum (Productor\_Vendedor, Operador\_Vendedor, Comprador\_Consumo\_Propio). As demonstrated in the IVA matrices, a State 2 Producer suffers a different withholding rate (7%) compared to a State 2 Operator (5%).4 The ERP cannot calculate the tax without knowing this role definitively.  
* **Tax Inscriptions**: Boolean flags for is\_inscripto\_iva and is\_inscripto\_ganancias. These are absolutely required to determine if the punitive unregistered rates (e.g., the 28% for Ganancias) apply to the specific transaction.9

### **2\. Master Data: Infrastructure, Plants, and Spatial Data**

Because Joint RG 5673 dictates that physical infrastructure authorization dictates systemic capabilities 7, spatial data tracking is paramount.

* **Plant Identifier (codigo\_planta\_sisa)**: The numeric ID migrated from the old RUCA system or freshly assigned by SISA.7 A counterparty may have multiple active plants; the ERP must link the specific plant to the specific transaction.  
* **Plant Status (estado\_planta)**: Tracked as Active, Inactive, or Inhabilitada.  
* **RENSPA Code**: The Registro Nacional Sanitario de Productores Agropecuarios ID, which is strictly required for linking rural origin points on the electronic Cartas de Porte.

### **3\. Transactional Data: Liquidaciones (Grain Settlements)**

The table structure representing a Liquidación Primaria de Granos (LPG) must natively mirror the deeply nested XML schema demanded by the WSLiquiGranos endpoint.21

* **Base Document Linking**: Mandatory foreign keys referencing the specific *Carta de Porte* and the physical *Ticket de Pesada* (Scale Ticket). This enforces RG 5689's prime directive: no systemic movement without prior physical existence.2 The LPG must be cryptographically tied back to a verified physical intake at the weighbridge.  
* **Gross and Net Values**: Tracking Kg Brutos, Mermas (Shrinkage deductions for moisture and volatile matter), and the final Kg Netos used for financial calculation.  
* **Financials**: Recording the Precio de Referencia (Base Price), the Precio de Ajuste (if dealing with a futures market structure) 4, and the Subtotal.  
* **Withholding Sub-Ledger**: A highly normalized one-to-many relationship linking the parent LPG to multiple tax retention lines.  
  * Retention\_Type (e.g., IVA, Ganancias).  
  * Applied\_Rate (e.g., 5.00, 15.00).  
  * Base\_Imponible (The exact monetary subtotal the rate was applied against).  
  * Amount\_Withheld.  
* **ARCA Certification Data**: Storing the COE\_Number (String, returned synchronously by ARCA) and the COE\_Date (DateTime). If the COE field is null, the document is legally non-existent, and the ERP's internal state machine must block any integration with the general ledger or accounts payable modules.  
* **Adjustments Ledger**: If an LPG is modified, the ERP must track the Original\_COE and link it chronologically to the new Ajuste\_Credito\_COE to maintain a perfect, auditor-ready trail of the reversal.22

### **4\. Compliance Engines and Cryptographic Audit Logs**

* **Informative Deadlines**: Timestamps for IP1 and IP2 deadlines linked to specific Producer profiles, driving automated warnings.11  
* **Grace Period Tracking**: A dynamically calculated field computing Expiration\_Date \= Induction\_Date \+ 45 days based on the flexibilities introduced by RG 5748/2025 13, triggering escalating dashboard warnings for the acopiador.  
* **API Payload Logging**: The ERP must be architected to store the raw XML Request and XML Response for every single SOAP call made to WSAA, ws\_sr\_padron\_a4, and WSLiquiGranos. When ARCA audits a discrepancy months or years later, possessing the exact, cryptographically signed XML payload returned by their own servers at the precise time of the transaction constitutes the ultimate and only viable legal defense for the acopiador.
