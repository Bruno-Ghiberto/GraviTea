# **Gravitea Market Intelligence and Commercial Prospecting Report: ExpoAgro 2026**

## **Executive Summary**

The Argentine agricultural technology sector in March 2026 is characterized by a rapid convergence of strict regulatory enforcement, macroeconomic evolution, and forced digital transformation. For Gravitea, positioning a multi-tenant vertical SaaS ERP tailored specifically for agronomías (agricultural input distributors) in the Villa María–Marcos Juárez–Bell Ville corridor, the market conditions present an unprecedented commercial opportunity. The intelligence gathered ahead of ExpoAgro 2026—held March 10-13 at the Predio Ferial y Autódromo de San Nicolás 1—reveals a distinct vulnerability in the existing software landscape that Gravitea is uniquely engineered to exploit.

Three macroeconomic and regulatory pillars currently dictate the technological requirements of Argentine agronomías, creating intense operational friction for businesses reliant on legacy systems:

First, the transition from the former tax authority (AFIP) to the Agencia de Recaudación y Control Aduanero (ARCA) has introduced new paradigms in fiscal compliance and electronic invoicing. Notably, Resolution 5616/2024 formalized the framework for bimonetary operations, dictating strict protocols for invoicing in USD utilizing the Banco de la Nación Argentina (BNA) exchange rate.3 Furthermore, ARCA's Resolution 5824/2026, issued in February 2026, has expanded the scope of mandatory electronic invoicing and eliminated certain previous exemptions, tightening the digital control over all transactions.6 Agronomías operate in a heavily dollarized input market but transact locally in pesos; therefore, they require native bimonetary ledgers capable of managing USD, ARS, and soy-indexed transactions seamlessly.

Second, the implementation of SENASA Resolution 1175/2024, which mandated the National Traceability System for Phytosanitary Products, has fundamentally altered inventory management.9 The grace period ended in April 2025, meaning that currently, any distributor failing to accurately report stock movements, scan QR codes, and integrate via Web Services faces severe financial penalties and immediate operational blockades.9

Third, connectivity in the Argentine *zona núcleo* remains highly inconsistent. Cloud-based solutions that lack offline-first synchronization capabilities frequently fail during critical warehouse dispatches or field operations, frustrating users and halting commercial activity.

The primary finding of this analysis is that while horizontal ERP giants and farm management platforms command significant presence at ExpoAgro, there is a glaring absence of a lightweight, highly verticalized SaaS ERP specifically tailored for the daily operational realities of an input distributor. Legacy ERPs require heavy customization to handle the ARCA/SENASA nexus, while farm-centric applications focus too heavily on crop prescriptions or livestock traceability—such as the new 2026 SENASA electronic ear tag mandates 11—rather than wholesale chemical distribution. Gravitea’s competitive moat—combining ARCA compliance, SENASA traceability, bimonetary accounting, and offline-first capabilities into a single agronomía-focused platform—is highly defensible and immediately actionable.

ExpoAgro 2026 serves as the ideal strategic launchpad. By targeting the *Espacio Personal AgTech* for strategic integrations 12 and methodically prospecting mid-sized seed and chemical distributors in the main pavilions, Gravitea can efficiently secure its initial cohort of 10-25 high-value customers.

## **Competitive Landscape Map**

The AgTech software ecosystem in Argentina can be segmented into four distinct categories. Understanding where Gravitea sits within this matrix is vital for objection handling, pricing strategies, and competitive positioning during ExpoAgro negotiations.

### **1\. The Horizontal Enterprise Behemoths**

These are legacy, highly robust ERP systems that service multiple industries but have developed specialized "Agro" modules.

* **NeuralSoft (MyLogic):** A formidable presence with a highly integrated architecture, lightning-fast query speeds, and significant market share in Argentina.14 They offer advanced financial planning, MRP, and custom cost tracking.15 However, their solutions are heavy, requiring substantial implementation times, server infrastructure, and capital, making them an overkill solution for an average 10-person agronomía.  
* **TOTVS Agro:** A Brazilian multinational with deep penetration in Latin America. Their agro modules are excellent for massive agro-industrial complexes, bioenergy plants, and processing facilities.16 They offer deep ARCA integration via web services 17, but they lack the hyper-localized agility required by an independent input distributor reacting to sudden local regulatory shifts.

### **2\. Farm Management & Production Platforms**

These platforms are designed primarily for the *productor* (farmer) and the *asesor* (agronomist) rather than the *agronomía* (distributor).

* **Agrobit:** Deeply integrated with SAP, offering powerful agronomic insights, digital farming, and offline data capture.18 While they handle logistics and stock, their primary focus is crop evolution, farm management, and serving as a vertical layer on top of SAP.20  
* **Albor (Albor Campo):** Highly relevant in the current landscape due to their pivot toward AI and livestock management.22 With SENASA's 2026 mandate for electronic cattle identification 11, Albor is heavily marketing its traceability modules for *ganadería* at Stand P-01.23 Their agricultural modules are strong, but their focus is broad and producer-centric.  
* **AgroPro & EasyAgro:** Focused on precision agriculture, soil diagnostics, and agronomic prescriptions.24 They operate as complementary tools rather than core financial/inventory ERPs.

### **3\. Niche Traceability & Regulatory Tools**

* **Finnegans (App SENASA):** Finnegans recently launched a standalone application specifically designed to address SENASA 1175/2024 compliance.26 This indicates strong market validation for the exact problem Gravitea is solving. However, Finnegans approaches this as an add-on to their broader, heavier ERP ecosystem, leaving room for a nimble, all-in-one vertical SaaS.

### **4\. Ecosystem Enablers (Partnership Targets)**

* **AgriRed:** A B2B digital market connecting over 1,000 agronomías and cooperatives for the wholesale trade of inputs.27 They facilitate trading but do not act as the internal ERP.  
* **Auravant & Taligent (TABA):** Focused on data analytics, satellite imagery, and simplified business intelligence.29

**Gravitea's Strategic Position:** Gravitea occupies the highly lucrative, uncrowded intersection of *operational accounting ERP* and *specialized agricultural retail*. While NeuralSoft is too expansive and Agrobit is too farmer-centric, Gravitea is purpose-built for the distributor who buys wholesale, stores by lot, tracks expiration, and sells retail under strict SENASA and ARCA oversight, all while managing extreme currency volatility.

### **Discovery Methodology: The Mom Test**

**Never mention Gravitea by name. Never promise anything. Never say "we're building an ERP."**

Your cover story: *"Estoy investigando los problemas administrativos del sector agro para un proyecto de tecnología."* (I'm researching admin problems in the agro sector for a tech project.)

If someone tells you their pain WITHOUT knowing you have a solution, the pain is REAL. If they only complain after you pitch, the pain might be politeness.

### **The 5 Discovery Questions** *(Memorize these)*

1. *"¿Qué sistema usan hoy para facturar y manejar el stock?"* — What system do you use for invoicing and stock?
2. *"¿Cuál es la tarea administrativa que más tiempo les consume?"* — What admin task eats the most time?
3. *"¿Cómo están manejando lo de SENASA 1175 / trazabilidad fitosanitaria?"* — How are you handling SENASA 1175?
4. *"Si pudieran cambiar UNA cosa de su sistema actual, ¿qué sería?"* — If you could change ONE thing about your current system?
5. *"¿Cuánto pagan hoy por su sistema / contador / solución actual?"* — What do you pay for your current system/accountant/solution?

**APPROACH** (high discovery value):

## **Channel Partner Opportunities**

Selling vertical SaaS to agronomías one by one is highly capital intensive. Channel partners offer one-to-many distribution networks, acting as trusted validators.

* **Asociación de Cooperativas Argentinas (ACA):** With 130 member cooperatives and a 17% share of national grain production 39, ACA is a massive entity. Many of their member cooperatives function as input distributors. Partnering with ACA requires a highly robust product. At ExpoAgro, the goal is to make initial contact with their innovation arm, pitching Gravitea as a modern alternative for their smaller, technologically lagging member cooperatives.  
* **Confederaciones Rurales Argentinas (CRA):** A massive federation of local rural societies representing thousands of producers.40 They are highly politically active regarding taxation and regulations. Presenting Gravitea as a solution to "state-imposed administrative burdens" (ARCA/SENASA) aligns perfectly with their messaging.  
* **CREA:** Groups of premium, highly professionalized producers.41 While they are producers, their technical advisors (asesores) often dictate the technology stack for entire regions. Offering a free read-only portal for CREA advisors within the Gravitea ERP would create powerful localized network effects.  
* **Local Chambers (AERCA / ACOVIM \- Villa María):** Based in Gravitea's backyard 42, these are critical for the initial 10-25 customer push. They offer direct access to local merchants. A co-marketing launch event hosted at AERCA in Villa María is highly recommended to secure early adopters through community trust.

**Tier 4 Summary: Argentine PyME Manufacturers**

The Argentine agricultural machinery sector is a robust and historically significant segment of the national economy, featuring over 200 medium-sized enterprises (PyMEs) clustered primarily in Santa Fe and Córdoba. Based on standard market mapping and industrial registry data, approximately 40 to 50 of these manufacturers employ more than 50 people and operate with substantial industrial complexity.

**Typical ERP Usage in this Segment:** Unlike retail agronomías that buy finished goods and sell them, manufacturers require complex Material Requirements Planning (MRP) systems. They utilize software like Quality Sistemas 85, Softland 86, or even instances of NeuralSoft 15 to manage deep bills of materials (BOM), assembly line logistics, raw steel procurement, and long-term machinery financing.

**Gravitea Fit Assessment:**

The fit is strictly **NO**. Gravitea's current architectural feature set is highly optimized for retail and wholesale distribution (tracking expiration dates, managing highly regulated volatile chemicals under SENASA, and executing rapid over-the-counter POS transactions). It does not contain the manufacturing logic required to track labor hours against the assembly of a seed hopper. Attempting to service machinery PyMEs would severely dilute Gravitea's product roadmap and drain customer success resources.

**Exceptions (Dual Operators):**

Some entities classified generally as Tier 4 may also distribute parts or inputs on the side.

* **FARMQUIP:** Focuses on livestock installations.87 Not a fit.  
* **PRIORE AGROPARTES SRL:** Distributes metallic parts. While they operate as a distributor, the inventory logic for a tractor part is fundamentally different from a chemically volatile phytosanitary product.  
* **UNYPARTS / MOTORPART SRL:** Heavy parts distributors.

Gravitea should strictly avoid hardware and machinery operators. The go-to-market strategy must remain hyper-focused on seed, chemical, and fertilizer distributors where its SENASA 1175 integration and ARCA bimonetary moat provide a definitive, urgent advantage.
